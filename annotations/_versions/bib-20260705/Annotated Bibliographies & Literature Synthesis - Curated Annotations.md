Birkenmaier, Lukas, and Clemens Lechner. “Measuring politicians’ public personality traits using computational text analysis: A multimethod feasibility study for agency and Communion.” Political Analysis, 2 Dec. 2025, pp. 1–19, https://doi.org/10.1017/pan.2025.10025.

This article by German Professors Lukas Birkenmaier and Clemens Lechner explores the use of computational text analysis of public statements to derive the personality traits of political figures using third-generation AI and both supervised and semisupervised machine learning. The article delves into the utilization and validity of cues, how to properly distinguish between them, and the domain-specific traits of agency and communion. This article is highly relevant to the design of nodes and is applicable to other avenues within the present research, as it delves into the gaining of politically relevant personality traits, how to observe them, and how to pipeline them. Methods such as various codebooks will be necessary for continued research and are pre-built into the article, providing easy access. Finally, two weaknesses in the article are as follows: how to convert an individual's findings into a node in a social network analysis graph, and how to disentangle ambiguous statements.

Ardekani, Iman, et al. “Computational and Graph-Theoretic Analysis of Legislative Networks: New Zealand’s Mental Health Act as a Case Study.” MDPI, Multidisciplinary Digital Publishing Institute, 5 Feb. 2026, www.mdpi.com/2078-2489/17/2/161.

The article presents a case study of a computational framework used to analyze legislative citations for a single act in the New Zealand Parliament. This framework can be applied to the present research regarding how they mathematically compute the various nodes, vertices, and vectors. Additionally, machine learning methods used in the article, such as Named Entity Recognition (NER) or IE, can be applied to data extraction and processing in the applied product. Finally, the combination of structural graph analytics and semantic representation offers a potential layer to the ultimate analysis. The main drawback of this article is that it focuses on the legal citations of a bill and does not track key elements of lobbying and consulting, such as co-sponsorships, monetary congestion, or other variables. More thought will be needed as to how this can be practically applied to the intended product. However, the article goes into depth on the Louvian method, which can be used by communities and coalitions. Additional research into different forms of centrality is needed.

Furnas, Alexander C., Timothy M LaPira, Alexander Hertel-Fernandez, et al. “More than mere access: An experiment on moneyed interests, information provision, and legislative action in Congress.” *Political Research Quarterly*, vol. 76, no. 1, 13 May 2022, pp. 348–364, [https://doi.org/10.1177/10659129221098743](https://doi.org/10.1177/10659129221098743).

This article by Professors Furnas, LaPira, and various others, explores the effects that the identity of a giver of political information has on the receptiveness of the recipient of said political information within the context of legislative advocacy. By using a novel survey of 400 federal congressional staffers, where the identity of the individual or interest, the nature of the request, and the source of the statistics for said recommendation are provided, the authors reveal an “epistemic closure among partisan staffers.” They make clear their recommendation that an interest group should base their findings around evidence from a think-tank that is ideologically aligned with the office they are engaging in. Put simply, the staffers want to hear only what they want to hear and will be psychologically adverse to institutions they perceive as against their interests, and hesitant towards groups that they perceive as pursuing their own goals. The implications for these findings are profound, and can be seen as an explanation for the donation behavior of large dark money funds. By channeling funds toward the building of ideological infrastructure, such groups are not recruiting their own champions, but cultivating them. Finally, in regards to the current research, allocation of venture capital funds to the growth of industry-aligned think tanks should be considered, scouted, and ultimately incorporated into future allocation models.

Huneeus, Federico, and In Song Kim. “The effects of firms’ lobbying on Resource Misallocation.” *SSRN Electronic Journal*, 12 Nov. 2018, [https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.3275097](https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.3275097).

This article by Massachusetts Institute of Technology Professor In Song Kim, and Princeton Professor Federico Huneeus examines the optimization, and more specifically mis-allocation, of company revenue on political lobbying. The main finding is that a 10% increase in lobbying expenditures leads to a 3% gain in revenue. Additionally, the article introduces a novel methodology used to capture this finding, using the general equilibrium firm model that features endogenous variables. The fundamental application of this paper to the present research is being able to set fundraising goals, and use the current models as a basis to help inform the appropriate amount for future targets and institutions. However, there are several gaps within the literature as they pertain to the present research. First, is that the model does not distinguish campaign donations from events, meetings, etc. Second, is that the models make no differentiation between the representatives, however its state layer can shed insight as to who should be targeted based on general industry presence. In all, Professor Kim delivers another key step into the optimization and empirical application of mathematical models and principals to the realm of political advocacy, serving as the basis for business-applicable models.

Fink, Christian G., et al. “A Congressional twitter network dataset quantifying pairwise probability of influence.” *Data in Brief*, vol. 50, 28 Aug. 2023, p. 109521, [https://doi.org/10.1016/j.dib.2023.109521](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.dib.2023.109521).

This article presents a novel dataset highlighting the probabilities of influence among members of Congress, derived from Twitter’s API.  The data is presented as a social network, with edge weights representing the probabilities of influence, composed of quotes, retweets, mentions, and replies. It provides a fundamental, pre-built framework for pipelining the data required for the present research. The Viral Centrality Algorithm should be interrogated further, both for its ability to reduce compute time and for its ability to simulate scenarios that, by design, are similar to a Monte Carlo simulation. The two main weaknesses of the article are as follows: whether it quantifies a reply or mention as negative; how it quantifies agreement between two or more actors within the ecosystem when the sentiment is negative toward another member; and the necessity for temporal weighting to capture shifting relationships. Nonetheless, it is a fundamental framework for capturing the psychological and relational characteristics of actors within the ecosystem and among them.

Kim, In Song, and Dmitriy Kunisky. “Mapping political communities: A statistical analysis of lobbying networks in Legislative Politics.” Political Analysis, vol. 29, no. 3, 23 Nov. 2020, pp. 317–336, https://doi.org/10.1017/pan.2020.29.

This article by MIT professors In Song Kim and Dimitriy Kuinsky applies the theory of Social Network Analysis to the lobbying industry and members of Congress, using a novel Bipartite Link Community Model that captures mixed membership and representative congestion within an MOC. On the topic of “mixed interactions observed between actors,” what this means specifically is that, within the model, one can see that different interactions between the same two actors as a result of one of the actors, such as the Chamber of Commerce, taking on multiple, each distinct, issues. Regarding the present research, it is crucial for both data pipelines to capture congestion, as well as to identify champions for rival or ally industries and measure the level of commitment those champions have toward those industries. Its unique lobbying database is incredibly extensive and utilizes textual extraction from lobbying reports using machine learning. A weakness of this article as it pertains to the present research, is how to transform the bipartite nature of the current model into a graph format, where path-finding algorithms can be run. Regardless, the current model provides a useful tool for identifying champions and connections across rival industries.

Malaterre, Christophe, and Francis Lareau. “Inferring social networks from unstructured text data: A proof of concept detection of hidden communities of interest.” Data &amp; Policy, vol. 6, 2024, https://doi.org/10.1017/dap.2023.48.

This article focuses on the methodological approach to turning unstructured data, such as long policy memos, into refined data that can be input as vectors and edges within a social network analysis graph. The main theory emphasizes the use of shared semantic content between seemingly unrelated groups using a fitted topic model. It provides a good foundational overview of how Social Network Analysis operates and how to craft your own framework. One potential extension of the current research is to infer the level of closeness between two congressional offices based on mapping their co-occurrences within texts or on general textual similarities—requiring a temporal element. Additionally, this topic model could be domain-specific to the trade association or consulting partner assigned to it. This also mentions Louvian community detection. Finally, note how they slimmed down the articles to increase computational efficiency. The author explicitly mentions this method in its application to advocacy. Diagnosis: Priority – Very High – Foundational

Rongkuan, LI, et al. “Tactical environment path planning based on improved A* algorithm.” *2024 10th International Conference on Big Data and Information Analytics (BigDIA)*, 25 Oct. 2024, pp. 644–648, [https://doi.org/10.1109/bigdia63733.2024.10808219](https://doi.org/10.1109/bigdia63733.2024.10808219).

This article explores methods to improve the A* pathfinding algorithm under limited computing resources, budgetary constraints, and an unstable information retrieval pipeline. It outlines the use of machine learning and conventional neural networks to optimize the heuristic function used to determine the target candidate. This is paired with a distributed microservice framework to capture the environment's self-mobilizing nature. The main application of this was to military operations, where orders are in flux and terrain can vary drastically. The main applications of the current research are the following: the proposal of a centerless distributed manner to preserve network integrity in the case of the destruction of a node, and the use of machine learning to allow A* to better operate in unstable environments, both in the direction of the path and the environment itself. The main weakness is the necessity of a local service cache. Additionally, the method is applied to a physical environment rather than an abstract social network, as intended for the current research. Designation: Priority – Structure – Augmentation

Kim, Jison, et al. Measuring Interest Group Positions on Legislation: An AI-Driven Analysis of Lobbying Reports, 23 Apr. 2025.

Professors from the Massachusetts Institute of Technology and the Korea Advanced Institute of Science and Technology use Large Language Models and Graph Neural Networks to analyze and underpin the correlation between lobbying firms, the direction and shape of policy preferences, and ultimately derive insights regarding lobbying strategies and firm behavior. This article is foundational to the current research, as it provides a novel, large-scale, and pre-built data pipeline that can be directly implemented on a social network analysis vector graph, yielding insights into the behavior of competing firms. There are three main findings uncovered by the authors: A strong correlation between the bills' place and the legislative process and the positions taken by the interest group; the firm's size plays a key role in the adoption of monitoring positions; and industries align their efforts on issue-specific stakes. Finally, there are two main weaknesses relevant to the current research. First, this method is reactive rather than predictive due to the time lag between the release of filing reports and current industry positions. Finally, this method doesn’t explicitly highlight conflict between industries over a specific bill, which is vital for political intelligence.
