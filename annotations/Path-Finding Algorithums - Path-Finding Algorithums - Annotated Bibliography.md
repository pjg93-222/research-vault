Oropeza-Martínez, Peter, et al. “A new pathfinding evacuation decision-making algorithm in buildings on fire based on personality traits.” *2024 13th International Conference On Software Process Improvement (CIMPS)*, 16 Oct. 2024, pp. 01–09, [https://doi.org/10.1109/cimps65195.2024.11095951](https://doi.org/10.1109/cimps65195.2024.11095951).

This article outlines an alternative sub-branch of the A* algorithm that emphasizes people's real-life reactions and choices, and adjusts the optimal route to account for the psychological traits that underpin these decisions, using the OCEAN model. The methodology serves as a crucial missing link in the current research, addressing how the actors intended to be measured will respond psychologically to the pressures, interactions, and proposals presented to them. This allows for finer adjustment on what could be considered one of many invisible uncertainty variables. The main gap in this research is its lack of application to a congressional setting. The pipelining within the methodology can be transformed into the intended research. However, existing research fills this gap in the collection of said data. Additionally, a challenge is that the agents are not the subjects of the psychological characteristics, but the nodes; a methodology for detailing them as edge parameters is needed. Keep in mind the fact that 10 personalities collapsed into 4 observable ones. Finally, the article mentions the EHA*, SIR, and BFS models; look into these. Designation: Priority - Structure - Basis.

Rongkuan, LI, et al. “Tactical environment path planning based on improved A* algorithm.” *2024 10th International Conference on Big Data and Information Analytics (BigDIA)*, 25 Oct. 2024, pp. 644–648, [https://doi.org/10.1109/bigdia63733.2024.10808219](https://doi.org/10.1109/bigdia63733.2024.10808219).

This article explores methods to improve the A* pathfinding algorithm under limited computing resources, budgetary constraints, and an unstable information retrieval pipeline. It outlines the use of machine learning and conventional neural networks to optimize the heuristic function used to determine the target candidate. This is paired with a distributed microservice framework to capture the environment's self-mobilizing nature. The main application of this was to military operations, where orders are in flux and terrain can vary drastically. The main applications of the current research are the following: the proposal of a centerless distributed manner to preserve network integrity in the case of the destruction of a node, and the use of machine learning to allow A* to better operate in unstable environments, both in the direction of the path and the environment itself. The main weakness is the necessity of a local service cache. Additionally, the method is applied to a physical environment rather than an abstract social network, as intended for the current research. Designation: Priority – Structure – Augmentation.

Zareie, Ahmad, and Rizos Sakellariou. “Similarity-based link prediction in social networks using latent relationships between the users.” *Scientific Reports*, vol. 10, no. 1, 18 Nov. 2020, [https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-020-76799-4](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-020-76799-4).
