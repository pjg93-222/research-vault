# Large Language Model Enhanced Logic Tensor Network for Stance Detection

**Genan Dai, Jiayu Liao, Sicheng Zhao, Xianghua Fu, Xiaojiang Peng, Hu Huang, Bowen Zhang (corresponding) (2025 (volume year; published online 29 Nov 2024; received 25 May 2024, accepted 22 Nov 2024)).** *Neural Networks, vol. 183 (2025), article 106956.* [10.1016/j.neunet.2024.106956](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neunet.2024.106956). Theme: [[themes/machine-learning-sentiment-analysis|Machine Learning & Sentiment Analysis]].

> [!abstract] Summary
> Dai et al. (Neural Networks 183, 2025) propose FOLAR, a stance detector with three parts. First, chain-of-thought prompts ask an LLM to write a first-order-logic (FOL) rule for each text and target. Second, a logic tensor network (LTN) uses a shared BERT and soft operators (negation 1-U, product conjunction, implication 1-U1+U1U2) to turn the rule into a differentiable classifier. Third, the LTNs built from GPT-3.5, Claude-2 and LLaMA-2 are fused by attention routing. The tests use English political tweets (SemEval-2016, ISD) and VAST. On in-target SEM16 and ISD the gains are small: +1.7 F_avg on LA and +2.0 on DT_I, and FOLAR is not the best on FM or HC. Zero-shot VAST gains are larger: +4.2 with full training data and +6.2 with 10% of it. The fusion ablation is about 5.8 points, but it compares fusion with a single GPT-3.5 model, so fusion is not separated from the choice of LLM. The paper reports no standard deviations, does not name its significance test, and its tables contain internal inconsistencies. The interpretability claim rests only on a 100-item, 3-rater check.
> 
> For Paul's system, the paper matters most as a formal version of something already built. The two-hop CU-alignment rule in fec/stance/apply_two_hop_stance.py composes stance with valence by hard sign multiplication over a hand-curated registry. FOLAR is the soft, learned, LLM-elicited form of that rule. It also gives a candidate route for the registry-coverage problem, but its error analysis names LLM over-inference that links unrelated positive words to the target, a failure type to watch for in automatic registry variants. The fusion result suggests a cheap check on single-labeller bias in the Claude pre-labels. It does not show that switching to FOLAR would beat a DEBATE or SVM reader on CU press items. The domain, the text genre and the LLM versions all differ.

## Study

**Question.** Can first-order logic (FOL) rules elicited from LLMs by chain-of-thought prompting be encoded in a logic tensor network (LTN), and can fusing LTNs built from several LLMs produce interpretable and more accurate stance detection than fine-tuned PLMs, prompt-tuning, and LLM-only baselines, in both in-target and zero-shot settings?

**Theory.** Neurosymbolic learning: logic tensor networks (real logic with soft/product-style operators, after Badreddine et al. 2022) make predicates differentiable so that FOL rules constrain a deep network trained by backpropagation. Motivation draws on FOL-distillation work (Hu et al. 2016 DNNLR; posterior regularization) and on chain-of-thought prompting of LLMs. The paper asserts interpretability through FOL rationales; there is no formal interpretability test.

**Data.** Three English-language stance benchmarks. (1) SemEval-2016 Task 6 (SEM16; Mohammad et al. 2016): targets FM, LA, HC, DT (plus Ath and CC are named); Table 1 counts favor/against/neutral: DT 148/299/260 (707 total), HC 163/565/256 (984), LA 167/544/222 (933), FM 268/511/170 (949), so about 3,573 instances across the four targets listed. 15 percent of training data held out as dev. Unit: sentence-target pair (tweet). (2) VAST (Allaway and McKeown 2020): Table 2 lists 13,477 train / 2,062 dev / 3,006 test examples with stance labels Pro/Con/Neutral; the row labels for topic counts appear misaligned in the extraction (for example "#Zero-shot Topics 4003"). Evaluated on zero-shot topics under 100 percent and 10 percent training data. (3) ISD (Huang et al. 2023): text says 6,027 labeled tweets about DT_I and JB_I, averaging 2.14 hashtags per sentence, 80/20 train/test split; however Table 1 ISD rows sum to 5,588 (DT_I 3,105; JB_I 2,483), a discrepancy. Time period of data collection: not stated in the text. Population: social media posts about political figures and topics (abortion, feminism, Clinton, Trump, Biden, bike lanes, rail, cameras). LLM outputs come from gpt-3.5-turbo-0301, Claude-2-100k and LLaMA-2-70b-chat (temperature 0); GPT-4 appears only in Figure 5 (not extracted).

**Method.** FOLAR = three modules. (a) Knowledge Elicitation (Sec 3.1): chain-of-thought prompts ask an LLM to produce a FOL rule for each input and target, illustrated by Eq 1 and Table 5. (b) LTN (Sec 3.2): predicates are phrases embedded by a shared pretrained BERT, followed by fully connected layers; soft-logic operators are negation 1-U, conjunction product, disjunction U1+U2-U1U2, implication 1-U1+U1U2 (Eqs 2-5); output is a softmax over favor/neutral/against (Eq 6 composition). (c) Multi-Decision Fusion (Sec 3.3): one LTN per LLM (GPT-3.5, LLaMA-2, Claude-2), representation vectors concatenated into H and fused by attention-based dynamic routing over t iterations with layer normalization and sigmoid (Eqs 7-11); training minimizes cross-entropy (Eq 12). Training: AdamW, batch 16, up to 30 epochs, warm-up ratio 0.2, learning rate 1e-5, weight decay 1e-3. Baselines (Sec 4.4): statistical (Bicond, CrossNet, SEKT, TPDG); PLM (JoinCL, PT-HCL, TarBK, SDAgu, KPT, KEprompt, BERT-FT); LLM (Ts-CoT, KASD, MPPT, COLA) plus FOL-CoT (the LLM-only variant of FOLAR). Ablations: w/o FOL (compared to Ts-CoT-like structure), w/o LTN, w/o MDF (single GPT-3.5).

**Identification.** None in the causal sense. This is a predictive supervised classification benchmark. Generalization is tested by holding out targets (zero-shot: train on other SEM16 targets, test on one held-out target; VAST zero-shot topics) and by a 10 percent training-data condition on VAST. Each result is the average of three runs.

**Scope.** Population: English social media posts (tweets and similar short texts) with stance toward a named target, mostly US political figures and contentious topics. Institution: Shenzhen Technology University and collaborators (no institutional sample). Era: SEM16 (2016) and VAST (2020) benchmarks; LLM snapshots from 2023 (GPT-3.5-turbo-0301, Claude-2, LLaMA-2-70b). Not about lobbying, legislators, campaigns, networks, path-finding, allocation, elections as events, persuasion or turnout. Relevant to the project only as a text-measurement method (stance labeling of political text) and as an interpretable neurosymbolic classifier.

## Limitations

- The paper has no dedicated limitations section. Authors list future work: more complex logic forms, more contentious topics, newer dynamic logic, and better decision fusion (Conclusion, lines 1199-1206).
- Implicit or sarcastic stance is not captured by LLM-elicited FOL (Sec 4.8, lines 1149-1157).
- LLM over-inference and clause-level polarity conflicts cause errors (Sec 4.8, lines 1158-1167).
- Results come from three runs with no reported standard deviations or confidence intervals; significance test (p < 0.05) is not named (lines 703-711).
- Relies on proprietary, versioned LLM APIs (gpt-3.5-turbo-0301, Claude-2-100k) and temperature 0 to aid reproducibility; GPT-4 results appear only in a figure (lines 532-543, 1019-1025).
- Data availability is stated as 'available on request' (line 1224), while the abstract says source code will be publicly available (lines 29-30).
- Inconsistent SEM16 target handling: DT, Ath and CC are said to be excluded (lines 503-504) but DT is used in the zero-shot tables and the setting list (lines 509, 739).
- ISD size: text says 6,027 tweets (line 515) but Table 1 rows sum to 5,588 (lines 463-471).
- Table 2 topic-count rows look misaligned (for example '#Zero-shot Topics 4003' larger than examples per split logic) (lines 485-492).
- FOL-CoT All values are identical in both VAST rows of Table 4 (79.8), suggesting a transcription or copy error (lines 903-913).
- Fusion ablation (w/o MDF) uses only one LLM (GPT-3.5), so it does not separate MDF from simply choosing a different LLM (lines 1004-1009).
- Manual FOL check uses only 100 instances and three raters, and the kappa variant is not specified (lines 1181-1185).
- Gains over baselines are small on several in-target columns (for example FM, where KEprompt is higher in the extracted table).

## Gaps and flags

- The valence hop has no soft or probabilistic form: compose() is hard sign multiplication, so valence confidence and partial registry matches are discarded (apply_two_hop_stance.py:27-33).
- The pre-labels come from a single Claude rater, and the model and version differ across system files (claude_stance.py defaults to claude-sonnet-4-6; train_tweet_stance.py calls its api labels Opus prelabel and its docstring cites Fable API labels). No second or third LLM rater exists to measure labeller-specific bias before human override, which bears on the open question of how much DEBATE accuracy is LLM agreement.
- Claude's reason field is captured (claude_stance.py:93) but is never checked for quality. The paper at least rates rationales for salience, non-redundancy and non-contradiction (n=100, kappa scores averaging 76 percent).
- No low-data or new-target evaluation condition exists for CU stance. The paper shows that method rankings differ most in that condition.
- Nothing here conflicts with the SNA wording rules (D-025, D-021, the rejected cosponsorship and normalization proposals). The paper is pure text classification.
- Any LLM-elicited FOL rules or valence links adopted from this method are new agent analysis and stay UNVERIFIED until a second path (human gold) reproduces them (DE-062). The paper's own error analysis shows LLM over-inference on target links.
- The paper's quality is weak for adoption. It reports no SDs or CIs over three runs and does not name its significance test. FOL-CoT shows the same 79.8 in both VAST rows (a likely copy error). The ISD counts disagree (6,027 in the text vs 5,588 in the table), and the SEM16 target exclusions are inconsistent. Data are 'available on request', and the gpt-3.5-turbo-0301 and Claude-2 snapshots are versioned proprietary models (whether they are still available was not checked).
- The interpretability claim is asserted, not tested. Do not quote FOLAR rationales as explanations of stance without a separate validation.

Paul has no annotation of Dai et al. The paper does not support replacing the SVM or DEBATE readers: FOLAR's in-target gains are small, and no SVM baseline was run.
