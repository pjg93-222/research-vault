# Getting Personal: A Deep Learning Artifact for Text-Based Measurement of Personality

**Kai Yang, Raymond Y. K. Lau, Ahmed Abbasi (2023).** *Information Systems Research, 34(1):194-222 (March 2023; published online 28 March 2022).* [10.1287/isre.2022.1111](https://doi.org/10.1287/isre.2022.1111). Theme: [[themes/machine-learning-sentiment-analysis|Machine Learning & Sentiment Analysis]].

> [!abstract] Summary
> Yang, Lau and Abbasi (ISR 2023) build DeepPerson, a design-science artifact for Big Five detection from text. It stacks an ELMo-style CNN-LSTM encoder (one Bi-LSTM for syntax and semantics, one for LIWC/MRC/SPLICE concept embeddings), a word-layer-person hierarchical attention network (wlpHAN), and SPDFiT, a Bayesian pseudo-labeling fine-tuner whose entropy quality score both filters pseudo-labels and scales the learning rate. Labels are self-report Big Five median-split to binary. On PANDORA (Reddit, 1,568 users) DeepPerson reaches AUC 75.0 vs 64.6 for the best comparator (AttRCNN), 63.4 PersonaBERT, 61.5 BERT-Base, and roughly 56 for the IBM feature-based baseline; on myPersonality (Facebook, 250 users) AUC 70.7 vs 63.3. Essays-corpus results sit in an appendix not in the text file. In ablations, removing wlpHAN costs about 5 pp AUC, replacing SPDFiT costs about 5 pp (a similar size), and the LIWC-type concept encoder adds about 2 pp. Downstream, adding DeepPerson scores to GBDT forecasts of eight Compustat outcomes for 352 S&P 1500 executives with Twitter accounts cuts MSE by 14.29% on average (RFR 6.67%); this is framed as prediction, not causation, and executive-level scores are never validated against surveys. For Melange the paper is a benchmark in which the IBM feature-based baseline, which moved away from LIWC, ranks lowest (AUC about 56.1), while LIWC concepts still add signal inside a deep model; it makes no family-level claim about closed-vocabulary scoring. Its body does not touch legislators (an appendix COVID-19 case on state and world leaders is not in the text file), elite speech genres, observer or gold-panel criteria, or lobbying responsiveness, so its accuracy numbers are UNSTRATIFIED by genre and population relative to the system.

## Study

**Question:** RQ1: Relative to existing NLP methods, how effectively can DeepPerson detect personality dimensions from user-generated text? RQ2: Can enhanced personality measurement significantly improve downstream forecasting outcomes? (p. 195)

**Theory:** Systemic functional linguistic theory (SFLT; Halliday and Hasan 2004) as kernel theory, with ideational, interpersonal and textual meta-functions mapped to design components (Table 2, p. 201). Big Five (five-factor model; Goldberg 1990) as the personality construct. Psycholinguistic link between psychological states and language (Pennebaker and King 1999). For the downstream case, upper echelons theory (Hambrick 2007) is cited for links between executive traits and firm outcomes. Prediction is framed per Shmueli and Koppius (2011), not causal explanation (p. 196).

**Data:** Design evaluation (Section 4): (1) PANDORA (Reddit), 3,000,566 comments from 1,568 users per the text (Table 3 lists 3,000,068 posts); (2) myPersonality (Facebook), 10,000 status updates from 250 users per the text (Table 3 lists 9,917); (3) Essays, 2,479 essays from 2,479 psychology students (1.9 million words). Labels are survey-based Big Five, median-split to binary per dimension. Splits are 50/25/25 at the user level, repeated 10 times. Pretraining/pseudo-labeling corpora: 1B Word benchmark and Sentiment140 (Go et al. 2009). Downstream case (Section 5): 352 senior executives (219 CEOs, 40 CFOs, 22 CXOs, 188 directors, 62 presidents, 10 chairmen per text; these sum to 541, not reconciled in text) at S&P 1500 firms (425 firms identified), employed 1990-2017 with Twitter accounts; tweets 2006-2017, mean 529 per executive (~186,000 data points); Compustat financials 1990-2017; 519 firm-executive-biennial observations; dependent-variable N ranges 405-519 (Table 10). Unit: user (design eval); firm-executive-two-year period (case study).

**Method:** DeepPerson artifact has three parts: (a) CNN-LSTM encoder: CNN character encoder, word CNN embeddings, two multilayer Bi-LSTMs (ELMo-style, pretrained on 1B Word), one for syntax/semantics and one for LIWC/MRC/SPLICE psychological concept embeddings, aggregated to 1,024-dim (Eqs. 1-3, pp. 200-202); (b) wlpHAN, word-layer-person hierarchical attention with layer attention (Eq. 4), word attention (Eq. 6), and a person-level Bi-LSTM (Eq. 7), softmax over Big Five categories (Eqs. 8-9, pp. 202-204); (c) SPDFiT, self-taught fine-tuning: Bayesian pseudo-labeling with Gaussian class-conditional priors, entropy-based quality score s_m in [0,1] that both filters and scales SGD learning rate (Algorithm 1, p. 206; Section 3.3, pp. 204-206). Implemented on ELMo in PyTorch; mini-batch 500, dropout 0.5, grid search on validation users. Metrics: macro-averaged F, precision, recall, accuracy, AUC; Wilcoxon signed-rank tests. Downstream: Big Five scores as features added to baseline features in random forest regression (RFR) and gradient boosted decision trees (GBDT), scikit-learn, 20 estimators; comparators CNN-1, CNN-2, BERT, PersonaBERT, and ARIMA; outcomes measured as the log of the first two-year average after executive entry; improvement = (MSE_baseline - MSE_experimental)/MSE_baseline; Wilcoxon tests (p. 215-216).

**Identification:** None in a causal sense. The authors explicitly frame the downstream test as prediction, not explanation (p. 196, citing Shmueli and Koppius 2011), and acknowledge reverse causality concerns (Hambrick 2007) without resolving them (p. 196). Identification of value is incremental predictive lift: the same RFR/GBDT models are run with and without personality features, with a chronological train/test structure referenced for the main case and a single chronological split reported in Online Appendix G (p. 217). Personality is assumed stable over the analysis period (p. 214, citing Cobb-Clark and Schurer 2012).

**Scope:** Population: social media and essay authors (Reddit, Facebook, psychology students) for detection; S&P 1500 senior executives of US-listed firms for finance forecasting (Section 5). Era: Essays and myPersonality/PANDORA social media data; executive tweets 2006-2017; firm financials 1990-2017; paper published 2022. Institutional setting: IS design science; no institution-level or legislative/lobbying/electoral population is studied in the body text. The COVID-19 case (state and world leaders, Online Appendix H) is referenced but not in the text file. Languages: English implied; not explicitly stated. Nothing in the text addresses lobbying, networks, path-finding, allocation, elections, persuasion, turnout, or legislators, except that Hambrick (2007) is cited for personality being relevant to whether people are elected into leadership roles (p. 196).

## Key findings

- On PANDORA, DeepPerson reaches AUC 75.0 vs. best comparators AttRCNN 64.6, CNN-1 64.0, PersonaBERT 63.4; macro F 65.1 vs AttRCNN 59.5. Text says DeepPerson beats best comparators by 5-15 pp across measures. Numbers: AUC 75.0; Imp. +33.7% vs IBM baseline; AttRCNN AUC 64.6 (+15.2%). (Table 4 and Section 4.2, p. 209)
- On myPersonality, DeepPerson reaches AUC 70.7 vs AttRCNN 63.3 and PersonaBERT 61.2; macro F 67.0 vs 59.0 for AttRCNN. Wilcoxon tests significant (p<0.01) vs CNN-1 on most traits. Numbers: AUC 70.7 (+25.1%); AttRCNN AUC 63.3 (+12.0%). (Table 5 and Section 4.2, p. 209)
- Ablation on PANDORA: removing wlpHAN drops AUC by about 5 pp (75.0 to 70.0), and replacing SPDFiT with UDA, Self-Ensembling, or logistic/Lasso/Ridge pseudo-labeling lowers it to 64.7-65.2 (also about 5 pp); the paper says SPDFiT matters but does not rank it largest. The 58.3 floor is Doc2Vec (no pretraining), not an SPDFiT replacement. Numbers: CNN-LSTM (SPDFiT) AUC 70.0 (+20.1%); 1BWord only 62.3; UDA 65.1; Self-Ensembling 65.2; Logistic 64.9; BERT-Base 60.2; PersonaBERT 63.4; Doc2Vec 58.3. (Table 6 and Section 4.3, pp. 208-210)
- SPDFiT still beats comparators when only part of the unlabeled data is used: about 50% of unlabeled data gives fairly good results, and SPDFiT outperforms all alternatives at 40%+ of PANDORA or 30%+ of myPersonality unlabeled data. Numbers: Unlabeled proportion tested in 10% steps from 10% to 100%. (Section 4.3, p. 211 and Figure 4)
- wlpHAN outperforms other attention networks. Full wlpHAN AUC 70.3 vs AttRCNN 64.6, HCAN 61.1, HAN 60.7, Msg-Attn 60.5; the text claims a 3-6 pp lift, but Table 7 gaps are about 9-12 pp over HAN, HCAN and Msg-Attn and 5.7 pp over AttRCNN, so cite the table numbers (p<0.01). Each of the syntax/semantic, concept, and person encoders adds about 2 pp (text claim). Numbers: wlpHAN AUC 70.3 (+20.0%); SynSem+Concept+Word 68.0; SynSem+Word 66.2; Word only 64.0 (+9.2%); aspect attention DPA 64.7, CA 63.9, GA 64.8. (Table 7 and Section 4.4, pp. 211-212)
- Downstream: adding DeepPerson Big Five scores improves forecasts of firm policy and performance indicators. Average MSE improvement for GBDT is 14.29%, and for RFR 6.67%. Text reports 6.1-14.3% average range across models and MSE/MAE, and 4-15% per variable. Gains for all eight DVs are significant at p<0.05. Numbers: GBDT MSE avg +14.29% (CH 20.91, SG&A 31.74, INVEST 11.91, LEVER 12.95, IC 8.65, D&E 8.13, ROA 12.52, CF 7.49); RFR MSE avg +6.67%; GBDT MAE avg +9.46%. (Tables 11-12 and Section 5, pp. 216-218)
- Comparators add less or nothing downstream. GBDT average MSE improvement: CNN-1 3.38%, BERT 8.62%, CNN-2 -1.80%. ARIMA time-series model is worse, with about 17% higher MSE than GBDT-DeepPerson (ARIMA average -17.05%). Numbers: CNN-1 GBDT avg 3.38; BERT GBDT avg 8.62; CNN-2 GBDT avg -1.80; ARIMA GBDT avg -17.05. (Table 11, Section 5, pp. 216-217)
- Efficiency claim: DeepPerson uses 800 million pretraining tokens, versus 3.3 billion for BERT-Base and 4.1 billion for PersonaBERT. Authors argue that bigger generic LMs are not enough for personality measurement. Numbers: DeepPerson 800M tokens; BERT-Base 3.3B; PersonaBERT 4.1B; RoBERTa about 30B+. (Section 6 point (1), p. 218)
- Error analysis: on instances where DeepPerson most beats comparators, synsem and concept embeddings and SPDFiT yield the largest MSE gains across all five traits. Example concept patterns (LIWC tags such as posemo, anx, work, time) and synsem patterns (POS-tag n-grams) are shown per trait. Numbers: 25% of PANDORA instances with widest error margins analyzed per comparator (CNN-1, CNN-2, PersonaBERT, AttRCNN). (Section 4.5, Figure 5, Tables 8-9, pp. 212-215)
- Design evaluation uses user-level Pearson correlation and MSE to approximate user Big Five scores. Text says DeepPerson is at least 10-18 points higher in correlation and at least 10% lower MSE than the best comparator on PANDORA and myPersonality (user-level results in Online Appendix B, not in text file). Numbers: correlation +10-18 points; MSE at least 10% lower. (Section 4.2, p. 209)

## Limitations

- Authors state: bias is an important consideration for NLP models (Lalor et al. 2022); future work on other languages and on audio and video inputs would be beneficial (p. 219).
- Authors state the design evaluation covered only social media posts, forum messages, and essays; speech transcripts and written articles may warrant exploration (p. 219).
- Authors acknowledge reverse causality concerns in personality-outcome research (Hambrick 2007) but argue they do not undermine the prediction value of personality measures (p. 196). No causal claims are made.
- Authors assume personality is stable over the analysis window, citing Cobb-Clark and Schurer (2012) (p. 214).
- Detection model is trained on myPersonality (Facebook) labels and applied to executives' tweets (p. 214). The text reports no validation of executive-level scores against survey measures; user-level validation covers PANDORA and myPersonality only (Appendix B).
- Personality labels are binarized by median split for detection (p. 206), so the design evaluation is a binary classification task, not continuous measurement.
- Internal inconsistencies in the text: abstract says DeepPerson improves detection by 10-20 pp (p. 194), body says 5-15 pp vs best comparators (p. 209). Text says 3,000,566 Reddit comments and 10,000 Facebook status updates (pp. 205-206), Table 3 lists 3,000,068 posts and 9,917 updates. Executive job-title counts sum to 541 against 352 executives, unreconciled (p. 214).
- Downstream sample is 352 executives from 425 firms, with 519 firm-executive-biennial observations; only executives with Twitter accounts were kept, which may not be representative (p. 214).
- Essays comparison sample is psychology students (2,479 essays), not executives or public actors.
- Appendix results (user-level, robustness, COVID-19 case) are referenced but not in the text file, so they could not be checked here.

## Gaps and flags

- No deep or open-vocabulary trait model has been tested against the Senate gold panels; the paper ranks only the IBM feature baseline lowest (its feature rows include KNN 58.6 and XGBoost 58.9), not closed-vocabulary scoring as a family, so whether a stronger learner lifts convergence above the lexical held-out CV mean of .350 vs RKH is untested.
- The system has no with-vs-without incremental-lift test of trait features on a behavioural outcome of interest (H1/H2 lobbying responsiveness); the paper's Section 5 design is a ready template.
- The essays fit (fit_smoreg_essays.py) trains on student stream-of-consciousness text with binary labels; the paper's Essays comparison is appendix-only, so how far the system's LinearSVR sits below current methods on its own training corpus is unknown.
- Paper accuracy figures are UNSTRATIFIED by genre and population relative to the system (self-report labels; Reddit, Facebook, students, executive tweets); quote them only with that label and window.
- Internal inconsistencies: abstract claims 10-20 pp detection gain while the body reports 5-15 pp vs best comparators; executive title counts sum to 541 against 352 executives; corpus counts differ between text and Table 3.
- Downstream lift is prediction with acknowledged reverse causality; it is not evidence that traits influence outcomes and must not be carried into member influence or receptivity language.
