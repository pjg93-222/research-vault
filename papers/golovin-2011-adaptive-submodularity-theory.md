# Adaptive Submodularity: Theory and Applications in Active Learning and Stochastic Optimization

**Daniel Golovin, Andreas Krause (2011).** *Journal of Artificial Intelligence Research, vol. 42, pp. 427-486 (2011); extended version of a Conference on Learning Theory 2010 paper (pp. 333-345). Text copy is arXiv:1003.3967v5 [cs.LG], revised 6 Dec 2017..* [arXiv:1003.3967](https://doi.org/arXiv:1003.3967) Theme: [[themes/adaptive-influence-maximization|Adaptive Influence Maximization]].

> [!abstract] Summary
> Golovin and Krause extend the diminishing-returns idea behind submodular set functions to adaptive policies. In their setting you pick an item, observe its random outcome, then pick the next item. If the objective's conditional expected marginal gain never rises as more is observed (adaptive submodularity, which is defined relative to a known prior), then adaptive greedy gets at least (1-1/e) of the best adaptive policy. The paper also gives squared-log bounds for minimum-cost cover, a 4-approximation for min-sum cover, data-dependent upper bounds (Lemma 6), and a lazy greedy variant (Algorithm 2) that ran about 3.5-7x faster on 46 sensors and 30-38x faster on 357 sensors. Its limits matter as much as its results. The prior must be known. Sampled marginal gains carry only additive error bounds. The myopic-feedback influence model was retracted. Theorem 26 shows hardness within O(|E|^(1-eps/beta)) unless PH = Sigma_2^P, but only for a constructed treasure-hunting family of pointwise-modular instances (Sec 12 p.33), not for every objective that lacks adaptive submodularity.
>
> For Melange, the paper mainly sets the scope of the allocation tier's guarantees. The joint optimizer (fec/engine/optimize.py) uses lazy greedy and a G-K data-dependent bound. It correctly states that it is not an adaptive policy: `observations` is empty, and modeled threshold conversions void the certificate. In its fixed-state case, the separable concave structure gives an exact grid optimum, which is stronger than (1-1/e). The budget frontier, the coalition allocator and the dashboard's budgetRecruit are a different matter. Each claims or implies submodular guarantees that this paper does not supply, because of MC=20 sampled marginals, a competitive two-colour kernel, per-candidate fac or target-distance weights, and singleton-marginal water-fill. The seed gate's independent per-seed take-up matches the structure of the paper's Theorem 16. None of these systems observes real outcomes, so the adaptive half of the paper is not built.

>
> The single most load-bearing reference for the operational layer. Generalizes submodular set functions to *adaptive policies* — choose, observe the outcome, choose again — and proves that under adaptive submodularity a simple adaptive-greedy policy is competitive with the optimal policy, inheriting the classic (1 − 1/e) guarantee. Critically, the authors were the first to study influence maximization under the adaptive model, selecting seeds sequentially with each choice made after observing the spread of prior ones. This is the formal IM analog of D* Lite's incremental replanning: a greedy routine that re-optimizes as intelligence arrives, with a provable bound. The practical caveat — and the reason one still drifts toward learned methods — is that it presumes exact expected spread, which is NP-hard to compute in general.

## Study

**Question:** Can the diminishing-returns property of classical submodular set functions be generalized to adaptive policies, so that a simple adaptive greedy policy is near-optimal for partially observable stochastic maximization, minimum-cost coverage and min-sum cover problems, with applications to sensor placement, stochastic set cover, viral marketing and active learning?

**Theory:** Adaptive submodularity (Definition 3, Eq. 11, p. 8): for partial realizations psi subset psi', the conditional expected marginal benefit Delta(e|psi) of any fixed item e must satisfy Delta(e|psi) >= Delta(e|psi'). It is defined relative to a known prior distribution p(phi) over realizations, and reduces to classical submodularity when the prior is deterministic (p. 6-7). Adaptive monotonicity (Definition 2, p. 8) is nonnegativity of Delta(e|psi). Strong adaptive monotonicity and strong adaptive submodularity (Definitions 10 and 12, pp. 13-14) are stronger conditions needed for the average-cost bounds. Pointwise submodularity plus adaptive submodularity implies strong adaptive submodularity (Lemma 39, p. 45). The authors show pointwise submodularity alone does not imply adaptive submodularity (counterexample, p. 14).

**Data:** Theoretical core: no empirical sample. Items are ground-set elements (e.g., sensor locations, graph nodes, unlabeled examples or hypotheses) with a Bayesian prior over realizations. Experiments (Section 10, pp. 29-32): (a) Intel Research Berkeley temperature data, 46 sensors, sampled every 30 seconds for 5 consecutive days starting 28 Feb 2004, covariance estimated empirically; (b) traffic speed data from 357 sensors on I-880 South, California, working days 6 AM-11 AM for one month. Unit of analysis: sensor location (item) and realization. The Figure 5 experiments use a 50% (p_fail = 0.5) failure rate; the lazy-evaluation curves are averaged over 100 runs (temperature) and 10 runs (traffic) per Figure 5 captions.

**Method:** Formal analysis: define policies over partial realizations, define adaptive monotonicity and submodularity, prove greedy guarantees by a Nemhauser-style argument extended to adaptive policies (Theorem 5 with a lemma on budgeted policies), average- and worst-case cover bounds via a charging/integration argument (Theorems 13-14, Lemma 47), a min-sum bound via a geometric argument of Feige et al. (2004) (Theorem 15), data-dependent upper bounds via a greedy linear program (Lemma 6, Lemma 37, Algorithm 3), a lazy/accelerated greedy (Algorithm 2), application reductions (Sections 6-9), a gap construction (Theorem 25), and a hardness reduction from a permanent-based puzzle (Feige and Lund 1997) with treasure-hunting instances (Theorem 26). Empirical part: Gaussian-process entropy objective I(X_V; Y_A) for sensor selection with failure probability, comparing naive vs. lazy adaptive greedy by wall-clock time and count of objective evaluations, and comparing the data-dependent bounds beta_avg to the greedy reward.

**Identification:** Not applicable. This is a theoretical approximation-guarantee paper; no causal identification strategy. Guarantees are conditional on the adaptive submodularity property holding with respect to the stated (known) prior p(phi), and on complexity-theoretic assumptions for the hardness results.

**Scope:** Scope is the class of adaptive stochastic optimization problems over finite item sets with known priors (sensor placement with unreliable sensors; stochastic set cover; adaptive influence/viral marketing under the independent cascade model with full-adoption feedback; pool-based active learning and generalized binary search; optimal decision trees). No human population, institution, legislature, election, lobbying, or text corpus is studied. Era: conference version 2010, journal 2011, arXiv revision 2017.

## Key findings

- Adaptive greedy policy with budget k achieves at least (1 - 1/e) of the value of the best adaptive policy for adaptive monotone submodular objectives; an alpha-approximate greedy policy achieves (1 - e^{-l/(alpha k)}) when run for l steps. (1 - 1/e; general form 1 - e^{-l/(alpha k)}; Theorem 5, Section 5.1, p. 11; Theorem 16 (Section 6, p. 17))
- Adaptive submodularity generalizes classical submodularity; for deterministic priors it reduces to the classical notion, and the greedy guarantee of Nemhauser et al. (1978) carries over. (; Section 3.1-3.2, pp. 6-8; Conclusions p. 36)
- Average-cost minimum cover: greedy policy costs at most alpha * cavg(opt) * (ln(Q/(delta*eta)) + 1)^2 in general instances, and alpha * cavg(opt) * (ln(Q/eta) + 1)^2 for self-certifying instances. Worst-case cost bound (Theorem 14) is alpha * cwc(opt) * (ln(Q/(delta*eta)) + 1), with the self-certifying worst-case log bound left open. (squared-log factor for average cost; single-log factor for worst cost; delta = min prior probability; eta = coverage gap; Theorem 13, Section 5.2.1, p. 14; Theorem 14, Section 5.2.2, p. 14; Discussion Section 5.2.3, p. 15)
- Historical correction: an earlier version claimed logarithmic (not squared-logarithmic) average-cost factors; the authors say the proof was flawed, as pointed out by Nan and Saligrama (2017). Whether the log bounds hold, and whether strong adaptive submodularity is required, are open. (; Historical Note, Section 5.2.1, p. 14)
- Feige's (1998) Set Cover hardness implies no polynomial-time (1 - epsilon) ln(Q/eta) approximation for self-certifying Adaptive Stochastic Min Cost Cover, and no (1 - epsilon) ln(Q/(delta*eta)) approximation for general instances, under NP not in DTIME(n^{O(log log n)}); the worst-case self-certifying log bound remains open. (; Section 5.2.3, p. 15; Lemma 50, Section 15.4, p. 55)
- Adaptive min-sum cover: greedy policy is a 4 alpha approximation to the min-sum cost (Adaptive Stochastic Min-Sum Cover), extending Feige, Lovasz and Tetali (2004) and Streeter and Golovin (2008). (c_Sigma(pi) <= 4 alpha c_Sigma(pi*); Theorem 15, Section 5.3.2, p. 16; proof Section 15.5, pp. 56-57)
- Stochastic submodular maximization with multiple failure types (generalizing Asadpour et al. 2008 all-or-nothing failures): the greedy policy obtains (1 - 1/e) of the best adaptive policy when sensor states are independent and the utility is monotone submodular. (1 - 1/e; Theorem 16, Section 6, p. 17)
- Stochastic set cover with arbitrary per-item set distributions and independent sampling: the adaptive greedy algorithm is a (ln n + 1)^2 approximation, where n = |U|, improving on Liu et al. (2008) H_n for two-state items. ((ln(n)+1)^2; H_n = sum 1/x for x=1..n; Corollary 18, Section 7.1, p. 19; Liu et al. comparison p. 19)
- Adaptive viral marketing under the independent cascade model with full-adoption feedback is adaptive submodular for any monotone submodular reward, so greedy seeding achieves 1 - 1/e of the best adaptive policy; a minimum-cost cover version has a (ln(Q/eta)+1)^2 guarantee. (1 - 1/e; (ln(Q/eta)+1)^2; Theorem 19, Section 8.2, p. 21 (proof pp. 21-23); Theorem 20, Section 8.3, p. 23)
- The authors retract an earlier claim that viral marketing is adaptive submodular under the myopic feedback model, giving a 3-node counterexample (edges u->v with p=1 and v->w with p=1-epsilon); they conjecture a constant-factor greedy guarantee still holds there. (p_uv = 1, p_vw = 1 - epsilon; Delta(w|psi) = epsilon < 1 = Delta(w|psi'); Section 8.2.1 'A Comment on the Myopic Feedback Model', p. 23)
- Generalized binary search (GBS) for pool-based active learning with a Bayesian prior: expected number of queries is at most OPT * (ln(1/min_h p_H(h)) + 1)^2; with a uniform prior, OPT * (ln(|H|-1)+1)^2; with the modified prior p'(h) proportional to max(p_H(h), 1/|H|^2) the factor becomes O((ln|H|)^2). (OPT(ln(1/min p_H)+1)^2; OPT(ln(|H|-1)+1)^2; O((ln|H|)^2); Theorem 21, Section 9, p. 25; uniform prior Section 9.2, p. 26; arbitrary priors Section 9.3, p. 27; highly nonuniform priors Section 9.4, p. 28)
- Gupta et al. (2010) achieve O(log|H|) for active learning / optimal decision trees with non-uniform costs and general priors using a non-greedy algorithm; the paper states this removes the cost and probability dependence from the bound. (O(log|H|); Section 9.5, p. 29)
- Lazy (accelerated) adaptive greedy gives large speedups without loss of solution quality on sensor selection with unreliable sensors, because submodularity lets stale marginal gains serve as upper bounds. (temperature data (46 sensors): roughly 3.5-7x fewer evaluations/time; traffic data (357 sensors): 30-38x; Section 4.4, p. 9 (intro summary: roughly 4 to 40 times); Section 10.1.2, pp. 30-31)
- Data-dependent upper bounds beta_avg (average of k+1 bounds along the realized trajectory) are tighter than the worst-case guarantee in the sensor experiments; lazy-bound versions are almost as tight as eager versions with slightly higher variance. (bounds 'tighter than the worst case bounds' (no numeric values given in text layer); Section 10.1.3, pp. 31-32; Lemma 6, Eq. 13, p. 11; Figures 5(e)-(f) p. 32)
- Adaptivity gap: for adaptive submodular objectives, the adaptivity gap of Adaptive Stochastic Min-Sum Cover (and of min-cost cover in that instance) is Omega(n / log n), while for Stochastic Set Coverage without multiplicities it is Omega(|E|) (Goemans and Vondrak 2006); for Max-Coverage Asadpour et al. show a 1 - 1/e gap. (Omega(n/log n); Omega(|E|); 1 - 1/e; Section 11, pp. 31-33; Theorem 25, p. 33)
- Hardness for a constructed family: unless PH = Sigma_2^P, no polynomial-time algorithm approximates Adaptive Stochastic Maximization, Min Cost Coverage, or Min-Sum Cover within O(|E|^{1-epsilon}) on a constructed treasure-hunting family of pointwise-modular instances; this is not shown for every objective lacking adaptive submodularity. (O(|E|^{1-epsilon}); budget factor beta; Theorem 26, Section 12, p. 33 (proof Section 15.7, pp. 57-60); Corollaries 27-28, p. 34)
- Pointwise submodularity alone is not sufficient for adaptive submodularity; counterexample with f(S, phi) = |{e in S: phi(e)=1}| and a two-point prior gives Delta(e|empty) = 1/2 but Delta(e|{(e',1)}) = 1. (1/2 vs 1; Section 3.2 after Definition 12, p. 14 (text reads on p. 14 lines 733-736))

## Limitations

- Guarantees hold only for objectives that satisfy adaptive submodularity with respect to a known prior p(phi); the property is distribution-dependent (p. 8), and the authors note an objective can be adaptive submodular under one prior but not another.
- The prior is assumed known (main text of Sec 2.1, near p. 4). Robustness to prior error is shown only for multiplicative error alpha = d/c (Section 4.3, p. 9).
- Item selection is assumed not to alter the realization, so general POMDPs with action-dependent state changes are outside the framework (Section 3.4, p. 9; Section 13.7, p. 36).
- Treasure-hunting-type instances with strong synergies between items are not covered (Section 3.4, p. 9; Theorem 26, p. 33).
- The average-cost bounds carry squared-logarithmic factors; the authors say the logarithmic version is open, and that the earlier log-factor proof was flawed (Historical Note, p. 14).
- Open: whether strong adaptive submodularity is required for Theorem 13 (p. 14); whether the worst-case self-certifying log bound holds (p. 15).
- Myopic-feedback influence model: adaptive submodularity was retracted and only a constant-factor greedy guarantee is conjectured (p. 23).
- Greedy guarantees assume exact or alpha-approximate greedy selection; sampled marginal gains give only additive error bounds (Section 5.1, p. 11).
- Hardness results rest on complexity assumptions (PH = Sigma_2^P, NP not in DTIME(n^{O(log log n)})), and the Gupta et al. O(log|H|) result is cited rather than proven here (p. 29).
- Experiments are limited to two sensor datasets (46-sensor Berkeley temperature; 357-sensor I-880 traffic), with failure probability 0.5 in the reported runs; no statistical tests and no social-network or real-diffusion data are used. The viral-marketing and active-learning applications are analytic, not empirical.
- The paper treats uniform-cost items in the main text; cost-sensitive results are deferred to the appendix (Section 2.4, p. 6; Appendix 15).

## Gaps and flags

- No adaptive loop. No allocator observes real outcomes, such as cosponsorship or vote movement after a contribution or ask, and conditions later picks on them. optimize.py reserves `observations=[]` but never fills it, so the adaptive half of Golovin and Krause (Theorem 5 relative to the best adaptive policy) is unavailable.
- No explicit prior over member responses. The adaptive guarantees require a known p(phi). SGATE, seed_prob, DTHRESH and PERS are owner constants with no calibrated distribution, and the prospective H1/H2 contract that could estimate them is unscored (F5 underpowered).
- Sampled marginals carry no error control. The frontier and coalition engines rank on MC=20 estimates, while the paper gives only additive-error guarantees for sampled greedy. No MC-size-to-error-bound link is computed beyond the top-k stability check.
- Submodularity of the competitive cascade objective is asserted, not tested. That covers the two-colour first-to-reach race with money bystanders filtered by `n not in s` and an order-dependent tie-break. No numeric diminishing-returns probe like optimize.py's concavity_violations() exists for spread().
- The adaptivity gap is never measured. Theorem 25 shows adaptive versus non-adaptive cost can differ by Omega(n/log n) in cover problems. The system never estimates how much sequential re-targeting on observed outcomes would be worth.
- Min-cost cover versions are not built. A question like 'cheapest set of asks to reach a quota of Q cosponsors' would fall under Theorems 13-14 (squared-log bounds), but only budget-maximization modes exist.
- Citation numbering: optimize.py:108 cites 'JAIR'11 Lemma 5.3 / A.9' for the data-dependent bound. The vault's arXiv v5 copy numbers it Lemma 6 (Eq. 13), with appendix proof Lemma 37. Check against the JAIR print before quoting.
- Overstated guarantee claim: the comment at make_dashboard_html_00.tpl:775-776 claims (1-1/e) for budgetRecruit, but the target mode multiplies each candidate's marginal by (1 + 2 dT[c]), so the selected set is not certified for the reported reach.
- Unverified submodularity assertions: per_topic_coalition.py:122 and scenario.py:10-11 state the coalition spread is submodular, citing Bharathi-Kempe-Salek. Golovin and Krause cover only single-cascade IC (Theorem 19). The implemented kernel adds money-mobilized bystanders filtered against the seed set, an order-dependent first-to-reach tie-break, and a fac weighting in the frontier, none of which this paper licenses.
- Retracted model warning: if any future adaptive recruitment observes only immediate-neighbour responses (myopic feedback), Golovin and Krause retracted adaptive submodularity for that model (p. 23). It must not be cited as covered.
- Paul's margin note on Chen 2023 (pressure from high-ranking members 'not diminishing') describes increasing marginal returns. That would put such pressure outside the adaptive-submodular class (Theorem 26 hardness is shown only for a constructed family, not for all such objectives). Any model encoding it should drop the (1-1/e) language.
