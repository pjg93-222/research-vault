# Mathematical modeling and analysis of a dark money network

**Christopher Couch, William P Fox, Sean F Everton (2016).** *Journal of Defense Modeling and Simulation: Applications, Methodology, Technology, 13(3):343-354.* [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1177/1548512915625337). Theme: [[themes/social-network-analysis|Social Network Analysis]].

> [!abstract] Summary
> Couch, Fox and Everton (2016) take one US dark money network: politically active nonprofits and linked single-member LLCs that pass money between them without disclosing donors. They apply two decision-analysis tools to it. First, they rank the 8 organizations with the highest degree using TOPSIS over eight ORA centrality measures. The authors set the weights themselves through AHP pairwise judgments. Out-degree gets 0.502, total degree 0.145, eigenvector 0.110 and betweenness 0.075, with a consistency ratio of 0.062. Freedom Partners Chamber of Commerce ranks first (TOPSIS score 0.754). It stays first when the out-degree weight is raised, but Center to Protect Patients' Rights overtakes it when the closeness weight is raised. Only the top two match the plain out-degree order, and the authors admit the method produced "no significant changes". Second, they model State versus network as a 2x2 game with payoffs from AHP. The paper reports a State equalizing mix of about 85% exposure through the press and 15% prosecution or legislation; recomputing from Table 7 gives about 84.6% or about 80.2%, depending on which of two conflicting values is used for one DMN cell. There is no outcome data, no time window and no validation, and in the ten-strategy solution the printed x4 = 0.8 breaks the sum constraint (0.08 satisfies it).
> 
> For Paul's systems the paper is mainly a caution about scope and method, not evidence. Mentat's PAC transfer graph follows only FEC-reported committee cash, so it cannot see the nonprofit layer this paper maps. The retired cosponsor InfluenceScore has the same shape as the paper's composite: a hand-weighted mix of centralities led by out-degree. Neither the paper nor the system checks such a composite against an outcome. The paper's AHP consistency ratio and one-weight sensitivity sweep are reusable ways to document owner-judgment weights such as the pillar blend. They do not make those weights valid. The game theory section has no counterpart in the system.

## Study

**Question:** How can social network analysis of a single US dark money network (politically active nonprofits that take unlimited donations without disclosing donors) be made more rigorous by (a) ranking key nodes with a multi-criteria method (AHP/TOPSIS) instead of a subjective choice of centrality measure, and (b) turning the State-versus-network contest into a game with cardinal payoffs so that kinetic (prosecution/legislation) versus non-kinetic (exposure via press) strategy mixes can be solved for (Sections 1-2, 4; abstract).

**Theory:** Dark network analysis from the social network analysis literature (covert networks, key-node identification by centrality), combined with multi-criteria decision analysis (AHP and TOPSIS) and two-player game theory with cardinal utility (Nash equilibrium, Nash arbitration, security levels). The paper cites Krebs (2002) and Everton (2012) and argues for disrupting donor anonymity through targeted removal of nodes or through press exposure.

**Data:** No new sample. The unit is the organization (node) in one dark money network (DMN) that the authors analyzed in earlier unpublished work. Network-level description: a money-flow network in which ties mean funds were exchanged, node size is betweenness, tie width is dollar amount, and some nodes attended a strategy event (Figure 1, Section 1). Analytic set: the 8 organizations with the highest overall degree centrality, computed in ORA (Table 1, Section 2). Centrality values come from ORA, which lists only the top ten per metric, so some organizations have missing values (Section 4). Expert inputs: AHP pairwise judgments by the authors on Saaty's nine-point scale (criteria weights, Table 4, CR = 0.062; game payoffs, CR = 0.02244 for State and 0.03248 for DMN). No time period is stated for the network data. The number of nodes in the full DMN and the dollar amounts are not reported numerically.

**Method:** Three linked components. (1) AHP criterion weighting over eight SNA measures (out-degree, total degree, eigenvector, betweenness, hub, in-degree, closeness, authority), with out-degree ranked first (weight 0.50199) and authority last (0.03418) (Table 2, Table 4, Section 2). (2) TOPSIS ranking of the 8 organizations on the unscaled ORA values using those weights (Table 5). Sensitivity analysis follows Alinezhad and Amini (Eq. 1): change one weight in 0.1 steps and rescale the others proportionally (Section 3). (3) Game theory on a 2x2 State (non-kinetic A vs kinetic B) by DMN (maintain centralized C vs decentralize D) game. Ordinal ranks 1-4 (Table 6) are converted to cardinal utilities by AHP (Table 7). Solutions use Nash equilibrium, mixed equalizing strategies, prudential (security-level) strategies and Nash arbitration (Feix solver; Fox lottery-alternative approach). A 5-action State vs 2-action DMN extension uses a nonlinear program (Barron, Equation 2), solved in Maple 15 (Section 4).

**Identification:** None. This is a descriptive decision-analysis and modeling paper with no causal identification strategy, no treatment or comparison group, and no estimation of effects from data. Robustness is checked only by one-weight sensitivity analysis (Section 3).

**Scope:** Population: one US politically active dark money network of nonprofits and linked LLCs (SMLLCs), with the State modeled as the opposing player. Institutional frame: IRS rules on 501(c) political activity and disclosure, with sources cited to 2010-2014 news and IRS materials (accessed 2014-2015). The paper says the network is currently legal and that the State's kinetic options are prosecution, fines and legislation. Methodological scope is general: the authors state the method applies to any dark network. Era: circa 2010-2015 (inferred from cited sources and access dates).

## Key findings

- Out-degree is weighted most heavily (0.50199), followed by total degree (0.14517) and eigenvector (0.11045); the AHP consistency ratio is 0.062, under the 0.10 threshold. (weights 0.50199, 0.14517, 0.11045, 0.07489, 0.05459, 0.04378, 0.03525, 0.03418; CR = 0.062; Section 2, Table 2 and Table 4, p. 346-347)
- TOPSIS ranks Freedom Partners Chamber of Commerce first, Center to Protect Patients' Rights second, and Corner Table LLC third. Corner Table has low out-degree (7) but ranks third. TC4 Trust, third in out-degree (38), ranks seventh. Only the top two organizations match the out-degree ranking. (TOPSIS score: 0.7538 (FPCC, out-degree 90), 0.6192 (CPPR, out-degree 78), 0.4909 (Corner Table, out-degree 7), 0.4887 (American Future Fund), 0.4869, 0.4837, 0.4835 (TC4 Trust, out-degree 38), 0.4817; Section 2 and Table 5, p. 346-347)
- Sensitivity: raising out-degree weight in 0.1 steps until it is no longer the largest weight leaves Freedom Partners Chamber of Commerce ranked first. Raising the closeness weight, which the paper calls the lowest criterion (Table 4 gives authority the lowest weight, 0.03418, and closeness 0.03525), lets Center to Protect Patients' Rights overtake Freedom Partners. (Figures only; no numeric thresholds given in text; Section 3, Figure 4 p. 348 and Figure 5)
- Ordinal game equilibrium is AD (State non-kinetic, DMN decentralize). Cardinal AHP utilities give State values AD 0.480038, BD 0.28523, AC 0.139265, BC 0.095467 (CR 0.02244) and DMN values BC 0.480645, BD 0.290615, AD 0.137848, AC 0.090892 (CR 0.03248). (State and DMN AHP utilities as listed; Nash equilibrium AD at (0.48, 0.137); Section 4, Tables 6-7, p. 348-350)
- The paper reports a mixed equalizing strategy for the State of non-kinetic 84.8385% and kinetic 15.1615%. The paper says this replaces the earlier 50/50 recommendation, and the authors conclude that about 85% of effort should go to non-kinetic methods. Table 7 (DMN cell BC = 0.548) and the text (0.480645) conflict, and the figure is not exactly reproduced. (84.8385% non-kinetic; 15.1615% kinetic; Section 4 and Section 5, p. 349-351 and p. 352)
- Prudential (security-level) strategies: the State plays A (non-kinetic) with security level 0.139, which reproduces. The paper says the DMN plays C always at 0.137, but Table 7 gives the DMN's C security level as 0.091; its maximin strategy is D at 0.137848. (Section 4, Figure 6, p. 349)
- Nash arbitration places the outcome near BD, projected to the Pareto segment at about (0.31, 0.3192), which reproduces approximately (about 0.309, 0.320). ((0.31, 0.3192); Section 4, Figure 8, p. 351)
- Extension to a 5-action State by 2-action DMN game (decomposed actions A1-A3 kinetic, B1-B2 non-kinetic) with a nonlinear program. Solution: State randomizes between two actions, and DMN centralizes with probability 1. The printed x4 = 0.8 breaks the sum constraint; x4 = 0.08 satisfies it and reproduces q = 0.0595 and Z of about -0.0012 at p = 0.084. The paper's objective value is Z = -0.001129. (Z = -0.001129; p = 0.084; q = 0.0595; x2 = 0.92; y1 = 1; Section 4, Equation 2 and the solution after it, p. 351-352)

## Limitations

- The authors say explicitly that no significant changes resulted from the MADM analysis, though the findings were improved (Section 5, p. 352).
- All criterion weights and game payoffs come from the authors' own subjective pairwise judgments on Saaty's scale. No external validation of weights or payoffs is reported. Sensitivity checks cover only one weight changed at a time (Section 3).
- Single network, eight alternatives, unknown time period, and no reported N for the full network. Findings cannot be generalized to other dark networks without further study, although the authors state the method applies to any dark network.
- ORA reports only the top ten nodes per metric, so some centrality values are missing for lower-ranked organizations (Section 4).
- Kinetic and non-kinetic are defined by the authors: kinetic means IRS-related prosecution or fines, legislation on non-profit political activity, donation transparency and LLC anonymity rules; non-kinetic means press exposure of donors (Section 4, p. 348). The payoff structure depends on these definitions.
- Game results depend on the assumed payoff framing. The authors state that anonymity and money flow are the DMN's main goals. The paper also includes a partisan framing of the opposing side ('Liberal leadership in the United States is unable to conduct' kinetic operations, Section 4), which is the authors' premise, not a tested finding.
- The nonlinear solution as printed is internally inconsistent: x2 = 0.92 and x4 = 0.8 sum to 1.72, not 1. The value x4 = 0.08 satisfies the constraint and reproduces the reported q and Z, so the intended value is recoverable from the paper's own constraints.
- Game-theory analysis treats the State and the DMN as two rational players with fixed payoffs. Structural dynamics such as DMN adaptation or donor response are not modeled.
- Sources are news and advocacy reports from 2010-2014 (for example references 13, 15, 16, 18, 19), and the authors do not state dollar amounts or donor counts for the network analyzed.

## Gaps and flags

- Mentat has no non-disclosing nonprofit (501(c)(4)/(c)(6), SMLLC) layer upstream of reporting committees. Industry money routed through such conduits enters the transfer graph only at the receiving committee, so attributed industry totals are a lower bound on that channel. The paper shows this layer exists and is organized as a network.
- No recorded weight-sensitivity sweep exists for the four-pillar blend. A one-weight-at-a-time proportional-rescale sweep (paper Eq. 1) is a cheap answer to the open question on top-133 overlap between weight sets.
- Owner-judgment tables (pillar weights, IMP weights, SGATE, PERS) have no documented elicitation or internal-consistency check. AHP's consistency ratio is one way to record that judgments are at least coherent. It would not validate them.
- Neither the paper nor the system validates a weighted-centrality composite against an outcome. The retired InfluenceScore still feeds the underdog lens without such a test.
- D-025: the paper's TOPSIS output (Table 5, larger is better) frames high scorers as key or influential nodes. If any of this is ported, label it a heuristic model output: not network closeness, access, or influence probability.
- The paper's weights and game payoffs are the authors' own Saaty-scale judgments with no external validation. Do not cite it as evidence that out-degree should dominate a centrality composite.
- The printed nonlinear-program solution has x4 = 0.8 (weights sum to 1.72); quote it only with the corrected x4 = 0.08.
- The game's premise contains a partisan framing of the State player, which is the authors' assumption, not a finding. Keep it out of any Melange or Mentat surface.
- Neither rejected SNA proposal (dropping regular cosponsorship edges; per-edge volume normalization) is argued for here. Tie width = dollars is display, not normalization.
