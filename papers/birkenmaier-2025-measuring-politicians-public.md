# Measuring Politicians' Public Personality Traits Using Computational Text Analysis: A Multimethod Feasibility Study for Agency and Communion

**Lukas Birkenmaier, Clemens Lechner (2025).** *Political Analysis (2025), 00, 1-19 (received 20 April 2025; revised 23 September 2025; accepted 23 September 2025).* [10.1017/pan.2025.10025](https://doi.org/10.1017/pan.2025.10025). Theme: [[themes/personality-psychometrics|Personality & Psychometrics]].

> [!abstract] Summary
> Birkenmaier and Lechner ask whether a politician's *public* personality, meaning the traits they project rather than their intrinsic traits, can be measured from text. They use Brunswik's lens model: observers only ever see cues, and those cues mix true personality with strategic self-presentation. The operational definitions come from the PoliPers framework. Agency is Assertiveness & Will to Take Control. Communion is Empathy & Compassion. Only the positive poles count, and cues are split into strong and weak.
> 
> The data are German sentences, 15,000 each from Bundestag speeches, broadcast interviews and Facebook posts (about 45,000 in all, a total the paper does not state), covering 46 party leaders from 2017 to 2024. Few-shot prompting of DeepSeek-V3 and GPT-4o reaches macro-F1 0.74-0.77. Fine-tuned XLM-R reaches 0.70, bag-of-words SVM 0.55-0.63, and Llama 3-8B about 0.55-0.60. Human coders reach alpha 0.70-0.78, which the paper calls acceptable (moderate in its Sec 5 summary), and coders label 89-94% of sentences as having no cue. The paper reports strong-only definitions as better than strong plus weak, but the Table 2 gap (0.77 vs 0.76) is inside the reported SDs. On strong labels only few-shot GPT-4o and DeepSeek-V3 classify all 18 functional-test units; on weak plus strong labels only DeepSeek-V3 does.
> 
> For external validity, communion share falls from left to right (LINKE 19% down to AfD and FDP about 6%). In the one 2024 debate (machine-translated to German; Trump n = 655 and Harris n = 320 utterances), 63% of Trump's cue sentences were agency and 59% of Harris's were communion; cues made up about 10% of Trump's and 23% of Harris's utterances.
> 
> For Melange, this paper is the direct method source for `fec/members/agency_communion_llm.py`. That file copies the codebook, the positive-pole rule and the 18-unit test, and it reproduces the left-greater-than-right communion pattern (D .099 vs R .049). But the build departs from the paper in ways that matter:
> - It uses a different model family (Claude Sonnet, in English).
> - It keeps weak cues in the score.
> - It samples only self-referential sentences from the speeches table, 40 per member.
> - It has no human-labelled macro-F1 test set.
> 
> The paper says nothing to support the next step the system takes: turning projected persona into susceptibility to lobbying pressure. That mapping (.60 z(communion) − .40 z(agency) in the receptivity layer) is a design prior. Its one behavioural test so far (H4, +0.067 on Nokken-Poole drift) is below the threshold. The paper also gently undercuts the Ramey/Mairesse lexical lineage behind the Big Five reader.

## Study

**Question:** Can computational text analysis validly measure politicians' public personality, operationalized as the agency and communion traits they project in speeches, interviews and social media posts? Which measurement strategy (supervised SVM, fine-tuned XLM-RoBERTa, or zero/few-shot prompting of generative LLMs) yields the most valid measure? (Sections 1 and 3.)

**Theory:** Brunswik (1952) lens model (Fig. 1): observers never see intrinsic personality (Y_IPT) directly; they infer it from observable personality cues X1...Xn, which are shaped by intrinsic personality and by strategic self-presentation (Y_S) (Sec. 2.2, p.3). Two problems follow: cue validity (traits are fuzzy, and some are hard to observe) and cue utilization (behaviors are equifinal/multifinal and viewers' partisan biases shape interpretation) (Sec. 2.3, pp.3-4). Trait choice: agency and communion as "meta-traits" (Bakan 1966; Hogan 1982: "get ahead" vs "get along") (Sec. 3.2.1.1, p.7). Operational definitions come from the Lechner et al. (2025) PoliPers domain-specific politician framework (15 facets): agency = Assertiveness & Will to Take Control; communion = Empathy & Compassion, positive poles only (p.7). Strong cues are direct self-statements; weak cues are indirect (p.7). Background: candidate-centered personalization (McAllister 2007; Pedersen and Rahat 2021) and congruency/heuristic accounts of voter preference (Caprara and Zimbardo 2004) (Sec. 2.1, p.3).

**Data:** Corpus: 46 leading German politicians (leaders or co-leaders of party and/or parliamentary-group, covering all six Bundestag parties; list in Supplementary App. A), 2017-2024, spanning two full Bundestag terms (Sec. 3.1, p.5). Three sources: (1) 99 interviews with political leaders in five broadcast formats (PolInterviews dataset; Birkenmaier, Sieber and Bergstein 2025) (p.5); (2) a 300,000-post subsample of Facebook posts drawn from a raw corpus of 6.7 million politician posts (p.6); (3) German Bundestag speeches from the ParlSpeech V2 German subset (Rauh and Schwalbach 2020) (p.6). Unit of analysis: individual sentences/statements. Main corpus: a random sample of 15,000 sentences per source, so 45,000 sentences in total (implied; the text does not state the total) (p.6). Human annotation: 1,000 sentences coded by three annotators (the first author and two RAs) for agreement; a human-labeled corpus with at least 200 examples per class (agency, communion, none) for weak and strong cues (pp.7-8). Classifier evaluation set: the Fig. 3 caption reports n = 600 (balanced and unbalanced); the split is not described further in the text (p.10). Functional test set: 18 units (p.11). Partisan validation: party-level CHES expert positions (Jolly et al. 2022) for the full unlabeled corpus. US debate: transcripts of the 10 September 2024 Trump-Harris debate from the American Presidency Project, machine-translated EN to DE with the DeepL API; Trump n = 655 utterances, Harris n = 320 (p.13).

**Method:** Measurement workflow (Fig. 2, p.6): codebook development and manual coding, then computational classification with three strategies, then validation under the ValiText framework (Birkenmaier, Wagner and Lechner 2024). Labels: agency / communion / none, with a one-sentence rationale per prediction for prompting (p.8). Strategy 1 (supervised): linear SVM on bag-of-words, following Ramey et al. (2019), with fivefold cross-validation and 80/20 splits (p.8). Strategy 2 (semisupervised): fine-tuned XLM-RoBERTa base (279M parameters), threefold CV with 80/20 splits (p.8). Strategy 3 (prompting, no training): GPT-4o (proprietary), Llama 3-8B and DeepSeek-V3, each zero-shot and few-shot, with templates tailored to strong-only vs strong+weak datasets (pp.8-9). Dataset variants: strong-only vs weak+strong; balanced vs unbalanced (where "none" is twice as frequent as each trait class) (p.8). Each model run with three random seeds; metric is macro-F1 averaged across runs (pp.9-10). Validation: (a) agreement with the human test set (macro-F1; Krippendorff's alpha for human coders); (b) structural/functional tests on 18 units (6 self-report items from the Lechner et al. inventory, 6 opposing-pole items expected to be "other", 6 unrelated corpus sentences expected to be "other"); (c) sensitivity to partisan variation using party CHES positions and the debate. Results are reported only for DeepSeek-V3 in the partisan section because it was the only model to pass all earlier steps (p.12).

**Identification:** No causal identification strategy. Validity is argued through convergence with human labels (macro-F1), structural functional tests, and expected correlations with ideology (CHES) and with the 2024 debate's widely reported Trump/Harris contrast (Hyatt et al. 2018). The authors state that the relative weight of intrinsic personality and strategic self-presentation in any cue cannot be determined, and say this does not matter because the goal is to automate the judgments the public would form from the cues (Sec. 2.2, p.3).

**Scope:** Population: 46 leading German politicians (all six Bundestag parties) plus two US presidential-debate candidates. Institutions: German Bundestag (speeches and interviews) and social media. Era: 2017-2024 for German data; one debate on 10 September 2024. Language: German (the US debate was translated). Unit: sentence. Traits: only agency and communion, verbal cues only (no paraverbal, nonverbal or video). Authors position it as a feasibility and measurement-validation study, not a substantive test of drivers of personality change.

## Key findings

- Prompt-based classification with DeepSeek-V3 is the best-performing strategy on the human-labeled test data; GPT-4o is close behind. Macro-F1 is about 0.74-0.77 across the top configurations. (Sec. 4.2, Table 2, p.10) — DeepSeek-V3 few-shot, strong, balanced: 0.77 (SD 0.02); DeepSeek-V3 few-shot, weak+strong, balanced: 0.76 (SD 0.01); GPT-4o few-shot, strong, balanced: 0.76 (SD 0.01); DeepSeek-V3 few-shot, strong, unbalanced: 0.77 (SD 0.01); GPT-4o few-shot, strong, unbalanced: 0.74 (SD 0.00); DeepSeek-V3 zero-shot, strong, unbalanced: 0.74 (SD 0.01)
- Supervised and semisupervised baselines underperform. The SVM is best on strong balanced data; XLM-RoBERTa is best overall among the non-prompting methods. Llama 3-8B is weakest and most variable across runs. (Sec. 4.2, pp.10-11) — SVM: F1 0.63 strong balanced (large SD), 0.59 weak+strong balanced, 0.55 and 0.61 on the two unbalanced sets. XLM-RoBERTa: 0.70 strong balanced (best non-prompt result). Llama 3-8B: about 0.60 balanced and 0.55 unbalanced.
- No model exceeds macro-F1 0.80. Human agreement is acceptable (the paper calls it moderate in Sec. 5). (Sec. 4.1 and Sec. 5, p.9 and p.14; Table 1, p.10) — Krippendorff's alpha for cue presence: .73 strong, .72 weak+strong. Agency alpha .70 (strong) and .71 (weak+strong); communion .78 and .74.
- Human coders mostly label sentences as no cue. Among cue sentences, communion is more common than agency. (Sec. 4.1, p.9) — 'none' is 94% of strong-cue sentences and 89% of weak+strong sentences. Among cue sentences, communion is about 56% (strong) and 63% (weak+strong); agency about 44% and 38%.
- Few-shot GPT-4o and DeepSeek-V3 pass the functional tests reliably; on strong labels both classify all 18 units correctly, and on weak+strong labels DeepSeek-V3 alone does. SVM, XLM-RoBERTa and Llama 3-8B often fail to reach a 50% pass rate. (Sec. 4.3, Fig. 4, pp.11-12) — 18 test units: 6 self-report items, 6 opposing-pole items, 6 unrelated corpus sentences. Few-shot GPT-4o and DeepSeek-V3 get all 18 right on strong labels; zero-shot results are described as satisfactory with minor errors.
- Party-level communion tracks the left-right positions of parties on both CHES dimensions: a clear negative relation between CHES position and communion share. (Sec. 4.4.1, pp.12-13, Fig. 5) — Communion share by party: LINKE 19%, Greens 12%, SPD 10%, CDU 8%, AfD and FDP about 6% each. The text reports regression lines and standard-error bars but no coefficients, N or CIs in the main text.
- Agency at party level has only a weak positive link with the economic CHES dimension and none with the cultural dimension (footnote, Appendix L). (Sec. 4.4.1, footnote 11, p.13) — No coefficient reported in the main text.
- In the 2024 debate the measure separates the two candidates as expected: Harris shows more personality cues overall, and the agency/communion balance reverses between them. (Sec. 4.4.2, Fig. 6, pp.13-14) — Personality-cue share: Harris about 23%, Trump about 10%. Shares of each candidate's cue sentences: Trump 63% agency / 37% communion; Harris 59% communion / 41% agency. Utterances: Trump 655, Harris 320; one debate, machine-translated from English to German.
- Communion is higher on social media than in interviews; agency varies less across sources. Female politicians show higher communion. (Footnote 12 (Appendices M and N), p.13) — No values reported in the main text.
- Validity claim summary: prompt-based methods, particularly GPT-4o and DeepSeek-V3, allow the most valid measurement; the paper reports strong-only cue definitions as better than weak+strong, by a gap inside its own SDs. (Sec. 5, p.14) — Strong-only definitions performed better, as the authors expected; no delta is reported in the main text.

## Limitations

- Only two traits (agency and communion) are measured; the authors call for expanding to other traits (p.15).
- Verbal cues only; paraverbal and nonverbal cues (rhetoric, gesture, video, audio) are excluded; future work should be multimodal (p.15).
- Sentence-level analysis only; paragraph- or token-level extensions are suggested (p.15).
- Human agreement is alpha .70-.78 (acceptable per the paper; moderate in Sec. 5), and no computational model exceeds macro-F1 0.80, so the ceiling on measurement precision is set by fuzzy constructs (pp.14-15).
- LLM bias: generative models may reflect pretraining biases. The authors report no systematic bias in their checks but cite reports of pro-Chinese bias in DeepSeek models (Huang et al. 2025) and recommend extra validation for politically sensitive content (p.15).
- Small functional-test set (n = 18) and a small debate comparison (two speakers, translated transcripts) (pp.11-13).
- Partisan validation is descriptive at party level in the main text, with no reported inferential statistics; the Chapel Hill comparison relies on six parties (pp.12-13).
- Self-report personality inventories are argued to be susceptible to social desirability (footnote 3, p.4); the paper treats politicians' self-reports as a kind of self-statement cue rather than intrinsic traits.
- Supplementary appendices (codebook, prompts, hyperparameters, extra partisan and gender analyses) are not in the text file reviewed here, so they are unverified.
- Feasibility scope: the paper argues feasibility and does not test explanatory factors for changes in displayed personality (crisis events, role transitions), which it proposes as future work (p.14).

## Gaps and flags

- The English Claude agency/communion instrument has no human-labelled sentence test set (3 coders, codebook, Krippendorff alpha, macro-F1). Only the 18-unit functional gate and the party signature are reproduced, so the paper's 0.74-0.77 cannot be claimed for this model.
- No strong-only variant has been computed, although the paper reports strong-only definitions as better (by a gap inside its SDs). The cache holds strength labels, so this costs nothing to compute.
- Persona comes only from the speeches table (the whole table, which per the code comment holds CREC, INTERVIEW and legacy CHRG rows), with self-referential filtering and 40 sentences per member. The paper shows communion differs by source (higher on social media), and the system has no per-genre persona estimate.
- persona_susc_z is z-scored across parties together (receptivity_layer.py:152-155), even though both the paper and the system show a party gap in communion. There is no within-party standardization and no sex control (the paper reports higher communion for female politicians).
- Persona is a static snapshot. The paper flags role transitions (becoming chair or lead candidate) and crises as drivers of displayed personality, and no time window or change tracking exists.
- There is no audit for partisan or pretraining bias in the LLM classifier, which the paper recommends for political content. The party signature alone cannot separate a real party difference from model bias.
- UNSTRATIFIED: persona_susc_z pools D and R in its z-scores (fec/members/receptivity_layer.py:152-155) across a fault line that both the paper and the system's own party signature (D .099 vs R .049) show. Any figure quoting a persona effect must carry the UNSTRATIFIED label or be split by party.
- Attribution overreach in code comments: receptivity_layer.py:140-142 cites the Birkenmaier-Lechner cue shares and gives the communion→susceptible and agency→resistant mapping in the same comment. The paper makes no claim about susceptibility or pressure response, so the mapping should be attributed to Byrne-style design priors.
- Model mismatch: the paper validated GPT-4o and DeepSeek-V3. The system runs Claude sonnet-5 or fable (agency_communion_llm.py:24). 'Instrument replicates the paper' (brain line 55) should be scoped to the functional gate and the party-direction signature only.
- PERSONA_W 0.30 is a standing owner ruling (do not propose removal). Nothing in this paper is offered as grounds to remove it. Behavioural validity of the channel is still unverified (H4 +0.067, below the floor).

Paul's annotation values the paper's pre-built codebooks and its relevance to node design, and the system has built on them. For persona-agency-communion the codebook, positive-pole rule and 18-unit test are now in agency_communion_llm.py. The weakness he named, converting an individual's findings into a node in a social network graph, is still the open issue; he also named how to disentangle ambiguous statements. The .60/.40 mapping into susceptibility is not from the paper, persona homophily was unearned (|r| <= 0.015), and the Nokken-drift H4 was below the threshold.

The highlight about role transitions changing displayed personality points to a time-varying persona, which is not built.
