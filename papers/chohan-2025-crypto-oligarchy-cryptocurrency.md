# Crypto-Oligarchy: The Cryptocurrency Lobby in American Politics

**Usman W. Chohan (2025).** *Discussion Paper Series: Notes on the 21st Century (cover page, dated 2 January 2025). The SSRN 5080344 number from the metadata hint does not appear in the text and could not be confirmed from it..* Theme: [[themes/campaign-politics|Campaign Politics]].

> [!abstract] Summary
> Chohan's discussion paper is an essay, not an empirical study. It describes Fairshake, the crypto super PAC network that also includes Protect Progress and Defend American Jobs, and argues that the industry has moved from crypto-anarchism through statism to an 'oligarchic' stage, using a Marxian stage model. Its numbers come from the press or from Fairshake itself: $203M raised for the 2024 cycle (p. 7), 253 supported candidates with no win rate and no counterfactual (p. 8), $78M already raised toward 2026 (p. 9), and an unsourced count of 100,000 crypto millionaires (p. 14). It names targeted races: Moreno over Brown in Ohio, against Tester in Montana, against Porter in a primary and against Bowman. It says these show 'decisive' influence (pp. 9-10) but estimates nothing, and it notes that many of the ads never mentioned crypto (p. 8).
> 
> For Paul's systems the paper adds no measurement or method. Its value is descriptive, and it points to three places.
> 
> 1. **Super PAC money is a separate channel.** The paper reports receipts and a few named races and gives no oppose share or channel split. It is qualitative support for Mentat keeping a separate Super PAC channel and a support/oppose field. Separately, as a code observation, Melange's flip model industry money term counts only direct bank and finserv PAC receipts.
> 2. **A large money series appears for 2024 and early 2026.** This does not establish a level break or any earlier history, and it fits the registry decision to decline a crypto forecast for now, pending FEC-sourced totals.
> 3. **Primary-contest plays exist in practice.** Industry money has been used to contest primaries, the same kind of play the electoral-plays module models.
> 
> None of the paper's claims about effects on outcomes can be used as evidence. That applies especially to the competitiveness multiplier, where reciprocity is still not validated.

## Study

**Question:** How has the cryptocurrency lobby, led by the super PAC Fairshake, shaped the 2024 US election and subsequent policy, and does the sector's trajectory from decentralized anarchism through statism to oligarchy mirror Marxian historical materialism? (pp. 4-6, 11)

**Theory:** Marxian historical materialism, applied as a stage model: cryptoanarchism (bands), then statism (city-states), then oligarchy (concentrated capital and power). Also draws on the co-optation of retail "YOLO capitalism" by institutional and oligarchic actors (pp. 11-15).

**Data:** No dataset, sample, unit of analysis, or N. The quantitative content is a handful of press-reported or self-reported figures: Fairshake raised $203 million for the 2024 cycle (p. 7); Fairshake claims 253 pro-crypto candidates supported in 2024 (p. 8); $78 million raised toward the 2026 midterms to date (p. 9); "100,000 crypto millionaires" with no source in the text (p. 14). Sources cited are press and news items (refs 9, 28, 30, 34). Period covered: roughly 2008 to early 2025.

**Method:** Qualitative narrative essay. It combines descriptive reporting of campaign-finance and election events with conceptual argument. No statistical estimation, no coding scheme, no comparison group, and no formal test.

**Identification:** None. The paper makes no causal claim tested by an identification strategy. Its claims that Fairshake "decisively" influenced outcomes (pp. 9-10) are asserted, not estimated.

**Scope:** Population: the US cryptocurrency industry and its federal political activity, mainly the 2024 federal election cycle. Institutions: super PACs (Fairshake, Protect Progress, Defend American Jobs), Congress (a House-passed digital-asset bill, Senate pending), and federal regulators (SEC, CFTC). Era: 2008 to early 2025, with the 2024 election as the focal event. Also comments briefly on non-US jurisdictions (El Salvador, China), without analysis.

## Key findings

- Fairshake raised a record $203 million for the 2024 cycle, more than other major super PACs including MAGA Inc. and Senate Majority PAC. Described as the largest pro-crypto super PAC; figures are press-sourced. (p. 7, section 'The Crypto Lobby and the 2024 Election'; repeated p. 13 as 'more money than any other super PAC') — $203 million (2024 cycle)
- Fairshake targeted specific Senate races. It backed Republican Bernie Moreno against incumbent Democrat Sherrod Brown in Ohio, and opposed Democrat Jon Tester in Montana over his stance on digital currencies. (pp. 7-8) — Ohio and Montana, two named races
- Affiliated PACs Protect Progress and Defend American Jobs ran advertising that often did not mention cryptocurrency, focusing on candidate integrity or economic policy. (p. 8)
- Fairshake claims to have supported 253 pro-crypto candidates in 2024, 'a significant number' of whom won. This is a self-reported figure with no win-rate or counterfactual given. (p. 8) — 253 candidates supported (claimed)
- Major donors Coinbase, Ripple Labs and Andreessen Horowitz each contributed tens of millions of dollars. The paper reads this as a strategy of sustained long-term political investment. (p. 8) — 'tens of millions' per named donor (no exact figures given)
- Fairshake had begun raising for the 2026 midterms. (p. 9) — $78 million raised to date
- The paper asserts Fairshake's campaigns against Katie Porter (California Senate primary) and Jamaal Bowman (NY re-election) show it can 'influence electoral outcomes decisively'. No outcome counterfactual or estimate is offered. (pp. 9-10)
- The House passed a bill creating a digital-asset regulatory framework, which faces challenges in the Senate. The paper presents this as a sign of pro-crypto legislative clout. (p. 10)
- At the oligarchic stage, the sector is dominated by 'whales' and crypto is an asset class for the powerful. 100,000 crypto millionaires are called the primary beneficiaries. The figure is unsourced in the text. (pp. 13-14) — 100,000 crypto millionaires (unsourced)
- A proposed US strategic cryptocurrency reserve is read as state co-optation of crypto. The reserve is described as under consideration, and no details are given. (p. 14 and p. 17)
- Normative conclusion: it is easier to influence the financial system from within than to dismantle it from outside, and the sector is more likely to reinforce existing inequalities than remove them. (pp. 15, 16-17)

## Gaps and flags

- Melange's flip model industry money term counts only direct PAC contributions to members ('banks' and 'finserv'). Whether crypto outside money is absent from it was not traced to the field definitions, and the paper does not give a channel split.
- The model has no deterrence or spillover term. In the paper's account, punishing a visible opponent such as Brown or Tester is a signal to other members. The engine's money terms act only on the member who receives the money.
- Mentat keeps the attributed beneficiary estimate unbuilt (its producer was not traced), so super PAC money cannot yet be tied to candidate-level support or oppose totals.
- Crypto stays declined in the forecast registry. The paper's figures do not establish a level break or per-cycle history, so any admission would need FEC-sourced totals.
- The 'decisive influence' claims (pp. 9-10) and the 253 'supported' candidates (p. 8) have no denominator, no window beyond '2024', no win rate and no counterfactual. Do not quote them as effect evidence, and do not cite them as support for the competitiveness multiplier: reciprocity is NOT validated (D-104..D-108).
- Every dollar figure is press-reported or self-reported ($203M, $78M) or unsourced (100,000 millionaires). Any reuse must name the source and the window (2024 cycle; 2026 to date as of about Jan 2025) and treat the figures as unverified against FEC.
- The paper's 'oligarchy' and influence framing is normative. Do not carry that language onto staffer or revolving-door edges or into graph ranks (D-025).
- The paper makes no SNA proposal and no LDA edge use, so it does not trigger the rejected-proposal or D-021 flags.
