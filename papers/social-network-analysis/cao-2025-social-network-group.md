# Social network group decision making: Characterization, taxonomy, challenges and future directions from an AI and LLMs perspective

**Mingshuo Cao, Tiantian Gai, Jian Wu, Francisco Chiclana, Zhen Zhang, Yucheng Dong, Enrique Herrera-Viedma, Francisco Herrera (2025).** *Information Fusion, vol. 120, article 103107 (available online 21 March 2025; received 17 Dec 2024, accepted 9 Mar 2025).* [10.1016/j.inffus.2025.103107](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.inffus.2025.103107) Theme: [[themes/social-network-analysis|Social Network Analysis]].

> [!abstract] Summary
> Cao et al. (2025, Information Fusion) is a narrative review plus a descriptive bibliometric map of "social network group decision making" (SNGDM). It reports no new data and fits no model. It defines an SNGDM event as at least three decision-makers whose trust or social network shapes how they reach consensus on alternatives. It classifies the literature along three axes: trust modelling (representation, propagation, decay), internal structure (consensus measurement, power, communities) and consensus mechanisms (feedback, DeGroot or Hegselmann-Krause opinion dynamics, managing manipulation or non-cooperation). It then lists directions for LLMs and multimodal methods. The bibliometric base is 917 Web of Science articles (11,090 raw records, 1,800 after screening; snapshot 25 Sept 2024). Every substantive claim is either a taxonomy label or an agenda item. Nothing is estimated, and the authors say no unified standard exists for evaluating LLM or multimodal SNGDM.
>
> For Paul's systems, this paper is a vocabulary source, not evidence. Three points come close to built components. First, it names multiplicative discount-factor trust propagation and calls decay along chains (path length, node importance) systematically unstudied. That describes the uncalibrated 0.8-per-hop factor and 5-year half-life in the staffer router, and the ASKM product in the recruitment chain. Second, it separates single-path propagation operators from trust aggregation over multiple paths. The member recruitment chain uses only the single most reliable path and says in its own comment that this is a floor. Third, its "pseudoconsensus" warning says high agreement inside a subgroup need not mean agreement between subgroups. That fits the Ward press-lean blocs, which are all labelled pro-CU. Its SNGDM populations are small expert panels with elicited or preset trust, not legislators, staffers or lobbyists, so nothing in it validates any constant. All bearings are weak and UNVERIFIED (DE-062).

## Study

**Question:** What is social network group decision making (SNGDM), and how should it be defined, characterized and classified? The paper aims to (1) give a formal definition and characterization of the SNGDM framework relative to classical group decision making; (2) map the literature as a taxonomy of trust modeling, internal network structure and consensus mechanisms; and (3) set out challenges and future directions, including how AI, large language models (LLMs) and multimodal fusion could be used. Stated in the Introduction, pp.2-3.

**Theory:** No single formal theory. The paper builds on trust representation and propagation (Golbeck and Hendler 2006, ref [17]; Kamvar et al. 2003, ref [23]), consensus-reaching processes, and opinion dynamics (DeGroot and Hegselmann-Krause models, refs [9] and [71]). It also draws on the trust game (Berg, Dickhaut and McCabe 1995, ref [88]), implicit power from trust (ref [92]) and crowd wisdom (Almaatouq et al. 2020, ref [93]). Definition 1 of SNGDM is the authors' own.

**Data:** Literature records, not primary data. Bibliometric unit: journal article records from the Web of Science. Raw retrieval: 11,090 records (search window stated as 1990-2024; search snapshot 25 Sept 2024). After manual title/abstract screening: 1,800. After removing duplicates across the four searches: 917 unique articles, which form the bibliometric base (pp.3-4). Table 1 (raw / after screening) by keyword pair: 'Social network' & 'Consensus' 2,720 / 466; 'Social network' & 'GDM' 2,722 / 687; 'Trust' & 'Consensus' 2,776 / 306; 'Trust' & 'GDM' 2,872 / 341; total 11,090 / 1,800; de-duplicated 917. The paper states "No data was used for the research described in the article" (p.16).

**Method:** Narrative review organized as a taxonomy: trust modeling (representation, propagation, evolution), internal structure (consensus measurement, power structure, community structure), and consensus mechanisms (feedback, opinion dynamics, behavior management). Bibliometric step (Section 2.2, p.3): Web of Science Boolean searches on four keyword pairs, manual screening using field and institution shortcuts plus abstract reading, de-duplication. Descriptive outputs only: publications and average citations per year (Fig 2), journals (Fig 3), co-author clusters (Fig 4), keyword co-occurrence (Fig 5). Sections 4 and 5 are agenda-setting arguments with no models fitted or tested. Section 5 covers LLMs and multimodal fusion conceptually; no experiment is run.

**Identification:** None. The paper makes no causal or statistical identification claim. It is a descriptive review and bibliometric mapping.

**Scope:** Population: decision-making research on social network group decision making indexed in Web of Science, search window 1990-2024, snapshot 25 Sept 2024. The abstract calls the window "the past decade"; Section 2.2 uses 1990-2024. Institution: none (WoS only). Era: pre-LLM literature plus a 2024-2025 forward look at LLMs, multimodal models and small language models. Applications used as examples: village chief elections (p.3, illustrative), urban renewal, water-pollution compensation, vaccine uptake (p.1), disaster response, online product reviews, household energy use, and physicians' adoption of online health communities (p.13). No lobbying, campaign, legislator, turnout or persuasion literature is reviewed beyond these examples.

## Key findings

- Definition 1: an SNGDM event needs at least three decision-makers evaluating alternatives, plus a network among them (nodes, edges, edge weights) that shapes information exchange and the outcome. The authors say SNGDM is more than GDM plus social networks because the two processes are coupled. (minimum 3 decision-makers; p.3, Definition 1 and Section 2.1.1 (lines 214-220); coupling claim p.2 (lines 84-86))
- Bibliometric base: four Web of Science keyword pairs returned 11,090 records, manual screening kept 1,800, and de-duplication left 917 articles. The search counts are in Table 1. (11,090 raw; 1,800 screened; 917 unique; 2,720/466, 2,722/687, 2,776/306, 2,872/341 (raw/screened by pair); p.3, Section 2.2 and Table 1 (lines 264-333))
- Origin: Wu and Chiclana (2014, Knowl.-Based Syst. 59) first presented SNGDM. Papers from the inaugural year have the highest average citations, which the authors read as showing the earliest work is of high academic value. Figure values are not in the text. (not stated in text; p.3 (lines 334-343); Fig 2(b) on p.4)
- Keyword analysis: current SNGDM research centers on decision-making methods, social network analysis and consensus-reaching processes. The authors call trust behavior a deeper issue that is gaining importance. (917 articles in the keyword analysis; p.4 (lines 364-379); Fig 5 on p.6)
- Trust representation is classified into four types: binary (trust/distrust), multi-discrete levels, continuous values, and fuzzy-logic values (interval values, intuitionistic fuzzy numbers, trust score spaces). (4 representation categories; p.7, Section 3.1.1 (lines 423-438))
- Trust propagation is characterized by four properties (symmetry, follow-through, directness, divergence). Two approaches are named: propagation operators (six families listed) and trust aggregation over multiple paths. (4 properties; 2 approaches; 6 operator families; p.7, Section 3.1.2 (lines 449-470))
- Trust decay along propagation chains is an active line of work: discount-factor and exponential-decay operators penalize long chains. The authors call for systematic study of decay drivers such as path length and node importance, and of how these affect trust network reliability. (not stated; p.7 (lines 498-510); long-chain propagation effect in Tan et al. at p.7 (lines 493-495))
- Consensus measurement: the authors judge a combined measure of opinion similarity and trust, covering within-subgroup consensus, between-subgroup consensus and trust among members, to be the reasonable approach. (not stated; p.8 (lines 614-627))
- Overlapping communities: Gai et al. (ref [60]) report that overlapping communities raise experts' willingness to compromise relative to non-overlapping communities. The authors treat overlapping nodes as bridges, an area they say needs more work. (not stated; p.9, Section 3.2.3 (lines 699-724))
- Pseudoconsensus: clustering strengthens homogeneity within subgroups but also heterogeneity between them, so high within-subgroup consensus may not mean good overall consistency. (not stated; p.9, Section 3.2.3 (lines 725-731))
- Behavior management: weight manipulation and non-cooperative behavior are treated as threats to consensus. Yang et al. (ref 26) define three non-cooperative categories: bribery, passive participation and potential conflict. The authors argue these should be addressed by regulating trust relations. (3 non-cooperative categories; p.10-11, Section 3.3.3 (lines 860-955); Yang et al. at p.11 (lines 938-941))
- Future directions in SNGDM methods: trust incentives with personality types, trust games, implicit power of trust, crowd wisdom, reinforcement-learning feedback, personality-trait learning via multimodal sentiment analysis, online-review-driven SNGDM, and realistic scenario-driven SNGDM. Each is proposed, not tested. (8 directions (4.1-4.8); pp.11-14, Section 4 (lines 956-1220))
- LLM and multimodal opportunities are listed in six application areas: public opinion monitoring, recommendation, emergency decision making, policy evaluation, communication support, and ethics and fairness. (6 application areas; pp.14-15, Section 5.1 (lines 1251-1321))
- Technological challenges for LLM and multimodal SNGDM are listed as five areas: data heterogeneity, adaptability and generalization, computational cost, lack of unified evaluation standards, and use of small language models. (5 challenges; pp.14-16, Section 5.2 (lines 1322-1385); evaluation point p.15 (lines 1354-1364))
- Conclusion: LLMs, multimodal fusion and AI are named among the main future directions for SNGDM. The authors also raise the need for transparent, ethical and adaptable autonomous AI agents for group consensus, subject to regulation. (not stated; p.15, Section 6 (lines 1386-1447; key bullets lines 1415-1428; AI-agent reflection lines 1434-1447))

## Limitations

- Stated by the authors: no unified standards exist for evaluating the effectiveness of multimodal or LLM integration with SNGDM; LLMs are a black box; training-data bias can affect results; so LLM outputs should be combined with other methods (p.15, lines 1354-1364).
- Stated by the authors: the reliability of trust-evolution mechanisms still needs exploring (p.8, lines 565-567); trust-propagation decay is not yet systematically modelled (p.7, lines 504-510).
- Stated by the authors: most existing SNGDM studies build networks from questionnaires or preset them, rather than from real social-media data (p.13, lines 1166-1169).
- Stated by the authors: no data were used (p.16, line 1484). Sections 4-5 propose directions and make no empirical test, and the LLM and multimodal material is conceptual.
- Screening limitation: manual screening used a field and institution shortcut and deleted records by title for medicine, fisheries and some agricultural and astronomical institutions (p.3, lines 321-331). The search used only four keyword pairs and one database (Web of Science), so SNGDM work using other terms or indexes is excluded (p.3, Table 1).
- Window description: the bibliometric data are fixed at 25 Sept 2024 (p.3, line 332). The abstract (p.1, lines 26-27) calls the window 'the past decade', while Section 2.2 gives 1990-2024 (p.3, line 319). These descriptions are not fully aligned.
- Self-assessment: the authors state they are very familiar with the topic and belong to a significant academic school in the field (p.4, lines 360-363). No bias check is reported.
- Unsourced claims: the personality-trait claims in Section 4.6 (e.g., agreeable types compromise more; extroverts drive trust propagation) carry no citation (p.13, lines 1126-1139). Section 4.5 also states general RL claims without quantitative support.
- Citation-support mismatch to check: the sentence that LLMs such as ChatGPT can build more accurate prediction models for social-network trends cites ref [102] (Zhao et al. 2024, a deep multimodal fusion survey), not an LLM source (p.14, lines 1255-1258).
- Footnote-only citation: the BERT-based online-review study (Wu's laboratory, cruise passenger satisfaction) is attributed by footnote 30 with a DOI rather than by bibliography number (p.13, lines 1162-1165).
- Figure content not available in the text extraction: the numbers in Figs 2-5 (citation trends, journal counts, co-author clusters, keyword network) are not extracted, so the bibliometric figures can be checked only from the PDF.

## Gaps and flags

- No multi-path aggregation in the reported member recruitment chain hold. The system reports only the single best path's product, which the code itself calls a floor.
- No calibrated per-hop decay anywhere: the staffer HOP_FACTOR 0.8, the 5-year access half-life and ASKM 0.07 are all unestimated. The review confirms the field has no standard to borrow, so calibration would need the system's own outcome data, such as an outreach log.
- No check of direction between blocs for press-lean Ward blocs (the pseudoconsensus risk), consistent with the recorded all-pro-CU mislabel.
- The review contains no evidence about legislators, staffers or lobbying. Any citation of it as support for route costs, trust decay or recruitment probabilities would break D-025: route costs and graph scores are heuristics, not closeness or introduction probabilities.
- Neither rejected SNA proposal is argued for (dropping regular cosponsorship edges; per-edge volume normalization). Nothing to flag on that front.
- Section 3.3.3 frames 'bribery' and weight manipulation as threats to consensus. This is SNGDM vocabulary about expert panels. It must not carry over as influence or vote-buying language onto money or revolving-door edges.
- Incidental code observation, not from the paper: fec/stance/stance_dynamics.py:152 prints 'cells with >=3 informative neighbors', but the gate at line 132 defaults MINNB to 5. The log string is stale against the code.
- All bearings here are new single-path agent analysis and stay UNVERIFIED until a second path reproduces them (DE-062).
