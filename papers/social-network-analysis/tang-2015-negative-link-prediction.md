# Negative Link Prediction in Social Media

**Jiliang Tang, Shiyu Chang, Charu Aggarwal, Huan Liu (2015).** *WSDM 2015 (Shanghai, China; Feb 2-6, 2015).* [10.1145/2684822.2685295](https://doi.org/10.1145/2684822.2685295). Theme: [[themes/social-network-analysis|Social Network Analysis]].

> [!abstract] Summary
> Tang, Chang, Aggarwal and Liu (WSDM 2015) ask whether distrust or foe links can be predicted when none are observed, using only positive links and content interactions. They test this on Epinions (14,765 users, 52,704 negative links) and Slashdot (7,275 users, 20,851 negative links). They report three facts. First, negative-link pairs sit close together in the positive network: 82.64% and 87.86% are within 3 hops. Second, signed triads are mostly balanced (92.31% and 93.01%). Third, pairs with negative interactions are far more likely to hold a negative link (t-test p = 5.72e-89 and 1.93e-109). Their SVM, NeLP, adds reliability weights that grow with the interaction count, plus a balance-theory regularizer. Even so, the best F1 on the negative class is only 0.3242 on Epinions and 0.2441 on Slashdot. The simple rule "a negative interaction means a negative link" (negIn) reaches a precision of only 0.2097 and 0.1483. Removing the count-based reliability weights is the largest single-component ablation (precision -8.70% and -11.08%); removing all three components drops precision by 18.1% and 19.5%.
> 
> For Paul's systems, the paper's main point is a caution. Melange's signed member-reference layer builds negative edges directly from LLM-scored negative references. That is the paper's negIn signal, which the paper shows to be a real but low-precision proxy for an antagonistic relationship. Congress has no explicit foe declaration, so the system cannot score its reference signs against ground truth the way Tang does. Its negative edges should therefore be read as a measure of negative interaction, not of negative ties. The diagnostic signed-coalition partition uses these signs with no evidence-count floor, while the dashboard drawing does use one. The paper's balance statistics support the sign-product logic in the two-hop stance composer. An untested extension of Tang's positive-network distance test would ask whether negative-reference dyads are close together in the cosponsor graph. Every result here comes from older online trust-platform snapshots with an unstated collection period, not legislatures.

## Study

**Question:** Can negative (distrust/foe) links be predicted in social media when no negative links are observed, using only positive links and content-centric user interactions (likes/dislikes, ratings, posted opinions)? (Abstract; Sec. 1; Sec. 2, Eq. 1: f: {Gp, A, O} -> Gn.)

**Theory:** Balance theory (Heider; Cartwright and Harary) and status theory (Leskovec et al.) as structural constraints on signed triads, plus the empirical claim that negative interactions (disagreement/antagonism) correlate with negative links. The "enemies are close" pattern (negative-link pairs sit within a few hops in the positive network) motivates the features (Sec. 3.1-3.3).

**Data:** Two sites with explicit trust/distrust or friend/foe links, used for analysis and ground truth. Epinions: 14,765 users, 272,513 positive links, 52,704 negative links, 612,321 posts, 6,937,986 positive opinions, 163,502 negative opinions. Slashdot: 7,275 users, 67,705 positive links, 20,851 negative links, 300,932 posts, 1,742,763 positive opinions, 42,260 negative opinions (Table 1, p. 89). Unit of analysis: ordered user pair. Imbalance of negative to missing pairs is 1:4131 (Epinions) and 1:2534 (Slashdot) (Sec. 4.1, p. 90). Collection period is not stated. Epinions opinions come from helpfulness ratings (>3 positive, <3 negative); Slashdot opinions come from MPQA-lexicon polarity of comments (Sec. 3, p. 88). Users with no positive or negative links were filtered out.

**Method:** Descriptive analysis (shortest-path distributions, balance and status triad counts, a two-sample t-test and ratio curves), then a supervised classifier, NeLP. Labels: missing pairs are positive samples; negative samples come from pairs with negative interactions (Algorithm 1), refined by status-theory removals and additions, with reliability weights W = f(Nij) if Nij > 0, else r, where f(x) = 1 - 1/log(1+x) (Eq. 2; Sec. 4.1, 5.2). Features: 45 per pair (8 user features for each member, 7 pair features, 22 signed features including 16 weighted triads from Leskovec et al.) (Sec. 4.2). Model: soft-margin SVM with separate costs Cp (positives) and Cn (negatives), per-sample negative weights cj, and a balance-theory Laplacian regularizer weighted by Cb (Eqs. 4-6), solved in dual QP form with a kernel (Eqs. 7-15). Parameters: Cp=1, Cn=0.5, Cb=0.1 (Epinions); Cp=1, Cn=0.7, Cb=0.01 (Slashdot) (Sec. 5.2). Baselines: Random; sPath (predict negative for pairs at a fixed shortest-path length); negIn (predict negative for pairs with negative interactions); negInS (negIn refined by status-theory steps); NeLP-negIn (NeLP trained on negIn labels). Parameterized baselines are reported at their best setting (Sec. 5.2, p. 93).

**Identification:** None in a causal sense. Prediction is framed as supervised classification with constructed (noisy) labels. Evidence for signal comes from a t-test and conditional ratios (Sec. 3.3). Generalization is checked by training on one site and testing on the other (Sec. 5.2, Fig. 4). Ablations remove components (Cn, cj, Cb) one at a time (Sec. 5.3, Tables 4-5) and vary Cb over {0, 0.001, 0.01, 0.05, 0.1, 0.5, 1} (Sec. 5.4, Fig. 5). The text does not describe the train/test split, folds, or how evaluation negatives were drawn.

**Scope:** Population: online users of two platforms (Epinions product-review trust network; Slashdot technology-news friend/foe network). No institutional or regulatory setting. Era: not stated; the datasets are pre-2015 snapshots. Claims are limited to these two platforms and to pairs with at least one positive link or interaction. Generalization is tested only between these two sites.

## Key findings

- Negative-link pairs sit close together in the positive network: about 82.64% (Epinions) and 87.86% (Slashdot) are within 3 hops; over 45% have shortest path below 3 and over 80% below 4. (82.64% (Epinions), 87.86% (Slashdot) within 3 hops; Sec. 3.1, p. 89, Fig. 2)
- Triads are mostly balanced and satisfy status theory, supporting both theories as structural constraints. (Balanced triads: 92.31% (Epinions), 93.01% (Slashdot). Status-consistent triads: 94.73% (Epinions), 93.38% (Slashdot); Sec. 3.2, p. 89)
- Pairs with negative interactions are much more likely to have a negative link than randomly matched pairs; the t-test rejects H0 at alpha=0.01. The ratio of negative-link pairs tends to increase with the number of negative interactions K. (p = 5.72e-89 (Epinions), p = 1.93e-109 (Slashdot); random-pair negative-link ratio 2.4177e-04 (Epinions), 3.9402e-04 (Slashdot); Sec. 3.3, pp. 89-90, Fig. 3)
- NeLP is best on both sites. Random and sPath are near zero; negIn is a strong heuristic; negInS and NeLP-negIn improve on it; full NeLP is top on F1 and precision. (Epinions F1/Precision: random 0.0005/0.0002; sPath 0.0040/0.0075; negIn 0.2826/0.2097; negInS 0.2893/0.2124; NeLP-negIn 0.3206/0.2812; NeLP 0.3242/0.2861. Slashdot: random 0.0008/0.0004; sPath 0.0090/0.0172; negIn 0.1986/0.1483; negInS 0.2072/0.1524; NeLP-negIn 0.2394/0.2083; NeLP 0.2441/0.2139; Sec. 5.2, p. 93, Table 3)
- Status-theory refinement (negInS over negIn) gives a small relative F1 gain on both sites. (+2.37% relative F1 (Epinions), +1.45% (Slashdot); Sec. 5.2, p. 93)
- A classifier trained on one site transfers to the other with little loss, so the features are site-general. (Figure values not extractable; text says the decrease is 'remarkably little' in both directions; Sec. 5.2, p. 93, Fig. 4)
- Ablations show each component contributes. Using cj=1 (no interaction-based reliability) is the largest single-component precision drop. Removing balance regularization (Cb=0) also hurts. (Epinions full NeLP F1 0.3242 / P 0.2861; Cn=1 gives 0.3188/0.2793; cj=1 gives precision 0.2612 (-8.70%); Cb=0 gives 0.3084/0.2686; all removed gives 0.2992/0.2342. Slashdot full 0.2441/0.2139; cj=1 precision 0.1902 (-11.08%); Cb=0 gives 0.2347/0.1972; all removed gives 0.2213/0.1722; Sec. 5.3, pp. 93-94, Tables 4-5)
- Balance regularization is sensitive to Cb. Precision rises from Cb=0 to 0.001, is flat over a middle range, and drops sharply at large Cb. (Precision +4.02% (Epinions) and +4.41% (Slashdot) from Cb 0 to 0.001; stable from 0.001 to 0.1 in Epinions; sharp drop after about 0.05 in Slashdot; Sec. 5.4, pp. 94-95, Fig. 5)
- Accuracy is rejected as a metric because of extreme class imbalance. Precision and F1 are reported on the negative class. (A trivial all-missing classifier can reach 99.99% accuracy in a sparse network (text example); Sec. 5.1, pp. 92-93)

## Limitations

- Negative labels are not observed. Training labels are constructed from negative interactions and status-theory refinement, and the paper says they are noisy, especially for negatives (Sec. 4.3, p. 91; Sec. 7, p. 95).
- Negative links in Epinions and Slashdot serve only as ground truth for analysis and evaluation; the model itself does not use them (Sec. 3, p. 88).
- Opinion labels are proxies: Epinions helpfulness ratings are thresholded at 3, and Slashdot comment polarity comes from the MPQA lexicon (Sec. 3, p. 88).
- Severe class imbalance (1:4131 and 1:2534), so precision and F1 stay modest (best F1 0.3242 Epinions, 0.2441 Slashdot) (Sec. 4.1, Table 3).
- Balance regularization uses only pairs at shortest-path length 2 plus the labeled sets, a simplification for scale (Sec. 4.3, p. 92).
- Parameterized baselines are reported at their best setting, and NeLP parameters differ per site (Sec. 5.2, p. 93). The text gives no held-out tuning protocol.
- The evaluation protocol is incomplete: the text does not describe the train/test split, folds, or how evaluation negatives were drawn.
- User-generated content (text) is not used, and only one classifier family (soft-margin SVM) is tested (Sec. 7, p. 95).
- Only two platforms with explicit signed links are studied. Collection period is not stated, and the datasets are older snapshots, so transfer to current platforms is untested.
- Recall is defined (Sec. 5.1) but not reported separately; only precision and F1 appear.

> - Summary issue: The summary treats the share of negative-link pairs as rising with K. The paper says the ratios tend to increase, which is a softer claim than the summary's rises with K.
> - Summary issue: The summary's 'cheap test' (are negative-reference dyads close together in the cosponsor graph?) is an analyst extension of Tang's positive-network distance test, not a Tang result. Label it as an untested extension.

## Gaps and flags

- No evidence-count reliability on signed reference edges. net = (pos-neg)/sub treats a single scored context the same as many unanimous ones, and signed_coalitions.py applies no n_scored floor. Only the dashboard drawing filters at n_scored >= 2.
- No ground truth for negative ties. The system cannot estimate the precision of LLM-valenced negative references as relationship indicators. Tang's negIn precision (0.15-0.21) shows that this gap matters.
- An untested analyst extension of Tang's positive-network distance test (not a Tang result): Tang's 'enemies are close' pattern (82.64% and 87.86% of negative pairs within 3 hops of the positive graph) could be measured for negative-reference dyads on the cosponsor graph. The result would show whether negative references sit inside working relationships or across camps.
- Signed triad census not reported: the share of balanced triads in member_references.json is not measured. That share would show how much of the Aref-Neal frustration is structural and how much is label noise.
- Negative signs never enter the cascade kernel. Out-party sources get a cpf_pressure of 0.5 rather than a negative edge. The paper argues that negative links add value, but this project has not tested that for Congress.
- Flag: NEAR-REJECTED PROPOSAL: Tang's count-based reliability weight f(Nij) = 1 - 1/log(1+Nij) is a form of per-edge evidence-volume weighting. Per-edge volume normalization was measured and REJECTED in this project. Do not recommend reweighting cascade or SNA edges by volume on this paper's authority. At most, apply the existing n_scored >= 2 floor as a diagnostic sensitivity run on signed_coalitions.
- Flag: Status theory (94.73% and 93.38% status-consistent triads) concerns directional asymmetry in declared links. It must not be read as evidence for reciprocity, which is NOT validated in this system (D-104..D-108).
- Flag: The edge counts quoted here (7,944 directed edges, 1,161 with net < 0, as of 2026-10-09 on disk).
- Flag: Negative references and signed-partition outputs must not be described as measured hostility, closeness or access (D-025). They are model outputs built on LLM-valenced interactions.
