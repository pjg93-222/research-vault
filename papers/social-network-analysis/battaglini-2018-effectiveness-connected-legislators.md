# Effectiveness of Connected Legislators

**Marco Battaglini, Valerio Leone Sciabolazza, Eleonora Patacchini (2018).** *NBER Working Paper 24442 (March 2018; NBER states it is not peer-reviewed).* Theme: [[themes/social-network-analysis|Social Network Analysis]].

> [!abstract] Summary
> Battaglini, Leone Sciabolazza and Patacchini (NBER WP 24442, 2018; a working paper, not peer reviewed) model House members who put effort into lawmaking and gain from the effectiveness of the colleagues they cosponsor with. In equilibrium, a member's Volden-Wiseman Legislative Effectiveness Score (LES) is proportional to a characteristic-weighted Katz-Bonacich centrality of the cosponsorship network. They estimate this by nonlinear least squares on 2,176 member-Congress observations (House, 109th to 113th Congresses). Cosponsorship ties are endogenous, so they correct with a two-step control function that uses shared-alma-mater ties as the instrument (first stage on 945,382 dyads). The spillover parameter phi is 0.44 uncorrected and 0.83 corrected (SE 0.05). The selection term is significant. Betweenness, closeness and eigenvector centrality are each significant in naive OLS, but none is significant after the correction. In a horse race, only the model-derived Katz-Bonacich term survives (phi 0.71). The committee-chair effect shrinks about 18 percent (1.97 to 1.61) once the network enters the model. Network effects are positive at every stage from BILL to LAW and grow at later stages. Cross-party and within-party ties have similar coefficients (0.87 vs 0.83), and the gap is small next to their standard errors. For Paul's systems, the paper is the empirical twin of the 2016 targeting paper behind the diagnostic Katz-Bonacich benchmark. It supports the June decision to retire the direction-blind InfluenceScore blend. It also flags that the live cosponsor graph is used without any endogeneity correction. The system already loads CEL LES data, so a direct replication of this paper's KB-to-LES link is cheap. That replication has not been run, and any such result stays UNVERIFIED until a second path reproduces it.

## Study

**Question:** Do social connections (cosponsorship ties among members of Congress) causally raise legislative effectiveness, once the endogeneity of those ties is controlled, and does the network effect predicted by the model beat traditional centrality measures?

**Theory:** Each legislator chooses effort to maximize his own effectiveness, which depends on his characteristics, his effort, and the effectiveness of socially connected colleagues weighted by tie strength (complementarity between own effort and peers' effectiveness). Simultaneous effort choice gives a linear equilibrium in which effectiveness is a weighted Katz-Bonacich centrality (Ballester, Calvo-Armengol and Zenou 2006 style) with weights set by member characteristics. The paper says the equilibrium is unique under Assumption 1 and that the model nests the standard characteristics-only regression at phi = 0.

**Data:** Legislative Effectiveness Scores (LES) for U.S. House members from Volden and Wiseman (2014), 109th to 113th Congresses (election cycles 2004 to 2012). Unit: member-congress; N = 2,176 connected congressmen (about 433 Representatives per Congress, including midterm replacements). Cosponsorship network built from THOMAS: two members linked if they cosponsored the same bill, weights = shared bills over total bills cosponsored. Instrument network: alumni ties from the Biographical Directory of Congress, two members linked if they attended the same institution within an 8-year window; about 40 percent of members linked. First-stage dyadic sample: 945,382 dyads (Table 1). Controls: seniority, margin of victory, DW-NOMINATE distance to median, size of state delegation, gender, race, party, committee chair, majority and minority leader, Speaker, Congress fixed effects.

**Method:** Structural model yields equilibrium effectiveness E = [I - phi W]^-1 (beta x + epsilon) style spatial autoregressive system (eqs 5-7). Estimated by nonlinear least squares (NLLS). Endogeneity handled by a two-step Heckman-type control function: first stage dyadic OLS of cosponsorship links on alumni links, covariates and absolute differences; selection term xi built from first-stage residuals and inserted in the outcome equation (eqs 8-10). Standard errors from residual bootstrap with 500 replications resampling structural errors by Congress. Partial F tests for phi = 0. Robustness: committee fixed effects (21 committees), state legislative experience subsample, heterogeneous spillovers (eqs 11-14), within- versus between-party networks (eqs 15-17), outcome split by legislative stage (Table 7).

**Identification:** Two-step control function (Heckman-type) for an endogenous network. Stage 1: dyadic link-formation regression of cosponsorship ties on alumni ties, dyad-level differences in characteristics (absolute values), and covariates. Stage 2: NLLS of the spatial model with xi, the function of stage-1 residuals, added to the outcome equation; joint normality (Assumption 2) implies a common selection effect across members. Exclusion restriction: alumni ties affect LES only through cosponsorship. Footnote 5 says identification could come from nonlinearity without exclusion restrictions.

**Scope:** U.S. House of Representatives only (the Senate is discussed but not analyzed), 109th to 113th Congresses (2004 to 2012 election cycles). The sample covers members with cosponsorship and alumni data, so 2,176 member-congress observations rather than all members. Results are for LES as defined by Volden and Wiseman.

## Key findings

- Theory (Sec 2.2, Proposition 1): unique Nash equilibrium in which effectiveness equals a weighted Katz-Bonacich centrality of the social network, given Assumption 1 (invertibility). With no spillovers the model reduces to the standard characteristics-only model.
- Uncorrected NLLS (Table 2, col 2): network spillover phi = 0.4448 (SE 0.0233); partial F test of phi = 0 gives 350.81, reported p below 0.001. Positive and significant, consistent with externalities.
- Corrected NLLS (Table 2, col 3, bootstrap SE): phi = 0.8324 (SE 0.0516); selection term xi = -0.4133 (SE 0.0741), significant; partial F = 59.51. Phi rises and stays significant after correction, which the authors read as network endogeneity biasing naive estimates downward for phi and biasing characteristic effects upward.
- Committee chair effect (Table 2, Sec 4, p.16-17): OLS 1.9669 (SE 0.1147) versus 1.6107 (SE 0.1545) in the corrected network model, about 18 percent smaller in magnitude. The authors say OLS partly captures network effects.
- Traditional centralities (Table 4): OLS betweenness 0.0079, closeness 0.0011 and eigenvector 0.1203 are all significant, but after the correction none is significant (betweenness -0.2599 SE 0.1662; closeness 0.0085 SE 0.0086; eigenvector -0.4122 SE 0.2500).
- Horse race (Table 4, last block): when the model term is included with the other centralities, only phi stays significant; reported Phi = 0.7095 (SE 0.0817). Text cites this as Column (8) but the table header lists seven columns, so the column mapping is uncertain.
- First stage (Table 1, 945,382 dyads): alumni-tie coefficient 3.8658 (SE 0.194) with full controls, stated as multiplied by hundreds; R-squared rises from 0.0007 (alumni only) to about 0.079 with controls, supporting alumni ties as a predictor of cosponsorship links.
- Heterogeneous spillovers (Tables 5a and 5b, as parsed): the susceptibility coefficient for No White is 0.1584 (SE 0.0955, significant at 10 percent); seniority is -0.0339 (SE 0.0123); the committee-chair cell is -0.5981 (SE 0.1588). Text: minorities gain more, senior members and chairs gain less.
- Party ties (Table 6): the between-party term is 0.8668 (SE 0.2136) and the within-party term 0.8346 (SE 0.0584) in the last column; the first column has 1.5984 (SE 0.4742) vs 1.4208 (SE 0.1000). The gap is small relative to SEs in the corrected columns, so the claim that cross-party ties matter more is only weakly supported in these numbers.
- Legislative stage (Table 7, corrected Phi): BILL only 0.5634 (SE 0.0539); BILL plus AIC 0.8387 (SE 0.0465); ABC plus PASS plus LAW 0.8643 (SE 0.0767). Network effects are positive and significant at all stages and larger later; xi is significant in each corrected column.
- Robustness (Table 8): with committee fixed effects, corrected Phi = 0.7114 (SE 0.0664) on N = 2,176; on the state-experience subsample (N = 1,775) corrected Phi = 0.8150 (SE 0.0517). Estimates are stable; table column labels conflict with the text on which columns carry committee dummies.

## Limitations

- Working paper, not peer-reviewed (NBER disclaimer, p.1).
- Identification relies on the alumni network being exogenous to politics and relevant for cosponsorship; the paper asserts this and gives no formal exclusion test. Footnote 5 notes identification may also come from nonlinearities alone.
- Selection term xi is a generated regressor; bootstrap resamples structural errors only, and the authors note no closed-form NLLS standard errors exist in this network setting.
- Alumni links cover only about 40 percent of members, so the sample is 2,176 member-congress observations, not the full chamber.
- Assumption 1 (invertibility, positivity) is assumed for the empirical range, not verified; the authors say it fails only with very large spillovers.
- Model and results are for House LES only; the Senate is not analyzed and LES is a single composite index with weights set by Volden and Wiseman.
- Text calls the correction Heckman but the procedure is a two-step control function, as the paper's own footnote 4 notes (citing Qu and Lee 2015).
- Table and text references conflict: horse-race column (8) vs seven headed columns; committee dummies placed in Table 8 columns 5-6 in the table but in columns 1-2 in the text.

## Gaps and flags

- kb_benchmark.py labels MBC.scores() as the legislative score, but moneyball_core.py:252 returns the composite four-pillar score. The 2026-08-18 rho of +0.65 (n = 533) is KB versus the composite, not versus the legislative pillar or LES.
- kb_benchmark.py uses an unweighted unit vector on the saturated build_P kernel. Neither the 2016 nor the 2018 papers use that form: both use characteristic-weighted KB on a row-share cosponsor matrix. The benchmark is a loose analogue, not a replication.
- The CEL LES data are already loaded (fec/members/lep_criterion.py) but have never been regressed on KB or network spillovers. That makes the paper's central result a cheap read-only replication target, which has not been attempted.
- No component treats cosponsorship ties as endogenous. All centralities, the cascade kernel and recruitment routes take the observed graph as exogenous, and nothing addresses selection into ties.
- The undervaluation lens still uses Centrality% from the retired InfluenceScore blend. The paper finds no predictive value for those centralities once tie selection is corrected.
- The effectiveness shadow has no peer-spillover term, although the paper finds spillovers at every one of its five stages.
- The paper's edge weight is shared bills over the member's total cosponsored bills, a per-member volume normalization. This project measured and rejected per-edge volume normalization, so do not adopt the paper's weighting into the live graph.
- Alumni (shared-background) ties appear here only as an instrument for link formation. Under D-025, any shared-background score in Paul's systems remains a heuristic, never a closeness or introduction probability.
- The paper's phi (0.83) is pooled across five Congresses and both parties. Its only stratification is the within- versus between-party tie split in Table 6. Quote phi as pooled, House only, 109th-113th.
- Working paper, not peer reviewed. The text and tables disagree on the horse-race column and on which Table 8 columns carry committee dummies.
- Any replication of KB to LES on Paul's graph is new agent analysis and stays UNVERIFIED until a second path reproduces it (DE-062).
