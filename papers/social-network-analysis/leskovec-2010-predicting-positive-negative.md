# Predicting Positive and Negative Links in Online Social Networks

**Jure Leskovec, Daniel Huttenlocher, Jon Kleinberg (2010).** *WWW 2010 (Raleigh, NC), Full Paper.* [10.1145/1772690.1772756](https://doi.org/10.1145/1772690.1772756). Theme: [[themes/social-network-analysis|Social Network Analysis]].

> [!abstract] Summary
> Leskovec, Huttenlocher and Kleinberg (WWW 2010) ask whether the sign of a hidden directed edge can be predicted from the rest of a signed network, using Epinions (119,217 nodes, 85.0 percent positive), Slashdot (82,144 nodes, 77.4 percent positive) and Wikipedia adminship votes (7,118 users, 78.7 percent positive). A logistic regression on 7 degree features plus 16 signed directed triad counts reaches about 93.4 percent accuracy on Epinions on a balanced sample of edges with at least 25 common neighbours (error 6.58 percent versus 14.7 percent for Guha et al.). Slashdot and Wikipedia accuracies appear only in plotted Figure 1, and Wikipedia is clearly lower. The model transfers across sites with little loss. In the undirected 4-type model, learned triad coefficients agree with structural balance except for the enemy-of-my-enemy triad, whose learned coefficient is negative; the authors call this consistent with Davis weak balance (no prediction). Balance alignment is notably weaker for Wikipedia. Status theory explains directed patterns, and a global status ordering satisfies 79-85 percent of edges, well above permuted and rewired baselines. Global balance does not: a two-faction partition scores near the trivial all-positive value (about 0.80) and below degree-preserving rewired networks, so the authors find no significant faction structure. Negative links modestly improve prediction of where positive links exist (for example 0.5612 to 0.5911 on Epinions, chance 0.50). For Melange the sharpest consequence is for the signed mention-layer coalition diagnostic, which compares frustration only against the one-cluster baseline on a substrate that is 83 percent positive by edge count, with no rewired or sign-permuted null and with edge direction discarded. The paper qualifies the relationships pilot's sign-agnostic treatment of shared-enemy counts (it supports not assuming a sign, not that the pattern carries none), and offers a cheap structural consistency check for the LLM-valenced reference edges.

## Study

**Question:** Can the sign (positive or negative) of a hidden directed link in an online social network be predicted from the signs of the rest of the network? Which local structural principles drive signed-link formation, and do they agree with balance and status theory? Secondary: does knowing negative links help predict positive links, and does global balance (factions) or global status (ordering) appear in these networks?

**Theory:** Structural balance (Heider; Cartwright and Harary: the enemy of my friend is my enemy, and related triad rules), Davis's weak balance (makes no prediction on the enemy-of-enemy triad), and status theory (a positive edge means the source regards the target as higher status; a negative edge means lower; signs flip when direction flips). Each theory is written as a mapping from triad types to predicted sign and compared to learned logistic regression coefficients.

**Data:** Three explicitly signed directed networks. Epinions trust/distrust: 119,217 nodes, 841,200 edges in Table 1 (text says 841,000), 85.0 percent positive, data from 1999 to 12 Aug 2003. Slashdot friend/foe: 82,144 nodes, 549,202 edges, 77.4 percent positive, crawled Feb 2009. Wikipedia admin-election votes: 7,118 users, 103,747 votes across 2,794 elections (1,235 promotions, 1,559 failures), 78.7 percent positive, from the Jan 2008 dump. Unit of analysis: a directed signed edge (u,v). Main accuracy results use edges with minimum embeddedness 25 (default Em), averaged over 10-fold cross validation, on a balanced sample in which each negative edge is paired with a random positive edge. Full unbalanced data is also reported.

**Method:** Logistic regression over 23 features: 7 degree features (signed in- and out-degrees of u and v, total out-degree of u, total in-degree of v, and embeddedness C(u,v)) plus a 16-dimensional count of directed signed triad types formed by a common neighbor w with u and v. Reduced models: 4-type undirected balance counts, 4-type status counts after canonicalizing signs and directions, and 4-type counts on the all-positive subgraph. Hand-written heuristics (balance majority, status score sigma(x) with predicted sign from sigma(u) versus sigma(v), out-degree majority, in-degree majority) as baselines. Global tests: randomized local search for a two-set partition maximizing satisfied edges (balance), and random pairwise-swap search for a node ordering maximizing satisfied edges (status, as a maximum acyclic subgraph problem), compared with permuted-sign and degree-preserving rewired baselines. Positive-edge existence task: classifier separates true positive edges from matched non-edge pairs with equal embeddedness.

**Identification:** No causal identification. Sign prediction is a leave-one-out style task: hide one edge sign and predict it from all other edges, reported with 10-fold cross validation. Cross-dataset transfer trains on one network and tests on another (Tables 6-7). Interpretation of triad coefficients as support for balance or status is associational.

**Scope:** Three online platforms: a product-review trust site (Epinions), a technology news site with friend/foe tags (Slashdot), and Wikipedia adminship votes. Snapshots from roughly 2003 to 2009; web-era pre-2010 data. Population is active users with signed links. The paper's claims of generalization are limited to these three domains.

## Key findings

- Learned logistic regression predicts edge signs with high accuracy on Epinions, beating the best error reported by Guha et al. (Error on Epinions: 6.58 percent All23, 6.64 percent 16Triads, 11.45 percent Degree, versus 14.7 percent best in Guha et al. (balanced sample, Em 25); Sec 3.1, p.644, Figure 1 text)
- On the full unbalanced data, random guessing is about 80 percent accurate and the logistic models reach 90-95 percent, roughly 15 points above chance; AUC is consistent with accuracy. (Balanced sample: AUC about 0.90. Full data: AUC about 0.95; random accuracy about 0.80 on full data, 0.50 on balanced; Sec 3.1, p.644)
- Triad features gain value as embeddedness rises; degree features are stronger for edges with few common neighbors. Wikipedia accuracy is clearly lower than the other two networks even at high embeddedness. (Qualitative from Figure 1; accuracy curves plotted for Em = 0, 10, 25 with no table values in the text; Sec 3.1, p.644, Figure 1)
- The All23 model transfers across networks with little loss: even a model trained on Wikipedia predicts Epinions and Slashdot nearly as well as one trained on the target network. (Test on Epinions: 0.9342 self-trained, 0.9249 trained on Slashdot, 0.9272 trained on Wikipedia. Test on Wikipedia: 0.8021 self-trained, 0.7722 trained on Epinions, 0.7717 trained on Slashdot; Sec 3.3, p.647, Table 6)
- In the undirected 4-type model, balance theory matches the learned coefficients for every triad type except enemy-of-enemy (mm), whose learned coefficient is negative; the authors call this consistent with Davis's weak balance (no prediction). Balance alignment is notably weaker for Wikipedia. (Undirected model coefficients listed per dataset (e.g., mm type disagrees across Epinions, Slashdot, Wikipedia); weak balance is the Davis 1963 variant; Sec 3.2, p.645-646, Table 3)
- Balance-based models transfer well across domains, while learned status models transfer poorly and often do worse than untrained status baselines. (BalanceLrn cross-dataset accuracies 0.90-0.92 among Epinions and Slashdot (e.g., 0.9027, 0.9166, 0.9020, 0.9203) versus BalanceDet 0.77-0.84; StatusLrn cross-dataset accuracies range about 0.61-0.83; Sec 3.3, p.647, Table 7)
- A triad with v linking positively to w and w linking positively to u is evidence for a negative (u,v) edge in all three datasets, a pattern status theory explains as a lower-status reversal. (Coefficient on the BB positive-path feature: -0.0692 Epinions, -0.0293 Slashdot, -0.0259 Wikipedia (all-positive subgraph model); Sec 3.2, p.646, Table 5)
- Simple heuristics beat chance but trail the learned models; out-degree of the edge generator is the strongest single cue on two of three networks, while in-degree performs poorly. (Figure 3 curves only; no table values in the text. The text says out-degree (OutSign) performs well on two of three networks; Sec 3.4, p.647-648, Figure 3)
- A global status ordering fits the networks far better than chance or baselines: about 79 to 85 percent of edges respect an ordering, versus 50 percent for random orderings. (Status satisfied fraction, Epinions/Slashdot/Wikipedia: network 0.7905/0.8221/0.8538; permuted signs 0.7241/0.7568/0.7767; rewired 0.6377/0.6644/0.6321; Sec 4, p.649, Table 8)
- No significant global balance (faction) structure: partition objective values are essentially the positive fraction, and rewired networks with the same degrees score higher. (Balance satisfied fraction, Epinions/Slashdot/Wikipedia: network 0.8344/0.8105/0.7809; permuted 0.8562/0.7779/0.7866; rewired 0.8993/0.8310/0.8316; trivial all-positive partition yields about 0.80; Sec 4, p.649, Table 8)
- Negative links help predict where positive links exist: adding signed triad features beats positive-only features on all three networks. (Positive-only accuracy 0.5612 Epinions, 0.5579 Slashdot, 0.6983 Wikipedia; positive plus negative accuracy 0.5911, 0.5953, 0.7114 (random 0.50); Sec 5, p.650, Table 9)

## Limitations

- Wikipedia sign prediction is much harder than the other two networks; the authors conjecture that public, consequential, information-based votes resist structural prediction.
- Global balance and status searches are approximate heuristics; the status problem (maximum acyclic subgraph) is hard to approximate in the worst case, and the authors say local-to-global scaling of sign patterns remains an open question.
- The naive all-positive partition already satisfies about 80 percent of edges, so balance objective values are only meaningful against rewired and permuted baselines.
- Triad features carry little information at low embeddedness, and their performance drops again at very high embeddedness, reflecting a tradeoff in sparsity.
- Balance and status each hold only approximately at the granularity where they are formulated; the 16-type model captures effects beyond either theory's resolution.
- Learned status properties are more dataset-specific than balance properties, so the status model does not generalize as well.
- Analysis is restricted to directed links; the undirected extension is mentioned but not developed. Opposite-direction edge pairs are treated as having no sign.
- Edge count for Epinions is given as 841,000 in the text and 841,200 in Table 1.

## Gaps and flags

- signed_coalitions.py reports F(k) with no weight- and degree-preserving rewired null or sign-permuted null, so the 14-member splinter and its antagonist-set label are untested against chance structure.
- Edge direction from member_references is discarded in the signed partition; no status-ordering or directed-triad test exists on the mention layer.
- LLM-valenced reference signs have no structural consistency check (leave-one-out sign prediction) and no embeddedness census.
- The component question whether negative references predict lower future cosponsorship is still untested; Sec 5 of this paper gives a matched-pair design for it.
- Flag: Wording (D-025): the code labels the k=2 splinter the antagonist set; this paper finds no significant global faction structure in three signed networks, so splinter membership and frustration are heuristic model outputs, not evidence of antagonism, closeness or persuadability, and need a null-model comparison before any such reading.
- Flag: Reciprocity (D-104..D-108): the paper treats opposite-direction edge pairs as unsigned and offers no evidence on reciprocity; do not cite it as reciprocity validation.
- Flag: No conflict with the two rejected SNA proposals: the paper does not argue for dropping regular cosponsorship edges or for per-edge volume normalization (its embeddedness thresholds are evaluation strata, not weights).
- Flag: Windows and denominators: paper figures are 1999-2009 web snapshots over edges (balanced sample, minimum embeddedness 25 unless stated); system figures are from the 2026-07-19 artifact with total |net| weight or edge count as denominator.
