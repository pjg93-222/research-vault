# Heuristic centrality methods challenge greedy optimization in influence maximization

**Laiali Almazaydeh, Arar Al Tawil, Amneh Shaban, Rabiah Al-Qudah (2026).** *Scientific Reports, 16:17085 (received 5 Jan 2026; accepted 31 Mar 2026).* [10.1038/s41598-026-47299-8](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-026-47299-8). Theme: [[themes/adaptive-influence-maximization|Adaptive Influence Maximization]].

> [!abstract] Summary
> Almazaydeh et al. compare eight influence-maximization methods under the Independent Cascade (IC) model. The graphs are Facebook ego-network subgraphs of 200 to 1,000 nodes, with uniform p = 0.1, k = 10 seeds, 10 runs and 50 Monte Carlo (MC) simulations per seed set. At 1,000 nodes, IMM is best (954.90) and RIS is close behind (952.53, five times faster). PageRank and Betweenness cannot be told apart (about 921.8, or 96.5% of IMM; the paper itself says 96.3%), and PageRank runs in 0.4 s. Degree, GCCDC and Dangling reach about 80-85% of IMM. CELF++ collapses to 12.9% of IMM (the abstract says 11.2%), with a CV of up to 71.5%. The authors blame 50 MC simulations per marginal estimate plus a Python implementation, and also name importance-based sampling bias, but they never test these explanations. The study has several internal inconsistencies: subgraph sizes, percentages, the 794x speed-up and the extrapolated runtimes do not match the paper's own tables.
> 
> This paper is already wired into Melange. `fec/engine/mc_stability_check.py` names it as its motivation. The skill library cites it to argue that the frontier's MC=20 greedy is safe because common random numbers (CRN) mean every candidate is compared under the same draws. That reasoning is sound for comparing marginals. The problem is that the recorded pass is older than a 2026-10-01 fix. The pass (MEA-7, holistic graph: 100% at MC=100, 98% across RNG seeds) was measured before QME fix 3. Before that fix, the splitmix keys did not depend on SEED, so the 'different RNG seed' runs reused the same edge and seed-activation draws. Neither leg validates the current frontier: the MC=100 leg was also measured before fixes 1, 4, 5 and 7 changed the pools, draws and ranking, and the cross-seed leg did not test independent draws. Re-measure under the current code before relying on it.
> 
> The paper's other point, that cheap centrality gets within a few percent of IMM, suggests a cheap ablation the system lacks: run a PageRank, degree or Katz-Bonacich seed baseline through the same CRN cascade. It would be an ablation only, because stance gating and seed activation are things centrality cannot see. The joint optimizer is untouched by this paper. It is deterministic (no MC), so the CELF++ failure mode cannot occur there.

## Study

**Question:** Do the theoretical guarantees of influence maximization algorithms (greedy CELF++, sampling-based RIS and IMM) hold in practice, and how do they compare with simple centrality heuristics (Degree, PageRank, Betweenness, Dangling, GCCDC) on spread quality, runtime and scaling under the Independent Cascade model on real social network subgraphs?

**Theory:** Influence maximization is NP-hard under IC and LT (Kempe et al. 2003, ref 12). The influence function is submodular, so greedy selection gives a (1-1/e) approximation (Nemhauser et al. 1978, ref 15). CELF and CELF++ use lazy evaluation to speed up greedy without changing that guarantee. RIS and IMM recast the problem as coverage of reverse-reachable sets with sampling bounds. Centrality heuristics carry no guarantee and are justified as cheap structural proxies. The paper's framing: asymptotic guarantees can diverge from practice because hidden constants, network structure and implementation matter (p.4, lines 260-269; p.13, lines 948-961).

**Data:** Source: Facebook ego network from the SNAP collection, 4,039 nodes and 88,234 undirected edges (p.6, lines 402-404). Sampled subgraphs: 200, 500, 800 and 1000 nodes per Methods and Table 2 (p.6, p.8); the abstract instead says 200, 400, 600, 800 and 1,000 nodes (p.1). Unit: one seed set per algorithm, size and run. Count: 8 algorithms x 4 sizes x 10 independent runs (seeds 42-51) = 320 seed sets, each scored by 50 Monte Carlo IC simulations (p.6, lines 411-434). No time period; a static snapshot of the 2014 SNAP dataset.

**Method:** Eight algorithms, implemented in Python 3.8 with NumPy 1.21 and NetworkX 2.6 (p.8): CELF++ (50 MC per marginal-gain estimate); RIS with theta = min(10000, 50n); IMM with epsilon = 0.2; Degree; PageRank (damping 0.85, power iteration, tolerance 1e-6, max 100 iterations); Betweenness (Brandes); Dangling centrality; GCCDC (p.4-6). Diffusion: IC with uniform p = 0.1 on all edges; seed budget k = 10. Subgraphs drawn by importance sampling: node importance is the average of normalised degree centrality and normalised PageRank; top-ranked nodes plus their highest-importance neighbours are kept to keep the subgraph connected (p.6, lines 402-410). Evaluation: mean spread over 50 MC simulations per seed set; 10 runs per configuration; mean, SD, min, max and CV reported; wall-clock seconds; spread per second as efficiency (p.7-8). Significance: paired t-tests with Bonferroni correction (alpha 0.05), with Mann-Whitney U said to give the same results (p.7-8, p.8).

**Identification:** None in a causal sense. This is a controlled simulation benchmark: algorithms are compared on identical subgraphs under the same IC settings, and seeds are paired across runs for the t-tests. No natural experiment or instrument is used.

**Scope:** One real-world network (Facebook ego network subgraphs) at 200-1000 nodes; one diffusion model (IC, uniform p = 0.1); one seed budget (k = 10); undirected graphs only. Not tested: directed, weighted or temporal networks; other diffusion models; large-scale graphs; deep RL or GNN methods. The paper says the sampled subgraphs have degree exponent above 3 (p.6, lines 408-409).

## Key findings

- CELF++ performs far below the other algorithms at 1000 nodes (about 12.9% of IMM), despite its (1-1/e) guarantee. The authors attribute this mainly to only 50 MC simulations per estimate and Python implementation, not to an algorithmic flaw. Numbers: CELF++ 122.95 +/- 24.37 vs IMM 954.90 +/- 1.27; ratio 12.9%; Delta 831.95, t = -107.78, p < 0.001. Abstract (p.1) says 11.2%, which conflicts. (Results p.8 (lines 473-486); Discussion p.13 (lines 948-955); Conclusion p.15-16)
- IMM is best at 1000 nodes and RIS is close behind. The IMM-RIS gap is small but significant. Numbers: IMM 954.90 +/- 1.27; RIS 952.53 +/- 2.72; Delta 2.36, t = 2.57, p < 0.05. RIS runs in 64.9 s vs IMM 323.5 s at 1000 nodes (p.10). (Results p.8-9 (lines 473-474, 492-493); Table 3 p.11)
- PageRank and Betweenness are statistically indistinguishable and reach about 96.5% of IMM spread at 1000 nodes, at 0.4 s and 8.8 s respectively. Numbers: PageRank 921.78 +/- 3.28; Betweenness 921.80 +/- 3.34; Delta -0.02, t = -0.01, p = 0.99; PageRank vs IMM Delta 33.12, t = 26.97, p < 0.001. The paper states 96.3% in the abstract, Discussion and Conclusion, but 921.78/954.90 = 96.5%. Stated 794x runtime speed-up: 323.5/0.4 = 809x from the paper's own times. (Results p.8-9 (lines 474-476, 495-499); Table 3 p.11; efficiency p.10 (lines 658-666); Discussion p.13 and p.15)
- Degree, GCCDC and Dangling form a lower cluster around 80-85% of IMM spread. Dangling is lowest. GCCDC does not improve on Degree at 1000 nodes. Numbers: Degree 810.87 +/- 6.77; GCCDC 811.59 +/- 5.00; Dangling 797.44 +/- 12.05 (CV up to 6.24%). Degree vs Dangling t = 2.67, p < 0.05; GCCDC vs Dangling t = 2.90, p < 0.05. (Results p.8 (lines 475-477); Algorithm-specific observations p.12 (lines 893-908); Broader Discussion p.15 (lines 1123-1129))
- Sampling methods (IMM, RIS) have very low variance and scale near-linearly. Heuristics have moderate CVs. CELF++ is unstable, with a non-monotone trajectory. Numbers: IMM CV 0.07-0.17%; RIS CV 0.13-0.23%; Betweenness CV 0.16-0.36%; PageRank 0.14-0.25%; Degree 0.66-2.59%; CELF++ CV rose to 71.5% at 500 nodes (p.10, line 646). (Scalability p.9 (lines 558-576); Algorithm-specific observations p.11-12 (lines 868-875); Fig. 4 p.10)
- Growth from 200 to 1000 nodes is close to proportional for most methods. The text and Table 4 disagree for some entries. Numbers: Text: IMM +402%, RIS +399%, PageRank +403%, Degree +411%, CELF++ +633%. Table 4: IMM +402%, RIS +400%, PageRank +404%, Degree +410%, CELF++ +740%. CELF++ at 500 nodes is 4.56 in the text and 4.91 in Table 2. (Scalability p.9 (lines 559-568); Table 4 p.11)
- Efficiency ranking: PageRank 2,304 spread/s; Degree 2,027; Dangling 1,994; GCCDC 279.86; Betweenness 104.75; RIS 14.68; IMM 2.95; CELF++ 0.18 (all at 1000 nodes). Numbers: PageRank 921.78 spread / 0.4 s; IMM 954.90 / 323.5 s; CELF++ 122.95 / 688.4 s max runtime. RIS vs PageRank: 3.3% lower spread for 162x runtime (p.10-11). (Computational efficiency p.10 (lines 658-664); Fig. 3 caption p.9)
- Extrapolated runtimes to 10,000 nodes are reported but were not measured. Numbers: RIS about 795 s; IMM about 3970 s; CELF++ about 840 s at 1000 nodes and over 8,400 s at 10,000 nodes (linear extrapolation). The limitations section also quotes PageRank at about 5 s at 10,000 nodes, a figure not given in the Discussion. Linear scaling from RIS's 64.9 s would give about 649 s, not 795 s, and the CELF++ 840 s figure for 1000 nodes conflicts with its measured maximum of 688.4 s at 1000 nodes. (Scalability considerations p.13 (lines 977-987); Limitations p.14 (lines 1045-1049))

## Limitations

- Stated by the authors (p.14, lines 1017-1098): only one real-world network (Facebook), so results may not transfer to directed, weighted or temporal networks.
- Stated: uniform IC probability p = 0.1 only, with no weighted-cascade or LT model; the authors expect ranking changes under other models.
- Stated: a single seed budget (k = 10); behaviour at k = 1-3 or k > 50 is not tested.
- Stated: scales limited to 200-1000 nodes; results at millions to billions of nodes are extrapolated and not validated.
- Stated: CELF++ used only 50 MC simulations per estimate versus 10,000+ in practice, using a Python 3.8 implementation; the authors say the underperformance is likely a budget and implementation artefact, not a flaw in the algorithm.
- Stated: importance-based sampling (top degree and PageRank nodes) may densify the subgraph around hubs and favour centrality heuristics; the authors claim robustness to the extraction method without testing it.
- Stated: no comparison with deep RL or GNN methods (PIANO and others); the authors say such a comparison is future work.
- Reader-noted, not stated by the authors: (a) abstract subgraph sizes (200, 400, 600, 800, 1000) differ from Methods and Table 2 (200, 500, 800, 1000); (b) abstract gives CELF++ as 11.2% of sampling-based spread, but the body gives 12.9%; (c) PageRank is given as 96.3% of IMM, but Table 2 gives 96.5%; (d) the 794x runtime ratio does not match 323.5/0.4 = 809x; (e) RIS uses theta = min(10000, 50n), which equals 10,000 at every tested size, so it is a fixed budget rather than theory-determined; (f) the Dangling centrality implementation uses degree as a proxy for connectivity impact (p.6, lines 389-390), so the method tested is not the published metric; (g) Mann-Whitney results are described only qualitatively, and Bonferroni-adjusted p-values are not reported; (h) p.15 line 1117 refers to heuristics 'we proposed in SATIC', an undefined term, and the Broader implications paragraph (lines 1100-1101) contains an unclear sentence about CELF guarantees for static allocations; (i) refs 9-11 (traffic anomaly detection, side-channel attacks, sample-size guidance) are cited for influence-maximization claims but are off-topic; ref 52 lacks authors and ref 54 lists 'Others'; (j) abstract IMM 955.24 and RIS 951.27 do not match Table 2 at 1000 nodes (954.90 and 952.53) and are not explained.

## Gaps and flags

- The cross-seed leg of the MC-stability check was not re-measured after QME fix 3 (2026-10-01) added SEED-dependent splitmix keys. The recorded '98% across RNG seeds' (MEA-7) and the CU-vintage '100% overlap' therefore did not test independent edge and seed-activation draws.
- No heuristic seed baseline (degree, PageRank or Katz-Bonacich on build_P) has been scored under the same CRN cascade. What greedy selection adds over cheap structural seeding is unmeasured.
- The top-45 candidate pre-cut (engagement x priority x delivery) is itself a heuristic that has never been compared with an alternative pre-cut or with no pre-cut.
- Neither the frontier nor the coalition output reports spread variance (SD or CV) across RNG seeds. The paper treats run-to-run CV as a first-class quality measure.
- The system's citations misquote the paper. mc_stability_check.py:4-5 and .claude/skills/influence-modeling-reference/SKILL.md:108 give '11.2%' (the abstract figure; the body and Table 2 give 12.9%). They call it '11.2% of true spread', but it is a share of IMM spread. The skill also says the paper 'showed' that noisy MC estimates corrupt lazy greedy. The paper only offers the MC budget and implementation as a likely cause, untested. These are skill and docstring wording fixes for Paul to approve; nothing was edited.
- STALE VALIDATION: .claude/skills/moneyball-config-and-flags/SKILL.md:102, moneyball-architecture-contract/SKILL.md:107, salience_shock.py:44 and brain STATE.md:748-749 all treat MC=20 as validated across RNG seeds. That evidence predates the SEED-keying fix in budget_frontier.py:80-82, so the cross-seed agreement was partly by construction. Re-running mc_stability_check is Paul's call under the quant-model-evaluation melange binding (line 22).
- D-025: the paper's centrality results concern simulated IC reach. They must not be used to describe layer-balanced staffer PageRank, or any graph rank, as closeness, access or introduction likelihood.
- The paper argues for neither rejected SNA proposal (dropping regular cosponsorship edges, per-edge volume normalization). There is no conflict there.
- The paper has internal numeric inconsistencies (subgraph sizes 200/400/600/800/1000 vs 200/500/800/1000; 96.3% vs 96.5%; 794x vs 809x; abstract IMM 955.24 and RIS 951.27 vs Table 2 at 954.90 and 952.53; RIS theta fixed at 10,000 at every size). Quote only the Table 2 numbers.

Paul has no annotation on this paper. The paper does not threaten optimize.py, which is deterministic. For MC-driven greedy (frontier, coalition), the paper supports it only when marginal estimates are reliable. At 533 nodes, scale is not obviously the binding constraint, so the paper alone gives no reason to move to RIS, IMM or a learned policy. On centrality, the paper shows PageRank and betweenness are equivalent as spread proxies, but only on undirected graphs with uniform p, so it says nothing about directed or weighted networks.
