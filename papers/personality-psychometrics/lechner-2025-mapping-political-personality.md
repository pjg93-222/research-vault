# Mapping the Political Personality (PoliPers): An Integrative Framework for Assessing Ideal and Actual Personality Profiles in Politicians

**Clemens M. Lechner, Julian Aichholzer, Lukas Birkenmaier, Matthias Bluemke (2025).** *Working paper, version 0.1 (no journal or DOI shown in the text; the metadata hint's "not visible" note is correct).* Theme: [[themes/personality-psychometrics|Personality & Psychometrics]].

> [!abstract] Summary
> PoliPers is a working paper (version 0.1, not peer reviewed) that builds a survey inventory of 15 politically relevant personality facets. Examples are Trust & Faith in Others, Compromise & Cooperativeness, Assertiveness & Will to Take Control, Empathy & Compassion, and Integrity & Honesty. Each facet is mapped to the Big Five, HEXACO, VIA, the Agency/Communion "Big Two" and the Aaldering-Vliegenthart leadership traits. Study 1 is a literature mapping with no sample. Studies 2-4 use three German samples: self-ratings (N=404, fieldwork Oct 2020), ideal-politician ratings (N=589, May 2020) and pooled crowd ratings of Trump (n=104) and Merkel (n=107), N=211 (Apr 2021).
> 
> Reliability is uneven. Self-rating alphas run from .44 to .87, and Loyalty, Humility and Compromise all fall at or below .51. Assertiveness splits into two factors: the "power-hungry, claims leadership" item loads reversed in ideal ratings, and observer-rating alpha is .40. Observer ratings carry a large evaluative component. Empathy correlates with likeability at r=.70/.76 and Integrity with rated honesty at .70/.80. Rated facets also correlate with the rater's ideological proximity to the target, for example r=.46 between Integrity and proximity for Trump (printed sign positive; the paper does not reconcile it with its text). The paper calls these "predictive validity", but all of them are concurrent associations measured on the same occasion.
> 
> The paper matters here because it is the upstream source of the two facet labels in Paul's agency/communion persona codebook (fec/members/agency_communion_llm.py), which feeds the receptivity layer at PERSONA_W 0.30. It does not test how personality affects response to pressure, lobbying, cosponsorship or votes. Its evidence therefore bears on how the persona construct is defined and on rater-bias risks. It gives no support to the susceptibility signs the engine assigns. Transfer is limited by the setting: German raters, two very prominent leaders, and the 2020-21 era.

## Study

**Question.** How can political personality be assessed with a comprehensive, facet-level, contextualized, psychometrically validated inventory that works for (a) voters' ideal traits for politicians, (b) citizens' perceptions of actual politicians' traits, and (c) perceived self-other similarity between voters and politicians? (p.2, p.11-12)

**Theory.** Bottom-up construction of 15 politically relevant personality facets, each located a posteriori in the nomological net of established models: NEO PI-R, BFI-2, IPIP-NEO (Big Five facets), HEXACO, Values-in-Action character strengths, the Agency/Communion "Big Two" (Abele et al., 2020), and the six-category political leadership trait scheme of Aaldering and Vliegenthart (2016) (Table 1, pp.14-16). Argues global frameworks (Big Five, Big Two) lack specificity and contextualization, while ad hoc political measures lack generalizability and create jingle/jangle problems (pp.4-6). Argues contextualized items referring to the political role outperform non-contextualized ones (Danner and Lechner, 2024; Shaffer and Postlethwaite, 2012) and that items should target observable behavioral cues because voters judge the public persona (pp.8-10). Flags ideological content in items (Hatemi and Verhulst, 2015) and social desirability bias (Backstrom et al., 2009) as threats to neutrality (pp.9-10).

**Data.** Four German-language survey studies, unit = individual respondent rating self, an ideal politician, or a named real politician. Study 1 (pp.11-14, Table 1): no data sample; literature mapping and item pool construction (110-item pool). Study 2 (self-ratings, general population, pp.22-26): N = 404 after excluding 21 straight-liners (fieldwork Oct 2020; Respondi AG online panel, CAWI; German native speakers; quotas on age, gender, education from German census; 51.0% female; age M = 42.6, SD = 14.0, range 18-68; education 34.9% low, 33.2% intermediate, 31.9% high). Study 3 (other-ratings of ideal politician, pp.27-28): N = 589 after excluding 40 straight-liners (fieldwork May 2020; same sampling as Study 2; randomized male-politician (N = 289) vs female-politician (N = 300) prompts; 51.3% female; age M = 43.4, SD = 14.6, range 18-69). Study 4 (observer ratings of real politicians, pp.29-34): N = 211 after excluding 2 straight-liners (clickworker.de crowdsourcing, fieldwork April 2021; EUR 2.25 compensation; Trump n = 104, Merkel n = 107; 38.9% female; age M = 39.9, SD = 13.3, range 18-69; education 4.7% low, 19.0% intermediate, 76.3% high; authors describe sample as male-leaning, highly educated, and somewhat left-leaning). Study 2 used 110-item pool, planned missingness (three partial blocks A/B/C plus common block X, each respondent 85 of 110 items); Study 4 used 86 personality items plus catch-all candidate measures.

**Method.** Item construction from explicit facet definitions with two disambiguating trait words per item and balanced positive/negative keying (pp.21-22). 5-point agreement scale (Study 2, 3) and 1-5 scale for facet composites (Study 4). Composite facet scores are prorated means of 4-item scales (Dedication & Sacrifice uses 2 items). Factorial validity: principal components analysis of ipsatized items (centered on each respondent's mean to remove acquiescence) with Horn (1965) parallel analysis, 1,000 random draws, run in Stata fapara (pp.24-25, 28, 30). Reliability: Cronbach's alpha and composite reliability omega from a CFA congeneric model (Raykov, 1997), Stata sem/relicoef (pp.25, 28, 30). Convergent validity: correlations of facet composites with BFI-2-XS domain scores and a three-item Machiavellianism scale (Study 2, Table 3); Study 4 correlations with catch-all candidate measures on 11-point scales (likeability feeling thermometer, vote propensity, honesty, competence, charisma, assertiveness, left-right placement and absolute ideological proximity). Group comparisons: Cohen's d for male vs female prompt (Study 3) and Trump vs Merkel means (Study 4), plus sociodemographic means (Table A3). PCA reported with orthogonal varimax rotation for the prototype and candidate composites (Figures A4, A7).

**Identification.** None stated. The paper is psychometric and descriptive: cross-sectional survey correlations, mean differences, and factor structure. It does not claim causal identification. The text calls the Study 4 correlations "predictive validity" (p.36), but the analyses are concurrent associations among same-occasion ratings (Table 5).

**Scope.** Population: German native-speaking adults in general population panels (Studies 2-3) and German native-speaking crowdsourced raters (Study 4). Institutional focus: individual politicians as perceived by voters; item wording references the political role. Targets in Study 4: Donald Trump (US former president) and Angela Merkel (German former chancellor). Era: fieldwork 2020-2021; the paper's literature runs to 2024. Language: German items with English translations in Table A2. The authors note that generalization to lesser-known politicians and to other countries is untested (p.38).

## Key findings

- The framework has 15 politically relevant personality facets (e.g., Trust & Faith in Others; Compromise & Cooperativeness; Assertiveness & Will to take control; Dedication & Sacrifice), each mapped to HEXACO, Big Five, VIA, Agency/Communion, and the Aaldering and Vliegenthart (2016) traits. Numbers: 15 facets; 4-item scales plus one 2-item scale (Dedication & Sacrifice). (Table 1, pp.14-16; Study 1 framework, pp.11-16)
- Self-rated facet reliabilities (Study 2) were moderate to high for most facets but low for several. Numbers: alpha .44-.87; omega .43-.84. Lowest: Loyalty to Principles alpha .44 / omega .46; Humility alpha .46 / omega .43; Compromise alpha .51 / omega .45. Highest: Dedication alpha .87 / omega .84 (2-item scale). (Table 2, p.25; text p.25)
- Self-rated facets converge with the expected Big Five domains in the BFI-2-XS (Study 2): trust, compromise and empathy track Agreeableness; passion tracks Extraversion; optimism and composure track Emotional Stability. Numbers: Trust-A r = .50; Empathy-A r = .48; Compromise-A r = .33; Passion-E r = .57; Optimism-ES r = .63; Self-Confidence-ES r = .56; Manners-C r = .59; Creativity-O r = .33. Machiavellianism: Integrity r = -.44, Empathy r = -.44, Humility r = -.37. (Table 3, pp.26-27; text pp.26-27)
- Intellect & Mental Acuity correlated unexpectedly with BFI-2 Emotional Stability, mostly driven by one depression item. Numbers: r = .41. (p.27 (text on Table 3))
- Essential unidimensionality for most facets in self-ratings, with Creativity & Problem-Solving the clear exception and Loyalty to Principles borderline. Numbers: Horn parallel analysis retained one component for all facets except Creativity; 15 scales judged essentially unidimensional. (Figure 1 and text, pp.24-25)
- Ideal-politician prototypes showed almost no dependence on the politician's gender. Only Assertiveness differed, and it was rated more desirable for a female politician. Numbers: |d| = .00-.19; Assertiveness d = .19, p = .025 (male N = 289, female N = 300). (Study 3 results, p.28; Table A5, p.74)
- Ideal-trait scale reliabilities were modest. Assertiveness yielded two factors because one item (power-hungry, claims leadership) loaded reversed. Numbers: Text: alpha .40-.78, omega .55-.79. Table A4 gives alpha .53-.78 with Assertiveness not computed (n.c.). (pp.28-29; Table A4, p.70)
- Observer ratings of Trump and Merkel differed significantly on 14 of 15 facets. The clearest contrasts were Self-Confidence & Composure, Trust & Faith in Others, and Humility, with Trump lower; Trump was rated higher on Assertiveness, Passion, and Creativity. Loyalty to Principles showed no difference. Numbers: Self-Confidence d = -3.31 (Trump M 1.80 vs Merkel M 4.20); Trust d = -2.15 (1.72 vs 3.23); Humility d = -3.27 (1.31 vs 3.69); Assertiveness d = 1.41 (4.12 vs 3.33); Passion d = .93 (3.55 vs 2.73); Creativity d = .45 (2.98 vs 2.63); Loyalty d = .00 (3.31 vs 3.31); Trump N = 104, Merkel N = 107. (Table 4, p.31; text p.31)
- Facets correlate with catch-all candidate measures in theoretically expected ways. Compassion tracks likeability; Integrity and Honesty tracks honesty; general assertiveness tracks the Assertiveness facet. Numbers: Empathy-likeability r = .70 (Trump) / .76 (Merkel); Integrity-honesty r = .70 / .80; Assertiveness-general assertiveness r = .42 / .53; Passion-charisma r = .58 / .61 (Trump / Merkel); Intellect-vote propensity r = .67 (Trump); Creativity-vote propensity r = .71 (Merkel). (Table 5, pp.33-34; text pp.31-32)
- Ideological proximity to the target correlates with several facet ratings The authors interpret this as bias in observer ratings; no bias direction is asserted here. Numbers: Intellect-proximity r = .48 (Trump); Integrity-proximity r = .46 (Trump) and .36 (Merkel); Creativity-proximity r = .37 (Merkel). (Table 5 column Prx, pp.33-34; text pp.32-33)
- Observer ratings of Trump and Merkel were reported to show a reliability range that does not match Table A7 for two facets. Numbers: Text: alpha .40-.93, omega .48-.94. Table A7 lists Loyalty to Principles alpha .34 (omega n.c.) and Assertiveness alpha .40 / omega .48. Loyalty may be bidimensional in observer ratings (footnote 5, p.30). (Text p.30; Table A7, p.79)
- Study 2 Discussion states the Study 1 item pool was reduced to four core items per facet (60 total). The Study 2 section describes 58 retained items (14 four-item scales plus one two-item scale). Numbers: 58 vs 60 items; 110-item initial pool. (p.22 vs p.36)

## Limitations

- Social desirability and ideological bias may affect both self-reports and observer ratings (p.37, citing Rothschild et al., 2019).
- Reliance on survey data restricts applicability; longitudinal, experimental, and qualitative designs are called for (pp.37-38).
- Study 4 raters' affect toward the targets may drive ratings; Trump and Merkel are polarizing figures, and the authors separate positivity from descriptive normativity (p.38, citing Wessels et al., 2020).
- Lesser-known politicians are untested (p.38).
- Study 2 and Study 3 use general-population samples with neutralized wording ('other people' instead of 'other politicians'; 'work' instead of 'politics') (p.22), so the instrument has not been validated on actual politicians as self-raters.
- Study 4 sample is skewed: 76.3% high education, 38.9% female, male-leaning, somewhat left-leaning (p.29).
- Dedication & Sacrifice was tested with only two positively worded items and is 'yet to become fully fledged' (p.21).
- Several 4-item scales have low reliability (alpha .44-.52 for Loyalty, Humility, Compromise, Creativity in Study 2; Table 2, p.25).
- Assertiveness and Loyalty show multidimensionality or reversed items (pp.28-30).
- Predictive validity is claimed in the Discussion (p.36) but the evidence is concurrent correlations only.
- Inconsistent study labels: Table 2 and Table 3 are labeled Study 1 while the data are from Study 2; Table 4 and Table 5 are labeled Study 3 while the data are from Study 4; the Study 3 section heading is used for the ideal-trait study whereas the discussion labels it Study 3 too. Readers must map labels by content.
- Acquiescence control relies on ipsatization and balanced keying; the authors do not report a model-based acquiescence correction.

## Short quotes

- "Contextualized inventories, in which item content specifically references the relevant domain under study, have been shown to outperform non-contextualized ones" (p.5)
- "cannot fully capture the specific traits most relevant to political behaviors and voter perceptions" (p.4)
- "the prototype ratings showed hardly any variation in terms of the gender of the ideal politician" (p.28)

## Gaps and flags

- The persona channel collapses PoliPers' 15 facets into 2 cue shares. Facets with the most face relevance to lobbying receptivity have no measure: Compromise & Cooperativeness, Trust & Faith in Others, and Integrity & Honesty.
- Opposite poles (indifference, distrust, rigidity) are coded as 'none' rather than as negative evidence, so low communion and missing communion cues are indistinguishable.
- There is no check of whether the gold validation panels' ratings track rater ideological proximity, which the paper reports as associated with observer trait ratings.
- The assumption that the projected persona is what a lobbyist meets is untested. PoliPers measures persona as perceived by voters, and nothing in the system measures how lobbyists or staff perceive members.
- The agency construct is not checked for the leadership-claiming versus power-seeking split that PoliPers found in Assertiveness.
- Flag: Working paper version 0.1, not peer reviewed, with inconsistent study labels and item counts (58 vs 60). Cite with that status.
- Flag: The paper's 'predictive validity' wording (p.36) describes same-occasion concurrent correlations. Do not quote it as predictive evidence for any member-level forecast.
- Flag: Nothing in the paper validates the receptivity persona signs (communion means susceptible, agency means resistant). The persona term stays a design prior. PERSONA_W 0.30 is kept by owner ruling, and this paper is not grounds to propose removing it.
- Flag: No conflict with the SNA rejections (D-021, D-025, D-104..D-108). The paper makes no network, lobbying, or reciprocity claims.
- Flag: The assertiveness-split concern applied to text cues is an analyst plausibility warning, not a measured defect.

Paul's note treats Birkenmaier & Lechner as the most direct transfer and names two weaknesses: converting traits into node parameters, and ambiguous statements. PoliPers is the facet framework underneath that codebook, and it sharpens his first worry. It defines facets and shows that their ratings are reliable only in part, but it says nothing about how a facet changes a politician's response to pressure. The trait-to-node conversion (communion susceptible, agency resistant) therefore remains unsupported by this whole literature line. His receptivity note that ten personalities collapse into four observable ones understates the reduction: PoliPers offers 15 facets, and the system uses 2. His notes are not stale, but they do not record that the codebook inherits PoliPers' unstable Assertiveness facet, or that observer ratings of persona track the rater's ideological proximity.
