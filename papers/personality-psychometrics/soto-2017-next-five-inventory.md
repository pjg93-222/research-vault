# The Next Big Five Inventory (BFI-2): Developing and Assessing a Hierarchical Model with 15 Facets to Enhance Bandwidth, Fidelity, and Predictive Power

**Christopher J. Soto, Oliver P. John (2017).** *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology, 113, 117-143.* Theme: [[themes/personality-psychometrics|Personality & Psychometrics]].

> [!abstract] Summary
> Soto and John build the BFI-2, a 60-item self-report Big Five inventory. It has 15 facets, three per domain, and each facet has four items with equal numbers of true-keyed and false-keyed items. Validation in independent internet (N = 1,000) and student (N = 470) samples gives domain alphas of at least .83 and facet alphas of .66 to .85. Self-peer agreement averages .56 at the domain level and .49 at the facet level (peer n = 184). In the student sample (N = 438), convergence with other Big Five instruments runs from .72 (NEO PI-R) to .92 (BFI). Raw-item PCA finds a sixth component, acquiescence. Within-person centering removes it, and the content-balanced facet scales effectively control it in the facet PCA, though acquiescence still biases same-domain facet associations. Across 20 self- and peer-reported criteria, BFI-2 facets explain more variance than domains (mean R2 .33 vs .27). The authors stress that facet quality matters more than the number of facets.
> 
> Nothing in the paper concerns legislators, lobbying or text. It bears on Paul's systems only through measurement mechanics. (1) The LLM item reader (big5_llm.py) says it uses "balanced keying", but the code keys O, C, E and A at 3 true to 1 false. Only ES is balanced. By the paper's acquiescence logic, a rater that tends to mark items +1 would push O, C, E and A up together and inflate their intercorrelation. The system already worries about this kind of halo. (2) The dormant expert-panel instrument is BFI-2-XS, a short form derived from the BFI-2. That panel was CANCELED (see flags). (3) The affective-N LLM read, with LIWC used only as an anchor, sums pessimism, anxiety, depression and volatility, which roughly match the BFI-2 Negative Emotionality facets. In the paper those facets predict different criteria. (4) The self-peer agreement figures give a realistic ceiling for reading convergence between text-based traits and gold ratings. All transfers are indirect: the paper studies general-population self-report, while the system reads politicians' text with LLM and lexical methods.

## Study

**Question:** Can a brief Big Five inventory be built with a robust hierarchical structure (three facets per domain), balanced true-keyed and false-keyed content to control acquiescence, and greater bandwidth, fidelity and predictive power than the original 44-item Big Five Inventory (BFI), while keeping the BFI's focus, clarity and brevity?

**Theory:** Big Five trait structure (Extraversion, Agreeableness, Conscientiousness, Negative Emotionality, Open-Mindedness) with a hierarchical facet level. Bandwidth-fidelity tradeoff (Cronbach and Gleser, 1957; John, Hampson, and Goldberg, 1991): broad traits predict broadly, narrow facets predict specifically. Facets are chosen as factor-pure (anchor the domain) plus complementary (broaden it). Acquiescent responding is a response style that biases reliability, structure and validity unless items are content-balanced (Jackson and Messick, 1958).

**Data:** Study 1 (item development): N = 1,137 members of the Eugene-Springfield Community Sample (Oregon adults), age 18-89 (M = 49.64, SD = 13.02), 52.9% female; 641 completed the BFI; 1,131 completed at least one trait-adjective list (885 adjectives); 995 completed at least one IPIP list (2,552 IPIP items); 401 completed all 12 IPIP lists. Study 2 (scale construction): N = 1,000 adults from personalitylab.org (500 men, 500 women), age 18-77 (M = 29.25, SD = 12.17), 79% US residents; all rated a 110-item pool. Study 3 (validation): internet sample N = 1,000 personalitylab.org visitors (500 men, 500 women), age 18-74 (M = 28.73); student sample N = 470 psychology students at a large public university (146 men, 313 women, 11 unreported), age 16-49 (M = 21.68). Subsamples: 8-week retest n = 110; peer reports n = 184 (60% friends, 30% romantic partners); behavioral self-reports (Schwartz values, about two weeks later) n = 439; psychological well-being n = 179; other Big Five measures (Mini-Markers, BFAS, NEO PI-R, NEO-FFI) n = 438. Unit: individual respondent. Study period: not stated in the text. Note: the student sample is 470 in the text but 459 in the gender breakdown of Table 5.

**Method:** Rational-empirical scale development in three studies. Study 1: three facets per domain chosen as one factor-pure facet plus two complementary facets (pp. 15-18); marker adjectives correlated against 885 adjectives, 2,552 IPIP items and 44 BFI items to find item content (pp. 18-19); preliminary pool of 110 items (44 original, 19 revised, 47 new) with at least three true-keyed and three false-keyed items per facet (p. 19). Study 2: rational review plus convergent and discriminant correlations with preliminary scales, and balanced keying, to select 60 items (four per facet, 15 facets, 5 domains of 12 items) (pp. 21-22); PCA with varimax rotation on the development sample, which the authors flag as possibly favorably biased (p. 22). Study 3 (independent samples): alpha and retest reliability; self-peer correlations; PCAs of raw and within-person-centered items; five-component varimax solutions for domains and facets with Tucker-type congruence; CFA of five model sets per domain (single domain, single domain plus acquiescence, positive/negative items, three facets, three facets plus acquiescence; acquiescence factor loadings fixed at 1 and uncorrelated with substantive factors); monotrait-heteromethod and heterotrait correlations with other Big Five measures; IRT test information comparisons with the BFI; and regressions predicting 20 self- and peer-reported criteria from BFI domains, post hoc BFI facets, BFI-2 domains and BFI-2 facets, with facets chosen by forward regression (inclusion p < .01, maximum three predictors) (pp. 22-38).

**Identification:** None in the causal sense. This is an observational psychometric validation. Acquiescence is handled three ways: content-balanced keying (equal true- and false-keyed items per scale), within-person centering of items (p. 10, p. 30), and modeling acquiescence as a latent method factor in CFA (p. 32-33). Validity evidence is correlational (convergent, discriminant, self-peer, and criterion correlations and regressions). No causal or quasi-experimental design is used.

**Scope:** Population: adults in economically developed English-speaking nations, mainly young or middle-aged and mostly with at least high-school education (Study 1 community sample from Oregon; Studies 2-3 volunteers from personalitylab.org, an online feedback site; Study 3 student sample from one public university). Not representative; the authors say population norms are still needed (p. 46). Era: not stated in the text. Institution: not applicable beyond the named samples. Measure scope: Big Five domains plus 15 facets, 60 items, 10 minutes or less.

## Key findings

- Hierarchical model: 15 facets (three per Big Five domain) selected as one factor-pure plus two complementary facets per domain. Extraversion: Sociability, Assertiveness, Energy Level. Agreeableness: Compassion, Respectfulness, Trust. Conscientiousness: Organization, Productiveness, Responsibility. Negative Emotionality: Anxiety, Depression, Emotional Volatility. Open-Mindedness: Intellectual Curiosity, Creative Imagination, Aesthetic Sensitivity. (Study 1, Phase 1, pp. 15-18; Table 1, p. 61; Conclusion of Study 1, p. 20) — 15 facets; 3 per domain; 110-item preliminary pool (44 original, 19 revised, 47 new)
- Final BFI-2: 60 items, four per facet, 12 per domain. Reliability in the scale-development sample (Study 2): domain alphas .87 E, .83 A, .88 C, .91 N, .84 O; facet alphas .65-.84, mean .76. First PCA: all 15 facets loaded most strongly on their intended component (primary loadings at least .73, M = .80); all 60 items loaded most strongly on the intended component (at least .41, M = .60). (Study 2 Results, pp. 22-23) — 60 items; domain alpha .83-.91; facet alpha .65-.84 (M = .76); facet primary loadings >= .73 (M = .80); item primary loadings >= .41 (M = .60)
- Study 3 reliability: all five domain alphas were at least .83 internet and .85 student; eight-week retest of domains was at least .76 (student). Facet alphas ranged .66-.85 in each sample (mean .76 internet, .77 student); facet retest .66-.83 (mean .73). (Study 3, Reliability section, p. 28; Tables 2-3, pp. 62-63) — domain alpha >= .83 (internet), >= .85 (student); domain retest >= .76; facet alpha .66-.85; facet retest mean .73
- Discrimination: absolute correlations among domains averaged .20 (internet) and .24 (student). Within-domain facet correlations averaged .53 (internet) and .55 (student), versus absolute between-domain facet correlations of .16 and .19. (Study 3, pp. 28-29; Table 2, p. 62; Table 3, p. 63) — domain intercorrelation mean |r| = .20/.24; within-domain facet r = .53/.55; between-domain facet |r| = .16/.19
- Self-peer agreement (student sample, peer n = 184): domain agreement .42-.69 (mean .56) versus absolute discriminant mean .11. Facet agreement .27-.73 (mean .49), within-domain discriminant mean .32, between-domain discriminant mean .10. Thirteen of 15 self-reported facets and 14 of 15 peer-reported facets had their strongest correlation with the corresponding facet on the other rater's side. (Study 3, p. 29; Table 4, p. 64) — domain agreement mean r = .56; facet agreement mean r = .49; 13/15 and 14/15 strongest-match counts
- Acquiescence: PCA of 60 raw items gave six components above the scree line (Big Five plus an acquiescence component) in both samples. Within-person centering removed the extra component. Five varimax components of centered items reproduced the Big Five, with primary loadings averaging .62 (internet) and .61 (student); congruence with raw-item components was .97 or higher. (Study 3, Domain-level structure, pp. 30-31; Figure 1, p. 73; Table 6, pp. 66-67) — raw-item PCA: 6 components vs 5 after centering; primary loadings M = .61 (internet raw), .60 (student raw), .62 (internet centered), .61 (student centered); congruence >= .97
- Facet-level acquiescence: the PCA of 15 facet scales showed only five components above the scree line (first five eigenvalues 4.05, 2.16, 1.90, 1.60, 1.32 in the internet sample, then 0.56 or lower), so the content-balanced facets did not produce an acquiescence component. Facet primary loadings were at least .73 (M = .81, internet) and at least .67 (M = .79, student), with congruence .98 or higher. (Study 3, p. 31; Table 7, p. 68) — 5 components above scree; eigenvalues 4.05, 2.16, 1.90, 1.60, 1.32; facet primary loadings >= .73 (M = .81) and >= .67 (M = .79)
- Confirmatory factor analysis within domains: a single-domain model fit poorly (CFI <= .805 internet, <= .800 student). Adding acquiescence or separating positive and negative items gave a large fit gain for Agreeableness but still poor fit (CFI <= .876 / .850). The three-facet model fit acceptably or nearly acceptably for E, C, N, O (CFI >= .902 / .895), and the three-facets-plus-acquiescence model fit acceptably for all domains (CFI >= .930 / .932). (Study 3, pp. 31-34; Table 8, p. 69) — single-domain CFI <= .805 / .800; three facets CFI >= .902 (internet) / .895 (student) for E, C, N, O; three facets plus acquiescence CFI >= .930 / .932; RMSEA about .054-.075 for the best model per domain (Agreeableness .054 / .058)
- Convergence with other Big Five measures (student sample): monotrait-heteromethod correlations averaged .92 with the original BFI, .82 with the BFAS, .80 with Mini-Markers, .75 with NEO-FFI and .72 with NEO PI-R; absolute heterotrait-heteromethod correlations averaged .21. Gains over the original BFI were largest for Agreeableness (.75 vs .70) and Open-Mindedness (.76 vs .72). (Study 3, Convergence, pp. 34-35; Table 9, p. 70) — convergent r: BFI .92, BFAS .82, Mini-Markers .80, NEO-FFI .75, NEO PI-R .72; heterotrait mean |r| = .21; A .75 vs .70; O .76 vs .72
- Facet convergence: 53 BFI-2 facet correlations with same-domain BFAS aspects and NEO PI-R facets were at least .50, and each facet had a distinctive profile (for example, Assertiveness with BFAS Assertiveness r = .85; Organization with Order r = .74; Depression with Withdrawal r = .77). (Study 3, Facet-level associations, pp. 35-36; Figure 3, pp. 74-76 (N = 438)) — 53 correlations >= .50; r = .85, .74, .77 as listed
- Information: IRT test information was similar in shape for BFI and BFI-2 domains, but the BFI-2 gave about 55% more information on Negative Emotionality, 15% on Extraversion, 29% on Agreeableness, 12% on Open-Mindedness and 11% on Conscientiousness. (Study 3, footnote 4, pp. 34-35) — +55% (N), +15% (E), +29% (A), +12% (O), +11% (C) total information
- Predictive power: across 20 self- and peer-reported criteria, mean R-squared was .25 for BFI domains, .27 for post hoc BFI facets, .27 for BFI-2 domains and .33 for BFI-2 facets. Entering BFI-2 facets after BFI domains added significant variance for 14 of 20 criteria. (Nomological network section, pp. 37-39; Table 11, p. 72; Figure 4, p. 77; footnote 7, pp. 38-39) — mean R2 = .25 (BFI domains), .27 (post hoc BFI facets), .27 (BFI-2 domains), .33 (BFI-2 facets); 14/20 criteria with significant incremental R2 for BFI-2 facets
- Domain-level criterion patterns were nearly identical between BFI and BFI-2: column-vector correlations of zero-order correlations across 20 criteria averaged .98, as did standardized coefficients. Associations were slightly stronger for BFI-2 domains, giving higher explained variance. (Footnote 6, pp. 36-37) — column-vector r = .98 (zero-order and standardized coefficients)
- Facet-level predictors were distinct and conceptually meaningful. Examples (Table 10, r/beta): Benevolence predicted by Compassion (.47/.29) and Trust (.45/.28); Self-direction by Intellectual Curiosity (.44/.39); Stress resistance by Emotional Volatility (-.47/-.32) and Anxiety (-.46/-.28); Positive relations by Depression (-.53/-.38). (Table 10, p. 71; text pp. 36-38) — see Table 10 r/beta values listed
- Acquiescence index: averaging all 60 raw items without reversing false-keyed items gives a yea-saying or nay-saying index (range 1.00 to 5.00). The authors propose the BFI-2 for studying acquiescence itself. (General Discussion, p. 45) — range 1.00-5.00 across 60 items

## Limitations

- Samples are mostly young or middle-aged adults with at least high-school education; population norms are not yet available (p. 46). Reliability and validity among older adults and less-educated respondents are untested (pp. 46-47). The BFI-2's revisions for easier wording have not been directly tested for improved measurement among younger or less-educated respondents (p. 46).
- All participants lived in economically developed English-speaking nations, so cross-cultural structure and correlates are untested (pp. 46-47).
- Brevity forces exclusions. Domain-level: traits beyond the Big Five and constructs such as characteristic adaptations and narrative identity are excluded (p. 47). Facet-level: behavioral Open-Mindedness facets and peripheral facets such as excitement-seeking, modesty, deliberation and impulsivity are excluded (pp. 47-48). Item-level: anger/irritability content is measured only indirectly through Emotional Volatility (p. 48).
- Study 2 facet and domain reliability and PCA results come from the same sample used to refine the item pool, so they may be favorably biased by capitalization on chance; the authors therefore emphasize Study 3 (p. 22).
- Study 3 internet and student samples are self-selected (online feedback-seekers and course participants). The student sample is strongly gendered (313 women, 146 men) and the internet sample reports higher Open-Mindedness and Negative Emotionality than students (p. 29-30).
- The 4-item facet scales have modest reliability (facet alphas .66-.85), and the authors note some Schwartz-value criteria have low alphas (.27-.70), which they retained as an a priori criterion set (p. 26-27).
- Self-reported behavioral and psychological criteria come from single-method self-report, and peer reports are from a subsample of acquaintances (n = 184).
- The authors recommend simple item aggregation for new studies. Regression-derived equations for switching from BFI to BFI-2 are available from the first author (footnote 3, p. 34), not reported in the text.

## Gaps and flags

- No acquiescence or response-style diagnostic exists for the LLM item reader. The paper's index (mean of raw, unreversed items) and within-person centering are not computed, even though O, C, E and A are keyed 3:1.
- The big5_llm.py docstring says 'balanced keying', which contradicts the item table (only ES is 2:2). This is a documentation-integrity gap.
- Traits are scored only at the domain level, apart from ad hoc facets (A-antagonism; E communication facets taken from NEO, not BFI-2). There is no hierarchical facet model, so the facet-specific prediction the paper shows (R2 .27 to .33) cannot be tested.
- Affective N merges anxiety, depression, volatility and pessimism into one sum. The paper shows these facets predict different criteria, and the receptivity mechanism (pressure-choking) is closest to Anxiety and Volatility.
- The expert observer panel (b5_panel_items.py / b5_panel_score.py, BFI-2-XS) was CANCELED by Paul on 2026-07-09 with the instruction never to re-propose it. Nothing here recommends recruiting raters or reviving the panel; the bearing is lineage only.
- The keying discrepancy in big5_llm.py and the acquiescence-leak mechanism are new agent analysis. No item change is proposed, because any change to item text or keying creates a new ITEMSET_V and cache.
- No graph-rank, revolving-door, LDA-edge or reciprocity wording is involved. The paper argues for neither rejected SNA proposal. Its 'nomological network' is a construct-validity term, not a social network.
- Citation check: the system cites Soto & John 2017 JRP 68:69-81 (the BFI-2-XS/S short forms), a separate paper from this JPSP BFI-2 paper. The panel citation is correct and does not need relabelling.

Paul has not annotated this paper. The BFI-2 evidence shows that finer-grained facets add predictive power, but only when each facet is measured reliably.
