# Adversarial Attack on Graph Neural Networks as an Influence Maximization Problem

**Jiaqi Ma, Junwei Deng, Qiaozhu Mei (2022).** *WSDM 2022 (Fifteenth ACM International Conference on Web Search and Data Mining), Tempe AZ, virtual, Research Paper track, pp. 675-685*. [https://doi.org/10.1145/3488560.3498497](https://doi.org/10.1145/3488560.3498497). Theme: [[themes/adaptive-influence-maximization|Adaptive Influence Maximization]].

> [!abstract] Summary
> Ma, Deng and Mei (WSDM 2022) study a strict black-box attack on graph neural networks. The attacker cannot see model parameters or predictions. It may change the features of at most r nodes (1 percent of the graph), each with degree at most m. The paper's main contribution is choosing which nodes to perturb. Under a ReLU path-independence approximation, the expected misclassification rate becomes an influence-maximization objective on a directed bipartite linear-threshold graph. Influence weights are B = M^L (an L-step random walk, L = 4), and the thresholds are random. The problem is NP-hard (Lemma 1). It is submodular in expectation, so greedy selection carries a guarantee on the expected objective, when each threshold CDF is concave on the positive domain (Proposition 2, a sufficient condition). Uniform thresholds give a saturated-coverage objective (InfMax-Unif) and normal thresholds give an erf objective (InfMax-Norm). On Cora, Citeseer and Pubmed, both methods usually cut test accuracy more than the GC-RWCS, PageRank, degree and betweenness baselines; PageRank and RWCS are the weakest baselines (degree and betweenness beat them in all 18 setups). One example is Cora GAT: 87.7 with no attack, 57.5 under GC-RWCS and 55.9 under InfMax-Unif. Near-ties occur on Citeseer. A qualitative SBM case study suggests the better selectors spread their seeds across communities.
> 
> The paper does not concern legislatures, lobbying or persuasion, so nothing about political behavior transfers. What does transfer is the mathematics. Proposition 2 gives a sufficient condition for a greedy guarantee on the expected objective of a threshold model: concave threshold CDFs. Paul's system uses deterministic tier thresholds in lt_priming, and the coalition allocator weights seeds by singleton marginals with water-filling. The paper's condition does not cover step-like deterministic thresholds. Its other lessons are that coverage-style saturating objectives beat raw centrality, and that seed sets should avoid redundancy. The adversarial result itself has no counterpart in the system, which runs no GNN. It does give an analogy for testing how neighbour-propagated stance imputation behaves when a few node inputs are corrupted. That analogy is weak.

## Study

**Question.** Can a restricted, realistic black-box attack on a GNN, which perturbs the features of a small set of nodes with no access to model parameters or predictions, be formalized and solved efficiently by connecting node selection to influence maximization on a threshold model? (Abstract; Sections 1 and 3.2, pp. 675-677.)

**Theory.** Adversarial attack on GNNs recast as influence maximization. Under a ReLU path-independence assumption (Assumption 1, after Xu et al. 2018), the expected mis-classification rate becomes a spread function on a directed bipartite graph with node-specific thresholds (general or linear threshold model, Kempe et al. 2003). The objective is submodular when threshold CDFs are concave on the positive domain (Proposition 2, p. 678), so greedy selection applies. Section 4.2, p. 677-678.

**Data.** Three public citation benchmarks: Cora, Citeseer, Pubmed (Sen et al. 2008; Yang et al. 2016). Unit: nodes. Graph sizes N are not stated in the text. Random 60/20/20 train/validation/test splits (Table 1 and 2); 10 percent training data in Table 3 and Appendix B.2. 40 independent trials per model-dataset pair. Victim models: GCN and GAT with 2 layers, JK-Net with 7 layers. Proxy models: 20 GCNs trained on separate splits to build the perturbation vector. Period: not stated; benchmark data only, no time period.

**Method.** Two-step attack: (1) select a node set S with |S| at most r and degree at most m; (2) add a constant perturbation vector epsilon to features of nodes in S. The paper focuses on step 1. InfMax-Unif greedily maximizes the saturated coverage objective h(S) = sum over j of (min(sum over i in S of B_ji, a) + b) divided by (a+b), with B = M^L (random-walk transition matrix, L = 4), uniform threshold distribution, a = 0.01 (Corollary 1, Eq. 6). InfMax-Norm greedily maximizes sum over j of (1 + erf(sum B_ji / (sigma sqrt 2)))/2 with sigma = 0.01 (Corollary 2, Eq. 7). Baselines: Degree, Betweenness, PageRank (alpha = 0.85), RWCS and GC-RWCS (Ma et al. 2020; L = 4, l = 30, k = 1). Constraints: r = 1 percent of graph size; m = lowest degree of the top 10 or 30 percent of nodes. Perturbation: lambda = 10, top 2 percent features by average gradient sign-consistent across 20 proxy GCNs, value in {-lambda, 0, lambda}. Significance: pairwise t-test at alpha 0.05. Section 3.2 and 5.1-5.2, pp. 676-681.

**Identification.** Not applicable in the causal sense. This is an adversarial-attack benchmark, not causal inference. Validity rests on the black-box design: proxy GCNs trained on different splits build the perturbation vector, so it does not depend on victim models. The authors note the Ma et al. 2020 setup used victim-model information (Appendix B.1).

**Scope.** Population: node-level, untargeted, evasion, black-box attacks by feature perturbation on GNNs (GCN, GAT, JK-Net) for node classification on three citation networks. Institution: University of Michigan School of Information. Era: 2022 publication on 2008-2016 public benchmarks. Excludes graph-topology perturbation and binary or nonnegative features (future work, p. 685).

## Key findings

- The mis-classification rate objective, with model parameters replaced by their expectation, equals an influence-maximization objective on a directed bipartite graph with node thresholds theta_j (Proposition 1, Eq. 3-4). Numbers: Influence weights B = M^L with L-step random walk; threshold theta_j = (ybar_j(empty) minus max-class logit margin) over (W_khat minus W_y)^T epsilon. (Section 4.2, Proposition 1, p. 677; proof Appendix A.1)
- Influence maximization on this bipartite linear threshold graph is NP-hard (reduction from Set Cover). (Lemma 1, Section 4.2, p. 678)
- If each threshold CDF F_j is concave on [0, inf), the expected mis-classification rate h(S) is submodular in S, so greedy selection has a guarantee. (Proposition 2, Section 4.3, p. 678; proof Appendix A.2)
- Uniform thresholds give saturated coverage objective (InfMax-Unif); normal thresholds give an erf objective (InfMax-Norm). Both are submodular. Numbers: a = 0.01 and sigma = 0.01 fixed in all experiments; authors say results are fairly robust to these choices (p. 679). (Corollaries 1 and 2, Eq. 6-7, pp. 678-679)
- On the 60 percent training setting, InfMax-Unif gives lower test accuracy than the strongest baseline GC-RWCS in most setups, with significance marked in Table 1. Example: Cora GAT, 10 percent threshold, 55.9 vs 57.5 (no attack 87.7). Pubmed GAT, 30 percent threshold, 64.1 vs 69.1 (no attack 85.2). Numbers: the best of the two InfMax methods strictly beats the best baseline in 17 of 18 setups and both methods do so in 14 of 18; Table 1 shows near-ties on Citeseer GCN and GAT at 10 percent (InfMax-Unif 60.7 and 62.4 vs GC-RWCS 60.5 and 62.4). (Section 5.2, Table 1, pp. 679-681)
- The 10 percent threshold setting shows Cora GCN 68.8 (InfMax-Unif) vs 70.0 (GC-RWCS), and Pubmed JK-Net 55.6 vs 56.7. Among baselines, Degree and Betweenness give lower post-attack accuracy than RWCS and PageRank in all 18 setups; PageRank is the weakest in 14 of 18 and RWCS in 4. Numbers: See Table 1 rows; Random baseline Cora JK-Net 70.5 (10 percent threshold). (Table 1, Section 5.2, pp. 680-681)
- Empirical threshold distributions, estimated by 1000 GCN initializations on Cora, are non-increasing on the positive domain in most cases, supporting the submodularity assumption. Numbers: 1000 model initializations; 3 nodes shown; rho set to 1 for visualization (footnote 5). (Section 5.3, Figure 2, p. 681)
- On a stochastic blockmodel with 4 communities, the better methods (GC-RWCS and the proposed methods) spread selected nodes more evenly across communities. Numbers: Qualitative; no statistic reported. (Section 5.4, Figure 3, p. 681)
- With 10 percent training data and in the Ma et al. (2020) setup, the same trends hold. Numbers: Table 3 Cora GCN no attack 81.1; InfMax-Unif 10 percent threshold 72.6 (JK-Net 58.1); the authors call the trend similar. (Appendix B.1-B.2, Tables 2-3, p. 685)

## Limitations

- Assumption 1 (ReLU activations independent with a common probability) is an approximation taken from Xu et al. 2018; it is a simplification of the real network.
- The thresholds are treated as random. The authors say independence across nodes is unrealistic for GNNs because neighbours' predictions correlate, and they leave concentration of the mis-classification rate around its expectation to future work (Section 4.4, p. 679).
- The distribution assumptions (uniform or normal thresholds) likely deviate from the real distribution; the authors state this (p. 679).
- Hyperparameters a and sigma are fixed at 0.01 without tuning; the theoretically optimal choice depends on epsilon and the dataset (p. 679).
- Only node selection is studied. The perturbation vector is constant across nodes (no personalization), and the attack is untargeted.
- Only feature perturbation with continuous features is treated; graph-structure perturbation and binary or nonnegative features are future work (Conclusion, p. 685).
- The bipartite connection uses rho (the path activation probability), which is unknown; visualization sets rho = 1 (footnote 5, p. 681).
- Evaluation covers three citation benchmarks and three GNN architectures with a single attack budget r = 1 percent of graph size. Graph sizes are not reported in the text.
- Greedy submodularity gives an approximation guarantee for the expected objective, not for the actual mis-classification rate. The paper does not prove the concentration of the actual rate.

## Gaps and flags

- No greedy step states whether its objective is submodular. per_topic_coalition ranks by singleton marginals with water-filling and has no greedy loop; budget_frontier has no lazy, CELF or greedy term. The paper gives a sufficient condition for a guarantee on the expected objective (concave threshold CDFs), so a (1-1/e) bound should not be assumed from greedy alone.
- lt_priming's thresholds are deterministic tier constants. A seed-set version of LT priming would not be covered by Proposition 2, and NP-hardness applies to the deterministic problem (Lemma 1).
- The coalition allocator funds by singleton marginals and checks redundancy only afterwards (joint vs naive-additive). There is no overlap-aware selection like that of the coverage-objective methods, which beat stand-alone-importance baselines.
- There is no robustness test of graph-propagated estimates (neighbour imputation, cascade spreads) against a few corrupted or mislabelled high-coverage nodes.
- Coverage in budgetRecruit is one hop. Multi-hop, saturating reach has not been tried.
- The paper's 'influence' means spread of misclassification in a GNN. It is not legislator influence, and its results must not be quoted as evidence about persuasion, lobbying or access.
- Random-walk importance B = M^L and walk-sum centralities are heuristic model outputs, never closeness, access or introduction probabilities (D-025).
- Porting M = D^-1 A to the cosponsor graph normalizes by node degree. Check that it does not bring back the REJECTED per-edge volume-normalization proposal under another name. The paper itself argues for neither rejected SNA proposal.
- Table 1 figures come from 3 citation benchmarks, 40 trials and a 1% node budget, with no graph sizes reported. Any number quoted must carry that window and denominator.
- Citeseer at the 10% threshold shows near-ties between InfMax-Unif and GC-RWCS.
