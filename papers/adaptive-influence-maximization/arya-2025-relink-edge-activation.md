# RELINK: Edge Activation for Closed Network Influence Maximization via Deep Reinforcement Learning

**Shivvrat Arya, Smita Ghosh, Bryan Maruyama, Venkatesh Srinivasan (2025).** *ACM CIKM 2025 (CIKM '25), Seoul, Korea, November 10-14, 2025*. Theme: [[themes/adaptive-influence-maximization|Adaptive Influence Maximization]].

> [!abstract] Summary
> RELINK (Arya, Ghosh, Maruyama and Srinivasan, CIKM 2025) treats influence maximization in "closed" networks as an edge-selection problem. The seed set is fixed, and each node may switch on at most k of its outgoing edges. Spread can travel only along the edges that were switched on. The method builds influence-aware node embeddings and trains an edge-scoring Double DQN on 40 small synthetic graphs (25-150 nodes). The same trained policy then runs on 20 public real networks. Spread is simulated under Independent Cascade (IC) with random or uniform 0.5 edge weights, using 100,000 Monte Carlo draws repeated 10 times. RELINK beats Random, Degree and FoF in about 462-464 of 480 configurations, and beats the IM-CSN baseline PSNA in 456 of 480. The margin is widest at small edge budgets and narrows as k grows. The abstract says up to 15% higher spread, while Sec. 6.3 says improvements frequently exceed 15%; both statements are quoted because they differ. On dense graphs PSNA can win at small seed sets. RELINK is slower than PSNA on small and medium graphs and only catches up on large ones. The paper offers no optimality guarantee and says the objective is non-submodular. No legislative, lobbying or empirical-persuasion data are involved.
> 
> The paper matters here because Melange already names RELINK as the design source for its cosponsor recruitment routes. These are `cosponsor_route.py` (champion seeds, at most K=5 asks per recruiter) and the dashboard's closed-network Max-cascade route (`closedCascadeRoute`, 6 edges, fan-out 2). What the system took is the problem framing: an action is an edge, and each node has an out-degree budget. It did not take the learned policy or any of RELINK's evidence. Both builds are hand-tuned greedy heuristics. The paper supports the code's own disclaimer that no (1-1/e) guarantee applies, because the objective is non-submodular. It gives no empirical support for K, the fan-out or the kernel probabilities, and Melange's kernel probability distribution was not compared with the paper's regime. It also weakens the case for replacing the money optimizer with a learned policy, because that optimizer's greedy is already exact for its separable concave objective.

## Study

**Question.** How can we learn effective edge activation strategies for influence maximization in closed (privacy-constrained) social networks, where node-level activation is infeasible and propagation is limited to a budgeted subset of existing edges? (Abstract; Sec. 1)

**Theory.** No social theory is developed. The diffusion basis is the Independent Cascade (IC) model (Kempe et al. 2003, ref. [49]), with the IM-CSN formulation of Huang et al. (2022, ref. [46]) as the problem definition. Motivation is privacy-focused and invitation-only platforms (Signal, Discord, Instagram Close Friends, Slack, Nextdoor), Sec. 1. The learning side is an MDP with n-step Double DQN (van Hasselt et al. 2016, ref. [85]).

**Data.** Training: 40 synthetic graphs (10 each from Barabasi-Albert, Watts-Strogatz, stochastic block model, Erdos-Renyi), node counts 25-150 (Sec. 6.1). Testing: 20 real-world social networks, unit = network (Table 1), with sizes from Residence hall (217 nodes, 2,674 edges) to Slashdot Feb'09 (82,140 nodes, 549,202 edges). Undirected edges are converted to directed pairs (Huang et al. preprocessing). Seed sets: sampled from top 5% by degree, |S| in {5, 50, 100, 200}; budget k in {5, 10, 20}. The text reports 480 experimental configurations over the 20 networks (Sec. 6.1, 6.2). Time period of the real data is not stated. Weighting: random IC probabilities in [0,1] and uniform 0.5 (Sec. 6.1).

**Method.** Edge-level DRL for IM-CSN. (1) Influence-aware node embeddings combining five feature classes: Louvain community ID and size, approximate betweenness, degree, bridge score, clustering coefficient; path features (2-hop neighbour count, path diversity, RMPP-inspired most-probable-path feature from Chaoji et al. 2012); outgoing-edge weight mean/max/variance; seed indicators and seed distance; Node2Vec embedding; SVD reduction to d=16 (Sec. 4.1). (2) Edge-aware aggregation with learned relevance score alpha_ij = sigma(a^T[h_i||h_j] * w_ij), two-hop aggregation via A^2, gated update and degree-normalized residual (Sec. 4.2). (3) MDP: state = partial subgraph, action = activate an edge respecting the per-node out-degree budget k, reward = exact marginal influence gain under IC (Sec. 5.1). (4) Edge Q-values from projected node features, edge weight, and a penalty on accumulated incoming weight (Sec. 5.2). (5) Training by n-step Double DQN with EMA target network, replay buffer of 1M transitions, epsilon-greedy exploration, valid-action restriction; true marginal gains are computed only at training time (Sec. 5.3). (6) Inference: greedy argmax over action-masked Q-values, one edge per step, until no valid actions remain; max steps O(|V|k) (Sec. 5.4). Hardware: NVIDIA A40 GPU, Xeon Silver 4314 CPU; PyTorch, Adam (Sec. 6.1). Baselines: Degree, FoF, Random (five-run average), PSNA (Huang et al. 2022) (Sec. 6.1).

**Identification.** None in the causal sense. This is a simulation benchmark. A single RELINK model is trained once on synthetic graphs and evaluated on 20 unseen real networks across all seed-set and budget settings (Sec. 6.1, 6.2). Out-of-distribution generalization is the claimed identification logic, not a designed comparison of causal effects.

**Scope.** Population: online and privacy-constrained social networks (messaging, trust, email, Facebook/ego, music and voting-style networks, per Table 1). Diffusion: IC only in all experiments (Sec. 6.1). Era: not stated. Institutional setting: academic benchmark on public network datasets; no platform or firm data. Other diffusion models (LT, Trigger, WC) are described as adaptable by changing the reward, but not tested (footnote 1, Sec. 6.1).

## Key findings

- RELINK achieves higher average influence spread than Random, Degree, and FoF in most configurations. Numbers: Wins out of 480: vs Random 462, vs Degree 464, vs FoF 462. Text says it consistently outperforms all methods in at least 90% of experiments.. (Sec. 6.2, Q1; Figure 3 contingency table)
- RELINK outperforms the IM-CSN-specific baseline PSNA in most experiments. Numbers: RELINK better in 456 of 480; PSNA better in 24 of 480.. (Sec. 6.2, Q2; Figure 3)
- RELINK's advantage is largest under small edge budgets; the gap to PSNA narrows as k grows. Numbers: Heatmap colour scale 0.85 to 1.15 (ratio). Text says improvements frequently exceed 15%. Dense graphs (email-Eu-core, wiki-Vote, sx-mathoverflow) favour PSNA at smaller seed sets.. (Sec. 6.3, Q4; Figure 6 heatmap (ratio InfRELINK/InfPSNA))
- Under k=20 and uniform weights, RELINK attains the highest spread when seed sets are small; baseline ordering is Random and PSNA best, then FoF and Degree. Numbers: Figure-only values; no numeric table of means in the text.. (Sec. 6.2 (Figure 4 panels a-j, k=20, uniform weights))
- Runtime: RELINK is slower on small and medium graphs but becomes competitive with or faster than PSNA as graph size grows; the Random, Degree and FoF baselines are cheapest. Numbers: Figure-only; text gives PSNA complexity O(I(|V|+|E|) log|V| + |V| d_m log d_m).. (Sec. 6.2, Q3; Figure 5 (runtime, k=20))
- Training is expensive because exact marginal gains are computed during training; inference needs no spread estimation. Numbers: 6,257.329 seconds for 2,000 epochs, a single model reused across all experiments.. (Sec. 6.4)
- Headline gain claim in the abstract: up to 15% higher spread, with improved scalability. Numbers: Abstract: 'up to 15%'. Body: 'exceeding 15%' in frequent cases. The two statements are not identical.. (Abstract; Sec. 6.3 says 'frequently achieving influence spread improvements exceeding 15%')

## Limitations

- Stated by authors (Sec. 7): extension to dynamic or time-evolving topologies, adaptive constraints, user-specific constraints, and uncertainty in diffusion models are left to future work.
- Stated by authors (Sec. 7): sample-efficient training and theoretical analysis of policy optimality under submodular objectives are not addressed; no optimality guarantee is claimed.
- Stated by authors (Sec. 6.1): all experiments use the IC model only; LT, Trigger and WC are described as adaptable but untested (footnote 1).
- Stated by authors (Sec. 6.4): training takes 6,257.329 s because exact marginal gains are computed during training.
- Stated by authors (Sec. 6.2, Q3): RELINK has higher runtime on small and medium graphs, with a fixed embedding overhead.
- Stated by authors (Sec. 5.1): the objective is non-submodular and the greedy/IC marginal-gain computation is costly; the method computes exact gains only at training time.
- Reviewer note (not stated by authors): the abstract says 'up to 15% higher influence spread' while Sec. 6.3 says improvements 'frequently' exceed 15%; these are different claims.
- Reviewer note (not stated by authors): 480 configurations do not follow from the stated grid (20 networks x 3 k values x 4 seed sizes = 240). The text does not explain the factor of two, which may come from the two weighting schemes, but it does not say so.
- Reviewer note (not stated by authors): results are reported as win counts and figure curves, with no numeric tables, confidence intervals or variance across the 10 repeats, so effect sizes are only partly recoverable from the text.
- Reviewer note (not stated by authors): the training graphs are synthetic and the test graphs are public real networks; no closed-network or platform-private data are used, so 'closed network' is simulated by constraining propagation to chosen edges.

## Gaps and flags

- No learned or optimized edge-activation policy exists. Both RELINK-labelled builds (cosponsor_route.py and closedCascadeRoute) are greedy heuristics. No Random edge baseline comparison was found in the code read for this check (the wider repo and brain were not searched); Random ranked among the strongest baselines in RELINK's Figure 4.
- When MCMODE=1 (off by default), cosponsor_route.py mc_spread lets activation run over every edge among the selected nodes, not only the activated edges, which is not the IM-CSN closed-network semantics the docstring claims. The default route uses tree-reach. The dashboard Max route's cascadeReach then runs an open-network cascade from the recruited set.
- The per-recruiter budgets (K=5 in cosponsor_route, fanout=2 and edgeBudget=6 in the dashboard) are not estimated from any observed whip or ask behaviour, and the paper gives no basis for legislative values.
- There is no sensitivity check of route rankings to the budget k. RELINK shows method ordering changes with k (its gap to PSNA narrows as k grows).
- The paper's evidence is IC-only, with random or uniform 0.5 weights. The system's kernel regime (crypto p_max 0.23, CU 0.52) was not compared with it, so it is untested territory for any edge-activation claim.
- Labelling caution: the code and brain call these routes the 'RELINK design' and 'RELINK Pareto' (cosponsor_route.py:3; make_dashboard_html_00.tpl:675,828; brain topic 2026-06-feature-melange-scenario-model-build). The system borrows only the problem framing. No RELINK performance result transfers to these heuristics, so any claim citing RELINK's '15%' (abstract: up to 15%; Sec. 6.3: frequently exceeding 15%) or '456/480' for Melange routes would be a misattribution.
- Route outputs (expected recruits, 'all asks hold' percentages, Pareto spread) are heuristic model outputs under unestimated ASKM, K and kernel constants. Under D-025 they must not be presented as introduction, access or closeness probabilities.
- The paper does not argue for either rejected SNA proposal (dropping regular cosponsorship edges; per-edge volume normalization), so there is nothing to flag there. It uses no LDA data, so D-021 does not apply.
- This mapping is agent analysis; the claims listed in the failure callout above were not verified.

Paul has not annotated this paper. RELINK is a learned edge policy that is competitive on runtime only for large graphs, and its spread advantage comes in a non-submodular setting. The joint optimizer's separable concave money problem is neither, so greedy is already exact there, and the system's own 'no ML needed at our scale' comment matches the paper. RELINK is silent on incremental replanning. RELINK did supply the per-recruiter budget idea that was built, but the built version is a greedy heuristic rather than RELINK's DRL, and RELINK's evidence covers synthetic-trained IC spread on public online graphs, not legislators.
