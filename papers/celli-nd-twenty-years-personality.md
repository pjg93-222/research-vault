# Twenty Years of Personality Computing: Threats, Challenges and Future Directions

**Fabio Celli, Aleksandar Kartelj, Miljan Đorđević, Derwin Suhartono, Vladimir Filipović, Veljko Milutinović, Georgios Spathoulas, Alessandro Vinciarelli, Michal Kosinski, Bruno Lepri (2026).** *ACM Computing Surveys, Vol. 58, No. 11, Article 293 (April 2026). Metadata hint had venue and DOI blank; corrected from the PDF header.*. [https://doi.org/10.1145/3806009](https://doi.org/10.1145/3806009). Theme: [[themes/machine-learning-sentiment-analysis|Machine Learning & Sentiment Analysis]].

> [!abstract] Summary
> Celli and colleagues review about twenty years of Personality Computing. They follow PRISMA 2020 and report 129 included papers, although the screening arithmetic in the paper gives 126 and the six system categories sum to 119. The review covers three tasks: Recognition (self-reported traits), Perception (traits as observers judge them) and Synthesis (traits that machines generate). Across text, image, audio, video, hybrid and LLM systems, the reported state of the art is modest. Text recognition reaches an average correlation of about 0.30 against a 0.133 baseline. Zero-shot GPT models reach r = 0.29 on Facebook posts, about the same as specialized models. Computer perception has a convergent validity of 0.30, against 0.38 for human judges, and the authors warn that these settings are not fully comparable. SVMs are the largest group of text systems (20 of 45), alongside psycholinguistic features (LIWC/MRC), drawn from Essays and social-network data. Biel et al. found that Extraversion carries the most audio-visual cues and Emotional Stability the fewest. The review recommends reporting rank correlations rather than median splits, judges MBTI and DISC invalid, and calls LLM personality a safety problem. It also lists personality-aware persuasion in elections as a threat.
> 
> For Melange, the paper sets an outside ceiling on what text- and audio-derived traits can deliver. It also places the system's validation targets on Brunswik's lens model. The RKH comparison is Recognition against a target that uses the same method as the system. The Personality on the Hill panels are Perception. Neither is a self-report criterion, so the system's figures do not line up with the paper's state-of-the-art rows. The paper also explains why the system was right to move receptivity off lexical traits and onto behavior. Starting audio work with Extraversion rather than Neuroticism is an inference from the cue pattern, not a finding of the paper, which also reports prosodic SVMs performing best for Extraversion and Conscientiousness.

## Study

**Question.** What has Personality Computing (computational recognition, perception and synthesis of personality) achieved over roughly two decades in methods, applications and benchmarks, what ethical threats does it pose (especially with LLMs), and what future directions should researchers take? (Introduction, p.2-3.)

**Theory.** Personality psychology trait models: Big Five/OCEAN and FFM as the main model (p.4), with MBTI and DISC judged to lack validity (p.4-5). Trait versus state distinction (p.3). Brunswik lens model of interpersonal perception, with ecological validity, representation validity and perceptual judgment (p.5). Three-task framing of Personality Recognition, Perception and Synthesis (p.5). Three LLM ethics themes: Machine Personality, Personality Hacking, Personality Safety (p.22).

**Data.** No primary data. The unit of analysis is the published literature. Corpus: 163 records identified, 152 after deduplication, 26 excluded on quality grounds, and 129 papers reported as included (45 text, 15 image, 7 audio, 25 video, 20 hybrid, 7 LLM, 10 LLM-ethics). Note: 152 minus 26 is 126, so the stated screening arithmetic (126) does not reconcile with the 129 reported as included, and the six system categories sum to 119 (p.12). Benchmarks in Table 2 (p.9-10): Essays (about 2,500 persons, text, 2007 era); MyPersonality (about 6,000,000 persons per Table 2 and text p.9, mixed, Facebook, 2007-2015); Big5Mbti (1,800 persons, text, multi-language); PANDORA talks (about 10,000, text); WCPR2013 (250 persons); WCPR2014 (about 460); PAN-AP-2015 (294 X users); PersIA (22 persons, 6,552 items); Personality Simulation 1 (400 entries, tabular). Period covered: from the 1999 Essays/LIWC work to 2025 LLM studies (field start 2005 per p.2).

**Method.** Narrative/systematic review following PRISMA 2020 (p.12). Search combined modality keywords with personality aliases on the web and in Google Scholar and Scopus. Iterative reference-list expansion to transitive closure. Scopus citation network built in Gephi, with in-degree of 5 or more shown as a foundational-works graph (Fig. 2, p.6-7). Two-phase screening: inclusive first pass, then a pruning pass that excluded inconsistent or flawed work (small samples, weak train/validation/test setup, no comparison to prior methods, unavailable data or code; example Souri et al. [252] excluded, p.12). A single reviewer handled valid articles; at least one more reviewer handled exclusion candidates (p.12). Separate state-of-the-art (SotA) table limited to open-science works, Big Five targets, and F1, accuracy or Rho metrics (Table 3, p.10-11). Section 3 categorizes systems by data modality with Big Five cue tables (Tables 5, 9, 11) and algorithm/feature/data-source matrices (Tables 4, 6, 7, 8, 10, 12). Section 3.5 surveys 10 LLM-ethics papers (Table 13).

**Identification.** None. This is a review article with no causal design, instrument or identification strategy of its own.

**Scope.** Population: human digital footprints (text, images, audio, video, social media metadata, phone and smartphone logs, EEG, physiological data) and LLM outputs. Institutions: academic Personality Computing research plus applied sectors (marketing, recruiting, healthcare, forensics, credit, education). Era: 1999 to 2025 references, field started 2005 (p.2). Language: mostly English, with some Chinese, Indonesian and multilingual work (p.6-7). Domain is personality computing, not legislative, lobbying or election research per se.

## Key findings

- SotA for Personality Recognition from text is an average correlation of about 0.3 over a baseline of 0.133, from WASSA shared tasks. Numbers: Rho about 0.30 vs baseline 0.133. (Section 2.6, p.11; Table 3, p.10 (WASSA2024 Rho 0.300; PANDORA talks Rho 0.292))
- Early shared tasks (Speaker Trait 2012, WCPR 2013/2014, PAN-AP 2015) reached F1 slightly above 0.7, with accuracy between 0.62 and 0.84 depending on input data. A T5 zero-shot replication on PAN-AP-2015 gave averaged F1 0.711. Numbers: F1 about 0.70-0.72; accuracy 0.62-0.84; replication F1 0.711; PAN-AP-2015 accuracy 0.840 in Table 3. (Section 2.6, p.10-11; Table 3, p.10)
- GPT-3.5 and GPT-4 with zero-shot prompting infer Big Five traits from Facebook status updates at an average correlation of 0.29, comparable to specialized ML models (Peters and Matz 2024). Numbers: average r = 0.29; WASSA-style Rho scale. (Section 2.6, p.11; Table 3, p.10; Table 12, p.22)
- Personality Synthesis (role-play LLM self-assessment consistency) has SotA averaged accuracy around 0.75. Table 3 lists Klinkert et al. 2024 at 0.740 and Wang et al. 2024 at 0.766. Numbers: accuracy about 0.75 (0.740 and 0.766). (Section 2.6, p.11; Table 3, p.10)
- Computational Personality Perception SotA is a convergent validity of Rho = 0.30 (meta-analysis of computer prediction studies), close to human perception meta-analytic convergent validity of rho = 0.38. Data source (social media, video, smartphone) significantly affects variance explained. Effect sizes are flagged as not fully comparable across settings. Numbers: computer rho = 0.30; human rho = 0.38. (Section 2.6, p.11 (citing Hinds and Joinson 2024 [119]); Table 3, p.10 (Hinds et al. 2024 marked 0.300 with a comparability caveat))
- Among 45 text-based systems, SVMs were the most used algorithm (20 articles), followed by neural networks (12) and Naive Bayes (10). Social network data was the most common source (27 articles), followed by Essays (15). Psycho-linguistic features were the most common homogeneous feature type (16), with broader word-based features in 20 articles. Numbers: SVM 20; NN 12; NB 10; social network 27; Essays 15; psycho-linguistic 16; broader 20. (Section 3.1, p.12-13; Table 4, p.13)
- Among 20 hybrid systems, SVMs (8), linear regression (6) and decision trees (5) were most used. Social media metadata (12) and smartphone usage or call data (6) were the most common sources. Example: Golbeck et al. 2011 combined Facebook friend networks with LIWC features. Numbers: SVM 8; linear regression 6; decision tree 5; social metadata 12; smartphone 6. (Section 3.3, p.19; Table 10, p.19; Table 11, p.21)
- Image-based systems (15 studies, 2014-2021) rely mainly on social media images, with NN and SVM most common and CNN use rising. Openness links to artistic images; Conscientiousness to conventional, detailed images; Extraversion to number of people in images. Numbers: 15 studies; 2014-2021. (Section 3.2, p.15-16; Table 6, p.15)
- Video-based systems (25 studies, 2008-2022) show that audio-visual cues carry trait information. Biel et al. 2012 found Extraversion and Emotional Stability carried the largest and smallest sets of informative cues, respectively. Numbers: 25 studies. (Section 3.3, p.17-18; Tables 8-9, p.17-18)
- MBTI and DISC have well-documented validity problems and should not be used in scientific work or as ground truth, although MBTI labels are easier to classify than Big Five scores. Numbers: n/a. (Section 2.2, p.4-5; Section 2.6 p.20; Section 4 p.25)
- LLM-based systems (7 articles) have shifted Personality Recognition toward zero-shot and few-shot prompting, and Personality Synthesis toward role-play prompting. Trend toward MBTI prediction. Gan et al. 2022 report significant (p<0.01) MBTI perception on 41,800 faces, but effect sizes are small (below 0.2 Cohen's h). Numbers: 7 articles; 41,800 facial images; Cohen's h below 0.2. (Section 3.4, p.20-22; Table 12, p.22)
- LLMs show consistent trait-like profiles (high Agreeableness, high Openness, low Neuroticism). Induced personality can shift safety, jailbreak resistance and fairness. Conscientiousness is associated with safer outputs. The paper argues LLM personality should be treated as a core safety concern. Numbers: 10 LLM-ethics articles. (Section 3.5, p.22-23; Table 13, p.23)
- Threat analysis: personality-aware marketing was used to influence voting in several elections; personality-aware systems can reach deep intimacy with users; personality-aware credit scoring risks social marginalization; personality-aware friend recommendation may strengthen echo chambers. Numbers: n/a. (Section 4.2, p.24-25 (cites [79] for voting, [143] for intimacy, [262] for echo chambers, [291] for credit))
- Recommendations: standardize on correlation coefficients (Rho) for all tasks; discourage median splits; host benchmarks on GitHub, Kaggle or Hugging Face; use Personality Synthesis to create synthetic anonymous data; use Personality Recognition to alter digital footprints and mask cues; add explainable AI to Perception; collaborate with cybersecurity, computational social science and computational history communities. Numbers: n/a. (Section 4.2, p.25)

## Limitations

- No dedicated limitations section. Limitations stated inline: Hinds and Joinson effect sizes must be read with caution because human and computer settings differ in methods and data (p.11).
- The SotA tables include only open-science works that target Big Five with F1, accuracy or Rho, so the table is a selected set, not a full census (p.10-11).
- Inclusion relied on a single reviewer for most articles, with extra reviewers only for exclusion candidates (p.12). Reported screening counts do not reconcile: 152 minus 26 is 126, the 129 reported as included differs, and the six system categories sum to 119 (p.12).
- Table 3 mixes metrics across studies (F1, accuracy and Rho) and marks some values as non-comparable (p.10). Several tables rely on author-assigned categories (for example Table 4 algorithm grouping, p.12).
- Many cited works are small-sample (for example PersIA 22 persons, WCPR2013 250 persons), and the review itself notes that small samples and weak validation were grounds for exclusion (p.12).
- Authors disclaim the causal and validity status of MBTI-based results and note LLM personality may not be a true psychological state (p.4-5, p.22).
- Scope is computer-science oriented. Authors state the review targets a computer science readership (p.3).

## Gaps and flags

- No self-report criterion exists for any member, so the system cannot place its trait scores on the paper's Recognition scale. The RKH comparison uses the same method and is not a Recognition benchmark; the held-out CV figure (.350) was not located in the Melange fec tree and is unverified.
- Validation records do not state which Brunswik task (Recognition or Perception) each look tests, so field benchmarks get mixed up in summaries.
- There is no audio or video Extraversion look, even though the survey reports Extraversion as the trait with the most nonverbal cues.
- There is no written ethics or misuse note for a personality-aware persuasion layer. The paper names personality-targeted persuasion in elections as a primary threat and asks for explainability.
- The paper's own screening counts do not reconcile: 152 after deduplication minus 26 excluded is 126, but 129 are reported as included and the six system categories sum to 119 (p.12). Quote its corpus totals with that caveat.
- Do not compare the system's RKH held-out CV figure (unverified; not located in the fec tree) with the field's 0.30 state of the art. The criteria differ (a method-shared text estimate versus self-report), and the paper itself flags cross-setting effect sizes as not comparable.
- Personality-aware persuasion appears in the paper's threat list (Section 4.2). The receptivity layer is that class of system, aimed at legislators. Wording should describe its traits as heuristic model inputs, not as measured psychology, and every figure needs its window and denominator.
- This paper argues for neither of the rejected SNA proposals (dropping regular cosponsorship edges, or per-edge volume normalization). There is no conflict with D-021 or D-025.
