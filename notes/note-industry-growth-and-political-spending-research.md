# Industry Growth and Political Spending: Research Plan

**Paul Goff (2026).** Theme: [[themes/paul-s-research-notes|Paul's Research Notes]].

> [!abstract] Summary
> Paul's own research plan (dated 2026-09-25) for testing whether industries spend more or less on federal politics when they have grown, are growing, or expect to grow. It specifies OLS with a separate intercept per sector, six core tests (C1-C3 contributions, L1-L3 lobbying, each with prior actual, same-year actual, or IBISWorld predicted growth), election-cycle dummies and IEM expected-control covariates, plus job-tier and per-chamber Democratic-share extensions. Its strongest contributions are discipline rules: predicted growth must come from a report vintage published before the spending year, missing forecasts stay missing, null results are kept, and independent-row OLS errors are ruled insufficient with nine or fewer sector clusters. It reports no estimates, and its own readiness check says contributions overlap the IEM forecasts only in 2021-2022. The document is the north star of the built sector growth study (Mentat sector_growth_study, in R). The build honours the inference caution with a wild cluster restricted bootstrap by sector and Holm adjustment. It keeps dollar levels as the main outcome (log outcomes are a robustness family only), fills 2023-2026 expected control from a series labelled RETROSPECTIVE / NOWCAST on Paul's ruling (with STRICT variants that drop values measured after their year; 538 is a robustness family only), and adds no Tossup or trend regressor. Reference values from the Codex methodology review of run analysis-R-20260926T070630Z, recorded in sgs_review.R: L3 on LobbyView lobbying base slope 0.352 (p 0.0375, Holm 0.1125; UNSTRATIFIED, pooled across sectors) flips to -0.477 under year FE and -0.537 in first differences (n 71), and growth adds no adjusted R2. The core annual partisan SHIFT tests are not estimable in the latest report; only a below-minimum exploratory preview exists. The plan's questions are therefore not yet answered, which bears on the live giving forecast that still feeds IBISWorld growth into general giving at 50/50.

## Study

**Question.** Do industries spend differently on politics (federal campaign contributions and lobbying) when they have grown, are growing, or expect to grow? Does a published industry growth forecast predict subsequent spending, and do expectations about House and Senate control help explain spending?

**Theory.** Better performance could supply more money for political spending; weak performance or a weak forecast could instead prompt spending to seek favorable policy. The sign of the relationship is to be estimated, not assumed.

**Data.** Target panel of annual sector-year observations, 2008-2025, for nine sectors where comparable data exist: commercial banks, credit unions, life insurance, auto manufacturing, crypto, Credit Arms, Fintech, Property and Casualty insurance, Telecom. Odd years retained; incomplete 2026 excluded. Unit is sector-year; spending in millions of 2025 dollars. Sources: IBISWorld revenue and archived forecasts; Mentat/FEC contributions split by job tier; Melange LDA and LobbyView lobbying; IEM congressional control forecasts; Minneapolis Fed CPI. Final N is not yet fixed: verified contribution years overlap the congressional forecasts only in 2021-2022. Strict annual growth inputs: 53 in the original collection, 86 in the expanded nine-sector grid.

**Method.** Multiple linear regression by OLS with a separate intercept per sector (sector fixed effects). Contributions: Y_it = a_i + b X_it + c Pres_t + d Midterm_t + e ExpectedDemHouse_t + f ExpectedDemSenate_t + error. Lobbying adds CurrentDemHouse_t and CurrentDemSenate_t. X is prior actual growth, same-year actual growth, or predicted growth (tests C1-C3 and L1-L3). Growth in percentage points. Job-tier extension: pooled model with sector-by-tier baselines and growth x tier terms, Employee as reference. Chamber extension: linear model of Democratic share of contributions. Further extensions: prior and predicted growth together; year indicators in place of national controls; logged spending for positive observations. Simple regression only to illustrate scatterplots.

**Identification.** No causal identification is claimed. Identifying variation is within-sector over time, after election-cycle and congressional-expectation controls. Predicted growth is taken from the latest IBISWorld report published by December 31 of the prior year, so it precedes the spending year. Congressional expectations are concurrent IEM averages and may not be known before all spending in a year. Results are framed as associations consistent with a response to public forecasts, not knowledge of firms' private expectations.

**Scope.** US federal political spending by nine industry sectors, 2008-2025: candidate-committee contributions (personal versus organizational/PAC channels) and lobbying expenditures. Sector membership follows workbook rosters (40 company names) and industry proxies: Credit Arms is a proxy for workbook companies, Telecom is analyzed at component level, and Fintech lacks a comparable annual series. Life insurance and auto manufacturing are not among the published workbooks checked.

## Key findings

- Design is OLS with sector fixed effects and six core tests: C1-C3 explain contributions and L1-L3 explain lobbying, each with prior actual, same-year actual, or predicted growth as the growth regressor. (6 core tests; growth in percentage points; spending in millions of 2025 dollars; Section 2 (table) and Section 3 (equations))
- Predicted growth must come from the latest IBISWorld report published by December 31 of year t-1 that forecasts year t, using numerator and denominator from the same vintage. (Example: report estimates 2019 revenue at 100 and 2020 at 105, giving 5 percent; Section 4, Predicting growth: preserve the original forecast date)
- Readiness check: verified contribution years overlap the prepared congressional forecasts only in 2021-2022, too little annual variation to identify the full control specification. (overlap years 2021 and 2022 only; Section 8, Readiness check)
- IBISWorld collection size: 63 original reports yielding 53 strict annual sector-year values; the expanded nine-sector grid has 86 strict annual revenue-growth inputs with explicit missing rows. (63 reports; 53 then 86 strict annual inputs; 12 supplemental observations kept separate; Section 4 (predicted growth) and Section 9)
- Banking and credit-union revenue include 2006, while life insurance and auto extracts start in 2007, leaving their 2008 lagged-growth observation incomplete. (2006 start for banks and credit unions; 2007 for life and auto; Section 6A, final paragraph)
- Crypto has only one verified original forecast: a Statista U.S. cryptocurrency-platform revenue forecast for 2025 of minus 3.78 percent, available by December 26, 2024, kept as a separate proxy. (-3.78 percent for 2025; Section 6A, table row for Crypto)
- Telecom is kept at component level (wireless and cable archives, 36 reports) because cable's revenue definition changes; Fintech has only dated multi-year projections and no comparable annual series. (36 Telecom reports; Section 6A, table rows for Telecom and Fintech)
- Congressional control forecasts (IEM) are available 2008-2022 only; 2023-2025 are missing, and the later election result must never substitute for a missing forecast. (available 2008-2022; 2023-2025 missing; Section 6D, forecast meaning paragraph)
- Job-tier test uses a pooled model with sector-by-tier baselines and growth-by-tier interactions, Employee as reference; the existing Employee class includes unrecognized-title fallbacks that must be flagged. (Chief slope term q; Middle Management slope term r; Section 5, Job-level comparisons; Section 6B)
- Partisan direction is tested with a per-chamber Democratic share outcome, D/(D+R), with zero denominators set to missing; party, presidential and other committee flows are not assigned to a chamber without a defensible mapping. (bounded 0 to 1 outcome; Section 5, Partisan direction)
- Lobbying outcome must keep expenses_total and income_total distinct, since client reports and outside-registrant receipts can refer to overlapping money; the four-sector LDA aggregate does not cover all nine sectors. (2 recorded measures analyzed separately; LDA annual rows 2021-2025 plus partial 2026; Section 6C, Lobbying)
- Inference caution: with few independent sector groups, ordinary independent-row OLS standard errors are not sufficient for strong significance claims, so repeated observations and small-sample uncertainty must be assessed. (9 planned sectors; fewer usable; Section 5, final paragraph)

## Limitations

- Plan only: no estimated results are reported in the document.
- Few independent sector groups (nine planned, fewer usable); ordinary OLS standard errors understate uncertainty; repeated observations must be accounted for.
- Verified contribution years overlap congressional forecasts only in 2021-2022, so the full control specification is not yet identified.
- IEM congressional forecasts are concurrent averages, not necessarily known before all spending in the year, and are missing for 2023-2025.
- IBISWorld forecasts proxy public expectations only; they do not reveal private firm forecasts or whether a donor read the report.
- Credit Arms is an industry proxy for workbook companies with OneMain and equipment-finance scope differences needing sensitivity checks.
- Telecom is component-level; Fintech lacks a comparable annual series; crypto has one verified forecast.
- Auto 2011-2012 uses a car and light-truck component reconstruction and needs a check excluding it; several forecast price bases differ and some values are inferred.
- Chief classification uses a non-date-specific executive-name override; historical tier comparisons need role at donation date or a sensitivity test.
- Employee tier includes unrecognized-title fallbacks; lobbying has no donor job-tier split in verified sources.
- Lobbying: expenses_total and income_total measure overlapping money and cannot be simply added; older undifferentiated amount totals are not comparable without checking.
- Historical contribution coverage before 2021 is not established in the local workbook extracts; FEC bulk data may be needed.
- Sources cover only the selected sectors and are not an exhaustive archive of forecasts.

## Gaps and flags

- The plan prescribes dollar-level outcomes and the build keeps dollar levels as the main outcome; log outcomes appear only as the R-log and R-logFE robustness family, so any log-based figure must be labelled robustness.
- Contribution history before 2021 is not established locally (plan Section 6B; the brain records no pre-2021 cycle beyond the 2020 extension), so C1-C3 rest on very few years per sector and the full control specification stays weakly identified.
- The live giving forecast keeps an IBISWorld growth term at 50/50 for general giving although the growth study's reference results show no growth gain in fit; no recorded backtest shows whether that term beats the cycle-only model.
- Credit Arms, P and C, Telecom, life insurance and auto lack LDA sector totals; the four-sector LDA aggregate does not cover the nine-sector design, so L1-L3 run on LobbyView with fewer clusters.
- The Chief tier uses a non-date-specific executive-name override and the Employee tier absorbs unrecognized titles; no dated-role sensitivity test is recorded in the build.
- With G sectors the wild cluster bootstrap cannot reach p below 2/6^G; tests with few sectors return no inference by design, which the plan anticipated but did not size.
- Flag: The build's 2023-2026 expected control is Paul's explicit ruling, labelled RETROSPECTIVE / NOWCAST (sgs_config.R:185), with STRICT variants that exclude values measured after their year; quote the STRICT variant beside any main-series H4 exp_dem result.
- Flag: Recorded growth-study results (L3-LV base 0.352, p 0.0375, Holm 0.1125; year-FE -0.477; first-difference -0.537, n 71) are the Codex methodology review's reference values from run analysis-R-20260926T070630Z; the core annual SHIFT tests are not estimable (only the exploratory SHIFT-Chamber-PV preview exists).
