# Review: industry growth and political spending

**Codex (2026), a methodological review of Paul's brief (Astra_Review_Brief_Sector_Growth_and_Political_Spending.md).** Theme: [[themes/paul-s-research-notes|Paul's Research Notes]].

> [!abstract] Summary
> A design review dated 2026-09-24, written by Codex, of Paul's brief (the Astra brief) that became the Mentat sector growth study. It estimates nothing. Its core advice: make prior-year real growth the main question and fit log spending on sector and year fixed effects plus lagged raw percent growth (g(t-1)), separately for organizational PAC giving and LDA lobbying, never summed. Treat election expectations as a separate, exploratory study, because more sectors do not create more elections (about 9 elections, 6 chamber flips). It also says three of the brief's specifications do not test their hypotheses: a contraction dummy is not an asymmetric slope (use g+ and g-); Shift = P - M plus current control only reparameterizes P plus M; and a unified-government term cannot separate expected from current control. On data, it says not to fill missing control probabilities with realized results. It says 538 models that use fundraising create a feedback loop, so a polls-only sensitivity is better. It warns that LDA in-house totals already include retained-firm payments and association dues. With five sectors, it says, use the restricted wild-cluster bootstrap and leave-one-sector-out checks. Against the system as built, several points were adopted: raw percent growth, g_pos/g_neg splits, the restricted wild-cluster bootstrap with a few-cluster guard, LOSO/LOYO, the 538 Lite seat forecasts, and lobbying series that are never summed. Others were not. The primary models use dollar levels with national phase and expected-control controls, and year fixed effects and logs are only robustness checks. The giving outcome is Mentat general giving across all recipient types. The 2023/2025 expected control was filled after the fact by Paul's ruling. The later Codex audit's figures are consistent with the review's main warning: the L3-LV lobbying slope of +0.352 flipped to -0.477 under sector and year fixed effects, and growth added nothing to fit (adjusted R2 0.884717 vs 0.884690), on 5 sectors (pooled slope, no per-sector strata quoted; the window is not verified).

## Study

**Question.** Does prior-year real industry growth predict subsequent organizational political spending (corporate and trade-association PAC contributions to congressional candidates, and reported LDA lobbying expenditure), and do election expectations (probability of chamber control) predict where PAC giving goes across parties, incumbents and committee members? The growth association is the main question; expectations are a separate, exploratory question.

**Theory.** Three mechanisms, not a predetermined sign: favorable performance can expand capacity; poor performance can raise demand for relief while reducing resources; threatened policy losses can induce spending in either economic state. Majority status has value (Cox and Magar). Committee access drives reallocation (Powell and Grimmer). Regulatory shocks can lower output and raise lobbying at once, producing a negative association without distress-driven behavior.

**Data.** Not yet assembled; no N estimated. Unit: industry-year. Case analysis uses five sectors; broad panel may use five to ten sectors or all insurance carriers. Proposed common panel 2008-2024 (about 18 annual observations; 2025 only after source audit). Outcomes: FEC committee-to-candidate PAC disbursements (DIME v4.0 covers 1979-2024 public itemized files, excluding CRP/NIMSP-sourced records); LDA LD-2 lobbying, quarterly from 2008. Growth: BEA real value added. Expectations: chamber-control markets (Iowa Electronic Markets, incomplete odd-year coverage) or archived forecasts, about nine elections, six realized chamber flips. Salience: Policy Agendas hearings catalog (listed through 2021) and Unified Agenda editions.

**Method.** Main model: log S_it = alpha_i + lambda_t + beta g_{i,t-1} + e, with sector and year effects, estimated separately for PAC contributions and lobbying (never summed). Growth g = 100 (V_t / V_{t-1} - 1) on raw real value added. Alternatives: PPML exponential conditional mean if genuine zeros exist; log initial sector size at t-2 as a control; split growth into g+ = max(g,0) and g- = max(-g,0) for asymmetry; dynamic FE only as sensitivity (Bruno bias correction; Arellano-Bond or system GMM not recommended with five to ten sectors). Expectations study: RShare = sector/chamber baseline + coefficient times earlier P + coefficient times current control + limited calendar controls, with a fractional-response sensitivity. Inference: small-sample cluster corrections, restricted wild-cluster bootstrap, cluster leverage, leave-one-sector-out, Driscoll-Kraay only as sensitivity, randomization inference only with a defensible assignment argument.

**Identification.** Identification rests on within-sector variation in lagged real growth net of sector means and common year shocks. Lagging fixes ordering but does not establish causality; the Bruno correction does not solve endogeneity in current-year growth. Expectations identification comes from election-cycle variation in earlier control probabilities, with roughly nine elections and six realized flips, so the review treats those estimates as exploratory. Chamber-by-year effects absorb chamber-specific probabilities in a pooled panel.

**Scope.** Prospective design for US federal politics: corporate and trade-association PAC disbursements to congressional candidates, LDA lobbying, BEA-classified industries, 2008-2024 (2025 if audited). Excludes unregistered advocacy, state politics, and industry-affiliated individual giving, party contributions, super PAC financing and independent expenditures, which are to be analyzed as separate channels.

## Key findings

- Make prior real industry growth the main analysis; treat election expectations and partisan allocation as a separate, more exploratory analysis because more industries add information about growth but not more national elections. (No estimates; 14 numbered sections of recommendations; Overall verdict)
- The measures (asset growth, profits, premiums, production, crypto valuations) do not yet share a common economic mechanism; z-scores change scale, not meaning. (; Overall verdict, ranked risk 1)
- Repeating a national forecast across industries adds no independent forecast shocks; coverage, timing, common shocks and roughly nine elections matter more than total row count. (About 9 elections; 6 realized chamber flips; Overall verdict, ranked risk 2; section 7)
- Shift plus current control is a reparameterization of probability plus current control; the current-control coefficient holding P fixed is g minus f. (f(P-M)+gM = fP+(g-f)M; Section 4, equation after 'Use P and current control directly')
- A contraction indicator is not an asymmetric slope; define contraction on raw real growth below zero and use g+ and g- with separate coefficients to test asymmetry. (Raw growth below 0 defines contraction; Section 14, bullets 2 and 3)
- Majority status has value: Cox and Magar estimate an approximately 36,000 dollar majority premium around the 1994 House switch, but this does not establish a response speed to forecast changes. (About 36,000 dollars; Section 4)
- Illustration only: beta of 0.03 would correspond to about 3.05 percent higher typical spending per one-point growth increase, computed as 100(e^beta - 1); this is not an estimated result. (3.05 percent for beta = 0.03; Overall formula section, paragraph on interpretation)
- Corporate PAC donations come from voluntary segregated funds, so a direct financial-capacity mechanism fits lobbying more naturally than PAC donations; PAC profits are not the contribution budget. (; Section 2)
- In-house lobbying totals can double-count retained-firm payments and association dues; use documented deduplication and report trade-association totals separately or with an overlap adjustment. (; Section 8)
- No seamless 2008-2025 probability series is verified; build a chamber-by-election inventory and leave unavailable IEM observations missing rather than filling with 0.5 or actual results. (2010 House IEM market opened November 2009; Section 5)
- Fairshake receipts are not outside spending; the FEC table reports about 260.071 million dollars receipts, 195.827 million disbursements and 40.662 million direct independent expenditures for Fairshake itself, with related committees needing consolidation. (260.071m receipts; 195.827m disbursements; 40.662m IE; Data and citation corrections table, row 5)
- Keep life insurance as a federal case with exclusion sensitivity; state data added only for insurers would change outcome scope, and broad insurance growth must not be labeled life-insurance growth. (; Section 11)

## Limitations

- Design review only: no models estimated and no panel assembled; numbers are documentary or illustrative.
- Claims about the OpenSecrets API end date (April 15, 2025) and bulk access could not be verified from primary pages and are treated as unconfirmed.
- DIME public itemized files exclude CRP/NIMSP-sourced records; separate academic request needed for those.
- The 538 generic-ballot historical CSV covers November 1995 to November 2016 and is not a contemporaneous forecast archive.
- Policy Agendas hearing catalog is incomplete and listed through 2021 only.
- Roughly nine elections and six realized chamber flips limit the expectations analysis; results would be exploratory.
- With five sectors and 18 annual observations, bootstrap and Driscoll-Kraay inference are unreliable; cluster corrections also have limits.
- Registered lobbying does not capture unregistered advocacy; a registration drop does not mechanically imply lower political effort.
- Fairshake related committees require separate consolidation before totals are used.

## Gaps and flags

- No primary sector+year-FE log-dollar growth model: year FE and logs exist only as robustness variants, though the Codex audit's reference values show the lobbying slope flipping sign under them.
- Contribution core models carry expected control without current control, so H4 cannot compare current versus expected power for giving.
- Expected control for 2023 and 2025 is post-period (Crystal Ball Nov-2024 call, Sep-2026 snapshot); no available_at gate in the main spec.
- Trade-association LDA spending is not reported separately or overlap-adjusted; issue-code dollars must not be split across topics.
- No count of active contributors/clients or concentration measure to separate participation changes from per-actor spending.
- No policy-salience proxy (lagged hearing counts or Unified Agenda proceedings) in the built specs.
- Driscoll-Kraay sensitivity named in the transcribed original contract is not visible in sgs_estimate.R.
- Flag: Conflict with Paul's ruling (2026-09-25 ~19:40, reversing 1a): the review says not to fill unavailable control probabilities with results or later information; the built 2023 value uses the Nov-2024 Crystal Ball call. Paul's ruling governs; label those rows retrospective.
- Flag: Conflict with Paul's rulings R4 and Keep those receipts: the review wants organizational PAC-to-candidate giving as the main outcome; the built outcome is all-recipient general giving including non-giving receipts.
- Flag: Authorship: this is a Codex review of Paul's brief; treat its recommendations as advice, not Paul's rulings.
- Flag: The review estimates nothing; it cannot validate or refute the growth effect. Its 3.05 percent figure is an illustration for beta = 0.03, not a result.
